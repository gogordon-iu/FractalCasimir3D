## 2. Literature Review: Vacuum Stresses, Energy Conditions, and Quantum Gravity

### 2.1 Conformal Symmetry, Trace Anomalies, and the Canonical Casimir Vacuum
In conformal quantum field theory, a classically conformally invariant field (such as a massless scalar with conformal coupling $\xi = 1/6$ or source-free electromagnetism in $3+1$ dimensions) possesses a traceless classical stress-energy tensor ($T^\mu{}_\mu = 0$). Brown and Maclay (1969) demonstrated that between two ideal, infinite, flat conducting plates separated by distance $d$, the renormalized vacuum expectation value of the stress-energy tensor is given by:
$$\langle T^\mu{}_\nu \rangle_{\rm flat} = \frac{\pi^2 \hbar c}{720 d^4} \text{diag}(-1, 1, 1, -3)$$
The trace of this tensor is identically zero:
$$T^\mu{}_\mu = -\rho_{\rm vac} + P_x + P_y + P_z = \frac{\pi^2 \hbar c}{720 d^4} \left( 1 + 1 + 1 - 3 \right) = 0$$
Under semiclassical gravity, where the classical spacetime geometry is coupled to the expectation value of quantum fields via the semiclassical Einstein equations:
$$G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G \langle T_{\mu\nu} \rangle$$
contracting with the metric $g^{\mu\nu}$ yields the Ricci scalar curvature:
$$R = 4\Lambda - 8\pi G \langle T^\mu{}_\mu \rangle$$
In flat Minkowski spacetime without a cosmological constant ($\Lambda = 0$), the tracelessness of the flat-plate Casimir vacuum implies that the Ricci scalar strictly vanishes ($R = 0$). Although the vacuum produces anisotropic tidal Riemann curvature ($R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} \neq 0$), it sources no scalar spacetime curvature.

### 2.2 Classical Energy Conditions and Quantum Energy Inequalities
In general relativity, the classical singularity theorems of Hawking and Penrose rely on energy conditions satisfied by classical matter:
- **Weak Energy Condition (WEC)**: $T_{\mu\nu} t^\mu t^\nu \ge 0$ for any timelike vector $t^\mu$ (requires $\rho \ge 0$ and $\rho + P_i \ge 0$).
- **Null Energy Condition (NEC)**: $T_{\mu\nu} k^\mu k^\nu \ge 0$ for any null vector $k^\mu$ (requires $\rho + P_i \ge 0$).
- **Dominant Energy Condition (DEC)**: $\rho \ge |P_i|$.
- **Strong Energy Condition (SEC)**: $T_{\mu\nu} t^\mu t^\nu \ge \frac{1}{2} T^\lambda{}_\lambda t^\mu t_\mu$ (requires $\rho + \sum P_i \ge 0$ and $\rho + P_i \ge 0$).

Quantum fields fundamentally violate all pointwise classical energy conditions. The Casimir vacuum is the quintessential laboratory demonstration of negative energy density:
$$\rho_{\rm vac} = -\frac{\pi^2 \hbar c}{720 d^4} < 0$$
Along the normal null vector $k^\mu = (1, 0, 0, 1)$:
$$T_{\mu\nu} k^\mu k^\nu = \rho_{\rm vac} + P_z = -\frac{\pi^2 \hbar c}{720 d^4} - \frac{\pi^2 \hbar c}{240 d^4} = -\frac{\pi^2 \hbar c}{180 d^4} < 0$$
This negative energy density is the theoretical prerequisite for exotic spacetime geometries, including traversable Morris-Thorne wormholes and Alcubierre warp metrics.

To prevent macroscopic causality violations and unconstrained negative mass, Ford and Roman (1995, 1997) and Fewster (2000) established **Quantum Energy Inequalities (QEIs)**. For a massless field along an observer's worldline with sampling function $f(t)$ of characteristic duration $\tau$:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \langle T_{00}(t) \rangle |f(t)|^2 dt \ge -\frac{C}{\tau^4}$$
These inequalities establish that negative energy densities cannot be arbitrarily large without being accompanied by a compensating positive energy flux.
