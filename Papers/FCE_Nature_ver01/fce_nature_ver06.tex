\abstract{%
The quantum fluctuation-induced Casimir force between neutral surfaces in vacuum is ordinarily attractive, governed by reflection symmetry and parallel-plate surface overlap. Although multiscale boundaries are predicted to alter vacuum fluctuations across hierarchy scales, isolating hierarchical forces in three-dimensional cavities is impeded whenever face-to-face surface overlap dominates the interaction energy. Conversely, three-dimensional slot-tooth interdigitation eliminates vertical overlap and generates sub-piconewton lateral fringing repulsion in vacuum, but uniform periodic gratings suffer from mutual evanescent screening and one-dimensional radial hierarchies leave coarse-generation slots under-coupled in curved annuli. Here we show through full-wave three-dimensional boundary-element and finite-difference time-domain calculations that combining slot-tooth interdigitation with multiscale prefractal Cantor hierarchies across radial, azimuthal, and axial coordinates eliminates parallel-plate overlap while partitioning transverse fringing fields across separated wavenumber bands. At matched discretization, radial Cantor rings enhance open-slot repulsive axial force by $\CantorNtwoFlatzVsUniformBemStdRatio{}\times\text{--}\CantorNtwoHybridrzVsUniformBemStdRatio{}\times$ ($N=2$) and $1.55\times$ ($N=3$) over uniform gratings, while extending middle-third Cantor subdivision across all three cylindrical coordinates $(r, \theta, z)$ reaches $+\roundtwo{\NthreeUnithreedFoneoneoneUnifiedFzPn}\text{ pN}$ ($\roundtwo{\NthreeUnithreedFoneoneoneUnifiedFa}\text{ pN}\,\mu\text{m}^{-2}$)---a $\roundtwo{\NthreeUnithreedRatioFoneoneoneVsFzerozerozero}\times$ gain over the radial-only $N=3$ Cantor ring and $\roundtwo{\NthreeUnithreedRatioFoneoneoneVsUnizerozerozero}\times$ over the uniform grating, with $+\roundone{\NthreeUnithreedSuperadditiveExcessPct}\%$ super-additive synergy and a $+\roundone{\NthreeUnithreedGainFoneoneoneVsShufonetwoPct}\%\text{--}\roundone{\NthreeUnithreedGainFoneoneoneVsUnionetwoPct}\%$ lead over all matched 12-sub-slot non-fractal controls. Rotating the teeth from open slots to recessed landing pads switches the axial force at zero electrical bias between repulsion and attraction, establishing multiscale interdigitation to control vacuum Casimir forces.}

% Nature Articles do not use an "Introduction" heading.
The Casimir effect---the force arising from boundary-modified quantum electromagnetic fluctuations\cite{casimir1948,bordag2009}---links quantum electrodynamics to the geometry of matter at sub-100-nm scales. In ordinary three-dimensional (3D) cavities in vacuum (background permittivity $\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$), both the sign and the scaling of this interaction are tightly constrained. By the Kenneth--Klich theorem\cite{kennethklich2006}, any two mirror-symmetric bodies composed of standard conductors or non-magnetic dielectrics in vacuum mutually attract, while in the proximity-force approximation (PFA) facing parallel surfaces attract in proportion to their overlap area ($F_z^{\rm PFA} \propto -A_{\rm overlap}/d^4$). Although repulsive Casimir--Lifshitz forces have been demonstrated via chemical liquid immersion\cite{munday2009}, and recent experiments on rotational Casimir torques\cite{somers2018}, self-assembled microcavities\cite{munkhbat2021}, and nonlinear superconducting resonators\cite{dejong2026} have expanded vacuum metrology, amplifying and mechanically switching repulsive Casimir forces in vacuum without liquid damping remains an open challenge\cite{rodriguez2011}.

Two geometric strategies have been explored to tailor vacuum forces without altering bulk material permittivities. First, in quantum fractals\cite{kempkes2019,xu2021} and self-similar plate stacks\cite{akkermans2010,shajesh2016,shajesh2017}, discrete scale invariance breaks continuous scale symmetry. Within an effective macroscopic trace framework for self-similar hierarchies incorporating semitransparent mode tunneling\cite{milton2004,milton2016,gordon2026prd}, discrete scale invariance is parameterized by a logarithmically running Casimir coefficient that yields a non-conformal stress-energy trace identity. Yet in one-dimensional (1D) scalar models\cite{gordon2026prd}, this running coefficient remained an effective analytical parametrization rather than a 3D vector electromagnetic result. Moreover, in standard 3D cavities with fractal boundaries, Weyl's law and Lapidus's spectral asymptotics\cite{lapidus1991} indicate that bulk volume and facing-surface-area contributions dominate high-frequency mode counts, while in separation-dependent Casimir interactions any finite parallel-plate overlap area ($A_{\rm overlap} > 0$) produces strong normal attraction that masks subleading perimeter and hierarchical boundary contributions.

Second, Levin et al.\cite{levin2010} demonstrated that reflection symmetry can be broken geometrically in vacuum without fractals: when an elongated metallic ridge or particle is positioned above a slotted aperture plate, transverse saddle-point fringing fields connecting the ridge tip to the slot sidewalls pull the ridge toward the slot mid-plane, yielding an axial repulsive restoring force along the vertical coordinate (accompanied by lateral Earnshaw instability that must be stabilized by a mechanical suspension)\cite{levin2010,rodriguez2011}. However, a single narrow ridge generates only sub-piconewton forces\cite{levin2010,rodriguez2011}, and scaling this interaction across a wide 3D annular cavity is limited because widening a single tooth collapses the sidewall fringing gradient while repeating identical periodic slots suffers from mutual evanescent screening.

Here we combine 3D slot-tooth interdigitation with prefractal Cantor hierarchies in concentric ring cavities---progressing from radial slot hierarchies to a unified 3D conformal-recursive polar Cantor $(r, \theta, z)$ architecture---to amplify and switch axial Casimir forces in vacuum (Figs.~\ref{fig:rotational_clutch_architecture}--\ref{fig:n3_unified_3d_fractal_factorial} and Extended Data Figs.~\ref{fig:all_constellations_blender}--\ref{fig:n3_unified_3d_blender_atlas}). When concentric rotor teeth sit centred inside open stator slots ($\theta = 0^\circ\text{--}6^\circ$), vertical face-to-face surface overlap vanishes ($A_{\rm overlap} = 0$). This eliminates the local parallel-plate PFA attraction ($F_z^{\rm PFA} \equiv 0$), creating a zero-overlap regime in which sidewall fringing and cross-track scattering govern the axial force. Whereas a single wide tooth loses sidewall proximity, a uniform periodic grating screens adjacent tracks within the same evanescent wavenumber band, and a 1D radial-only Cantor sequence leaves wide coarse-generation slots under-coupled in a curved annulus, recursively subdividing the interdigitated slots and teeth across all three cylindrical coordinates $(r, \theta, z)$ partitions fringing fields across separated wavenumber bands while reactivating coarse slots via internal Cantor stator sub-ribs. Conversely, rotating the teeth over recessed stator landing pads ($\theta = 18^\circ\text{--}48^\circ$) restores face-to-face surface overlap across a narrow vertical gap, switching the axial force at zero electrical bias between perimeter-driven repulsion and parallel-plate attraction.

\section*{Zero-overlap window and rotary clutch}
\label{sec:architecture}

To motivate our multiscale 3D cavity design, consider first the effective macroscopic description of self-similar boundary hierarchies\cite{gordon2026prd}. In a cavity hierarchy characterized by a logarithmically running dimensionless Casimir coefficient $C(d_s, \ln(d/\ell_*))$---where $d_s$ is the effective spectral dimension, $d$ is the characteristic separation scale, and $\ell_* = L b^{-N}$ is the $N$-th generation prefractal cutoff below outer scale $L$---the renormalized vacuum stress-energy tensor satisfies the effective macroscopic trace identity\cite{gordon2026prd}:
\begin{equation}
T^\mu{}_{\mu,\rm vac} = -\rho_{\rm vac} + 2P_\parallel + P_\perp = -\frac{\hbar c}{d^4} \,\partial_{\ln d}C\!\left(d_s, \ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right),
\label{eq:trace_running}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_{\rm vac}$ is the renormalized interaction energy density and $P_\parallel$ and $P_\perp$ denote the transverse (in-plane) and normal (axial) Casimir pressures, respectively. In a 1D transfer-matrix model of an $N$-th generation pre-Cantor stack $\mathcal{C}_N$ of scale factor $b$ (Methods), partitioning the hierarchy into its self-similar sub-blocks yields the functional recursion for the renormalized interaction energy per unit transverse area, $\Delta e_{\mathcal{C}_N}(d)$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta e_{\mathcal{C}_N}(d) = 2 \Delta e_{\mathcal{C}_{N-1}}(d/b) + \Delta e_{\rm cross}(d; \ell_*),
\label{eq:recursion_main}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta e_{\rm cross}$ captures evanescent cross-talk between sub-cavities across imaginary wavenumbers $\kappa = \xi/c$. Under the asymptotic fixed-point assumption that $\Delta e_{\rm cross}$ scales homogeneously as $d^{-3}$ up to log-periodic corrections in the prefractal window $\ell_* \ll d \ll L$ (Methods), the discrete scale-invariant recursion admits a log-periodic Fourier expansion for the dimensionless Casimir coefficient:
\begin{equation}
C\!\left(d_s, \ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k \, e^{i \omega_k \ln(d/\ell_*)} = C_0(d_s) \left[ 1 + F_{\rm per}\!\left(\ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right) \right], \qquad \omega_k = \frac{2\pi k}{\ln b},
\label{eq:fourier_running}
\end{equation}
where $F_{\rm per}$ is multiplicatively periodic with period $\ln b$ ($b=3$ for middle-third Cantor geometry). While Eqs.~\eqref{eq:recursion_main}--\eqref{eq:fourier_running} are derived in an idealized 1D scalar transfer-matrix setting rather than for a 3D vector ring, they establish a three-step discovery pathway for our 3D cavity optimization: first, isolating the analytical log-periodic oscillation frequency $\omega_k = 2\pi k/\ln b$ from first principles in 1D (Eq.~\ref{eq:fourier_running}); second, verifying numerically in 3D vector BEM that this log-periodic oscillation persists both as separated imaginary-wavenumber sub-bands ($\Delta\ln\kappa \approx \ln 3$) and as log-spaced force modulation across a vertically staged stroke ($\Delta\ln|z| = \ln b$), which diagnoses why 1D radial-only hierarchies leave the coarse first- and second-generation sub-bands under-coupled (Fig.~\ref{fig:clutch_log_periodicity}); and third, extending in-phase Cantor recursion across all three cylindrical coordinates $(r, \theta, z)$ to populate and phase-lock all three log-periodic sub-bands simultaneously in the optimal 3D configuration (\texttt{F3\_111}; Fig.~\ref{fig:n3_unified_3d_fractal_factorial}).

Translating these principles into a 3D repulsive force in vacuum requires overcoming parallel-plate area dominance and mutual evanescent screening (Fig.~\ref{fig:rotational_clutch_architecture}a). First, whenever two 3D bodies share vertical face-to-face surface overlap ($A_{\rm overlap} > 0$), normal parallel-plate attraction ($F_z^{\rm PFA} \propto -A_{\rm overlap}/d^4$) overwhelms lateral sidewall fringing. Second, even when vertical surface overlap is eliminated by interdigitating metallic teeth into open slots\cite{levin2010}, a uniform periodic grating of slot width $W_0$ couples every track to the same imaginary wavenumber band $\kappa \sim \pi/W_0$, so adjacent slots screen one another's transverse saddle-point fringing fields. By contrast, organizing concentric interdigitated slots into a prefractal Cantor hierarchy with generation-scaled radial widths $W_k = W_1 b^{-(k-1)}$, azimuthal lobe sub-apertures, and stepped vertical tooth depths $H_k$ distributes the 3D scattering interaction across separated wavenumber bands $\kappa_k \sim b^{k-1}\pi/W_1$. Narrower child tracks and recursive internal sub-cavities can then contribute strong saddle-point repulsive force in the near field with reduced screening of the primary slot.

We implement this zero-overlap PFA-cancellation geometry in vacuum using a concentric Cantor-ring rotary actuator comprising a four-fold symmetric ($C_4$, $90^\circ$ sector period) gold rotor suspended above a stationary gold stator (Fig.~\ref{fig:rotational_clutch_architecture}b--d). Downward-protruding concentric rotor teeth interdigitate radially between thin upper stator aperture rings positioned above recessed azimuthal landing pads (Methods). In-plane rotation $\theta$ (driven by an external rotary actuator) switches the axial Casimir force between two distinct sign regimes at zero electrical bias. In the disengaged open-slot state ($\theta = 0^\circ\text{--}6^\circ$; Fig.~\ref{fig:rotational_clutch_architecture}b,c), the rotor teeth sit centred inside open stator slots with zero vertical landing-pad overlap ($A_{\rm overlap} = 0$, $F_z^{\rm PFA} = 0$). Because the tooth tips extend below the stator aperture plane, transverse saddle-point fringing fields pull the rotor upward toward the slot mid-plane, generating an axial repulsive force ($F_z > 0$) governed by 3D sidewall scattering. Conversely, in the engaged landing-pad state ($\theta = 18^\circ\text{--}48^\circ$; Fig.~\ref{fig:rotational_clutch_architecture}d), rotating the rotor aligns the bottom faces of the teeth directly over the recessed stator landing pads across a narrow vertical vacuum gap ($d = 15\text{ nm}$), where parallel-plate surface overlap ($A_{\rm overlap} > 0$) dominates over sidewall fringing and switches the axial force to strong normal attraction ($F_z < 0$).

To trace this progression from 1D radial hierarchies to the optimal 3D $(r, \theta, z)$ configuration while separating multiscale width polydispersity, Cantor flanking order, periodic screening, and cylindrical-radius weighting, we first evaluate twelve core 3D radial cavity constellations spanning prefractal generations $N=0$ to $N=3$ alongside matched $N=2$ (three-track) and $N=3$ (seven-track) non-fractal control families (Fig.~\ref{fig:rotational_clutch_architecture}e, Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:all_constellations_blender}, and Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:fractal_geometry_control}; full coordinates in Methods). We next isolate the 3D wavenumber sub-band partitioning and vertical-stroke log-periodic oscillations predicted by Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier_running} (Fig.~\ref{fig:clutch_log_periodicity}), and use this spectral diagnosis to guide a complete $2^3$ factorial ablation across radial ($u_r$), azimuthal ($u_\theta$), and axial ($u_z$) Cantor recursion at $N=3$ ($R \in [\FractalNthreeCantorseventrackRInUm{}, \FractalNthreeCantorseventrackROutUm{}]\ \mu\text{m}$, $z_{\rm pad,top} = -115\text{ nm}$) benchmarked against ten matched 7-tooth and 12-tooth/24-lobe non-fractal controls (Fig.~\ref{fig:n3_unified_3d_fractal_factorial}, Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:n3_unified_3d_blender_atlas}, and Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:unified_3d_fractal_factorial}).

Because unrounded $90^\circ$ metallic corners exhibit singular edge-current crowding ($\rho \sim r_{\rm edge}^{-1/3}$) that causes raw BEM forces on sharp edges to grow with panel density, all comparative geometry benchmarks in Figs.~\ref{fig:quantum_clutch_architecture_and_narrative} and \ref{fig:n3_unified_3d_fractal_factorial} and Extended Data Tables~\ref{tab:fractal_geometry_control} and \ref{tab:unified_3d_fractal_factorial} are evaluated at matched Standard panel density; introducing a physical $r_{\rm edge} = 5\text{ nm}$ lithographic corner radius regularizes the mesh-refinement slope ($+\roundthree{\CantorNtwoHybridrzBemRfiveNmStdFZPn} \to +\roundthree{\CantorNtwoHybridrzBemRfiveNmHighFZPn} \to +\roundthree{\CantorNtwoHybridrzBemRfiveNmUltraFZPn}\text{ pN}$ on the hybrid $N=2$ Cantor ring versus $+\roundthree{\CtrlUniformperiodicBemRfiveNmStdFZPn} \to +\roundthree{\CtrlUniformperiodicBemRfiveNmHighFZPn} \to +\roundthree{\CtrlUniformperiodicBemRfiveNmUltraFZPn}\text{ pN}$ on the uniform three-track grating) while preserving a stable Cantor-over-uniform amplification ratio of $1.37\times\text{--}1.42\times$ across all mesh tiers (Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum_clutch_3solver_verification}a and Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:quantum_clutch}).

On the single-track baseline ($N=0$), deep interdigitation of the rotor tooth inside the open stator slot at $\theta=3^\circ$ ($z_{\rm tip} = -100\text{ nm}$, extending $75\text{ nm}$ below the stator ring bottom) generates an upward repulsive axial force of $+\roundtwo{\SingletoothTrackzeroBemThetathreeFZPn}\text{ pN}$, which switches to parallel-plate attraction of $\roundtwo{\SingletoothTrackzeroBemThetafoureightFZPn}\text{ pN}$ when rotated over the landing pad at $\theta=48^\circ$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum_clutch_architecture_and_narrative}a,b). Retracting the tooth upward to $z_{\rm tip} = -55\text{ nm}$ so that its top face overhangs the stator upper plane by $+45\text{ nm}$ reverses the vertical asymmetry across the slot mid-plane and flips the $\theta=3^\circ$ open-slot force from repulsion to attraction ($\roundtwo{\SingletoothTrackzeroOverhangBemThetathreeFZPn}\text{ pN}$), confirming that axial repulsion originates from saddle-point sidewall fringing\cite{levin2010}.

Single-scale interdigitation, however, fails to scale across a wide annular cavity (Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum_clutch_architecture_and_narrative}c--e and Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:fractal_geometry_control}). To test whether the first-principles 1D log-periodic oscillation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier_running} ($\omega_k = 2\pi k/\ln b$) survives in a 3D electromagnetic ring cavity---and to diagnose how it governs 3D optimization---we analyze both the imaginary-wavenumber coupling spectrum of a coplanar clutch and the vertical stroke response of a vertically stepped clutch (Fig.~\ref{fig:clutch_log_periodicity} and Methods). In an exact-scaling coplanar $N=3$ Cantor ring ($b=3$, radial clearances $g_{r,m} = g_{r,1} b^{-(m-1)} \in \{135, 45, 15\}\text{ nm}$), all seven concentric tracks engage at a single vertical plane ($z_{\rm tip} = -100\text{ nm}$), superposing all wavenumber contributions at a single stroke depth. Decomposing the diagnostic 3D Vector-Dyadic BEM radial-sidewall coupling norm $w_m(\kappa)$ alongside the exact signed force integrand $\sum_{m=1}^3 \kappa f_{z,m}(\kappa)$ across $\ln\kappa$ illustrates how the three radial clearance generations separate into distinct spectral sub-bands peaking at $\kappa_{{\rm peak},1} = \roundtwo{\LogperKPeakLoneUmInv}\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$ ($\ln\kappa = \roundtwo{\LogperLnKPeakLone}$), $\kappa_{{\rm peak},2} = \roundtwo{\LogperKPeakLtwoUmInv}\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$ ($\ln\kappa = \roundtwo{\LogperLnKPeakLtwo}$), and $\kappa_{{\rm peak},3} = \roundtwo{\LogperKPeakLthreeUmInv}\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$ ($\ln\kappa = \roundtwo{\LogperLnKPeakLthree}$), whereas the three-track uniform periodic control exhibits a single wavenumber band (Fig.~\ref{fig:clutch_log_periodicity}a and Methods). Because finite vertical ring thickness ($H = 100\text{ nm} \sim g_{r,1}$) and gold plasma skin-depth penetration ($\delta_{\rm skin} \approx 22\text{ nm} > g_{r,3}=15\text{ nm}$) regularize the near-field Yukawa decay, the empirical inter-generation peak ratios ($\kappa_{{\rm peak},2}/\kappa_{{\rm peak},1} = \roundtwo{\LogperKPeakRatioTwoone}$ and $\kappa_{{\rm peak},3}/\kappa_{{\rm peak},2} = \roundtwo{\LogperKPeakRatioThreetwo}$) are compressed relative to the idealized knife-edge clearance ratio $b=3$.

This spectral separation highlights a design trade-off between co-focused peak force at a single gap and multi-stage force modulation across a vertical stroke. When all tracks share a common vertical plane ($z_m = z_0$), their repulsive contributions add at one elevation. Conversely, staggering the stator slot centres and track cross-sections geometrically in both radius and vertical elevation as $|z_{{\rm stage},m}| = |z_{{\rm stage},1}| b^{-(m-1)}$ and $\ell_m = \ell_1 b^{-(m-1)}$ ($b=\LogperSteppedRzBScale{}$, $|z_{{\rm stage},m}| \in \{250, 100, 40\}\text{ nm}$; Methods) unfolds the three stages into a sequential, geometrically staged force profile along the vertical rotor coordinate $z_{\rm rotor}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:clutch_log_periodicity}b,c).

Sweeping the rotor vertically across $36$ logarithmically spaced depths $|z_{\rm rotor}| \in [25, 450]\text{ nm}$ ($\ln(|z_{\rm rotor}|/\ell_3) \in [-0.47, 2.42]$) in 3D Vector-Dyadic BEM shows that as the rotor passes each staggered elevation stage $z_{{\rm stage},m}$, the scale-compensated repulsive force $|z_{\rm rotor}|^2 F_z(z_{\rm rotor})$ exhibits three local maxima spaced by the designed logarithmic stage interval $\Delta\ln|z| \approx \ln b = \roundthree{\LogperSteppedRzLnB}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:clutch_log_periodicity}b). To quantify how closely this multi-stage stroke response follows the log-periodic functional form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier_running} relative to non-geometric staging, we fit $|z|^2 F_z$ via a joint quadratic-plus-harmonic ordinary least-squares regression at the geometric scaling frequency $\omega_1 = 2\pi/\ln b = \roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzOmegaone}$ and via an unconstrained free-frequency nonlinear / Lomb--Scargle sweep across $\omega \in [1.0, 14.0]$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:clutch_log_periodicity}c, inset, and Methods). At $\omega_1 = \roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzOmegaone}$, the geometrically stepped Cantor clutch yields a fundamental harmonic amplitude of $A_1 = (2.58 \pm 0.46) \times 10^{-3}\text{ pN}\cdot\mu\text{m}^2$ ($R^2 = \roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzRtwo}$, regression $F = \roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzFstatCantor}$, $p = 1.9 \times 10^{-4}$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} = \roundone{\LogperSteppedRzDeltaAicCantor}$, $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = \roundone{\LogperSteppedRzDeltaBicCantor}$ relative to the quadratic baseline), exceeding the single-stage uniform control ($A_1 = \roundfour{\LogperSteppedRzAmponeUniform}\text{ pN}\cdot\mu\text{m}^2$, $R^2 = \roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzRtwoUniform}$) by $\roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzAmponeRatio}\times$ in amplitude, whereas a non-geometric three-stage control with linearly spaced stator elevations ($|z_{\rm stage}| \in \{250, 145, 40\}\text{ nm}$) produces a non-log-periodic profile that is penalized at $\omega_1 = 2\pi/\ln b$ ($R^2 = \roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzRtwoLinear}$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} = +\roundone{\LogperSteppedRzDeltaAicLinear}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:clutch_log_periodicity}b,c). \subsection*{1D transfer-matrix recursion and motivating log-periodic scaling ansatz}
To motivate the scale-dependent Casimir coefficient $C(d_s, \ln(d/\ell_*))$ in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:trace_running}--\eqref{eq:fourier_running}\cite{gordon2026prd}, we consider the idealized 1D reduced Euclidean Green's function $g(z, z'; \kappa)$ at imaginary wavenumber $\kappa = \sqrt{\zeta^2 + \mathbf{k}_\perp^2}$ across an $N$-th generation pre-Cantor stack $\mathcal{C}_N$ of semitransparent delta-function barriers $V(z) = \lambda_0 \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_N} \delta(z - z_i)$ on $[0, d]$:
\begin{equation}
\left[ -\frac{\partial^2}{\partial z^2} + \kappa^2 + V(z) \right] g(z, z'; \kappa) = \delta(z - z').
\end{equation}
In the homogeneous vacuum intervals between barriers, $g(z, z'; \kappa) = A_j e^{-\kappa z} + B_j e^{\kappa z}$, where wave amplitudes across barrier $z_i$ are linked by the single-plate transfer matrix $\mathbf{M}_i(\kappa)$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}_i(\kappa) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - \frac{\lambda_0}{2\kappa} & -\frac{\lambda_0}{2\kappa} e^{2\kappa z_i} \\ \frac{\lambda_0}{2\kappa} e^{-2\kappa z_i} & 1 + \frac{\lambda_0}{2\kappa} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{M}_{\rm global}(\kappa) = \prod_{i \in \mathcal{C}_N} \mathbf{M}_i(\kappa).
\end{equation}
For any finite coupling $\lambda_0 < \infty$, high-frequency modes ($\kappa \gg \lambda_0$) tunnel across barriers with transmission probability $|t(\kappa)|^2 = 4\kappa^2 / (4\kappa^{2} + \lambda_0^2) > 0$, establishing global thermal equilibrium across all sub-cavities at temperature $T$ prior to taking the strong-coupling limit\cite{gordon2026prd}. Partitioning the $N$-th generation Cantor stack (scale ratio $b=3$) into its left and right $(N-1)$-th generation sub-stacks yields the functional interaction energy recursion $\Delta e_{\mathcal{C}_N}(d) = 2\Delta e_{\mathcal{C}_{N-1}}(d/b) + \Delta e_{\rm cross}(d; \ell_*)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:recursion_main}. In $(3+1)$ dimensions, the interaction energy per unit area carries scaling dimension $[\text{length}]^{-3}$; assuming that in the asymptotic prefractal scaling window $\ell_* \ll d \ll L$ the renormalized cross-talk $\Delta e_{\rm cross}$ approaches a discrete scale-invariant fixed point under spatial dilation $d \to d/b$ ($\Delta e_{\rm hom}(d/b) = b^3 \Delta e_{\rm hom}(d)$ with multiplicative periodicity in $\ln(d/\ell_*)$ of period $\ln b$), expanding the general solution in complex scaling frequencies $\omega_k = 2\pi k / \ln b$ ($k \in \mathbb{Z}$) yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier_running}:
\begin{equation}
e(d) = \frac{\hbar c}{d^3} \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k \, e^{i \frac{2\pi k}{\ln b} \ln(d/\ell_*)} = \frac{\hbar c}{d^3} C\!\left(d_s, \ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right).
\end{equation}
At finite temperature $T$, compactifying imaginary time with period $\beta = \hbar/(k_B T)$ replaces the continuous $\zeta$-integral with the Matsubara sum over $\zeta_n = 2\pi n k_B T/\hbar$, generalizing $C$ to the bivariate function $C_T(d_s, \ln(d/\ell_*), d/\lambda_T)$ with thermal wavelength $\lambda_T = \hbar c / (2\pi k_B T)$\cite{gordon2026prd}. We emphasize that in 3D interdigitated rings, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:trace_running}--\eqref{eq:fourier_running} serve as a motivating heuristic framework; all 3D force, torque, and spectral predictions in this work are computed directly from the 3D BEM and FDTD electromagnetic solvers below without assuming a value for $d_s$ or $C_k$.

\subsection*{Three-dimensional clutch architecture and constellation coordinates}
All evaluated 3D cavity constellations operate in vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) with exact four-fold cyclic rotational symmetry ($C_4$, $90^\circ$ sector period; internal repository catalog labels such as \texttt{Rot\_C4\_TightSlot\_N2}, \texttt{Rot\_C8\_Compact\_N2}, and \texttt{Rot\_C10\_Fractal\_N3} correspond to TightSlot-N2, Compact-N2, and Cantor-N3-7Track and denote catalog indices 4, 8, and 10 within our $C_4$-symmetric design sweep rather than 8- or 10-fold rotational symmetry; Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:all_constellations_blender} and Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:fractal_geometry_control}). \subsection*{3D boundary-element method, $C_4$ cyclic Bloch reduction and canonical validation}
In three dimensions, the Casimir interaction energy $E_C$ and axial force $F_z$ between the disjoint rotor and stator bodies in vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) are evaluated via the boundary-integral scattering log-determinant along the imaginary wavenumber axis $\kappa = \xi/c$\cite{reid2013pra}:
\begin{equation}
E_C = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi} \int_0^\infty d\kappa \, \ln\det\bigl(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}(\kappa)\bigr),
\qquad
F_z = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi} \int_0^\infty d\kappa \, \mathrm{Tr}\!\left[\bigl(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}(\kappa)\bigr)^{-1} \frac{\partial \mathbf{M}(\kappa)}{\partial z_{\rm rotor}}\right],
\label{eq:methods_bem}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{M}(\kappa) = -\mathbf{D}_{\rm diag}^{-1}(\kappa)\mathbf{V}_{\rm off}(\kappa)$ is the normalized block off-diagonal rotor--stator coupling operator constructed from the block-diagonal self-interaction matrix $\mathbf{D}_{\rm diag} = \mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{\Gamma}_{\rm rot,rot}, \mathbf{\Gamma}_{\rm stat,stat})$ and off-diagonal mutual-interaction block $\mathbf{V}_{\rm off}$. Writing $\ln\det(\mathbf{\Gamma}\mathbf{\Gamma}_\infty^{-1}) = \ln\det(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}(\kappa))$ subtracts the isolated self-energies of the rigid rotor and stator identically at every wavenumber $\kappa$, so that $\partial\mathbf{M}/\partial z_{\rm rotor}$ differentiates only the rotor--stator translation blocks. Surface panels are discretized with piecewise-constant collocation unknowns weighted by the dispersive Drude dielectric function of gold ($\omega_p = 9.03\text{ eV}$, $\gamma = 0.053\text{ eV}$)\cite{rakic1998}. In the diagnostic 3D Scalar-Yukawa formulation, panel interactions are governed by the scalar Green's function $G_0(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';\kappa) = \exp(-\kappa |\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|)/(4\pi |\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|)$ (one scalar degree of freedom per panel), whereas the 3D Vector-Dyadic formulation couples two orthogonal tangential surface-current components per panel via the dyadic electromagnetic Green's tensor $\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';\kappa) = (\mathbf{I} - \kappa^{-2}\nabla\nabla')G_0(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';\kappa)$. Coincident and near-field panel integrals are regularized at the sub-panel quadrature length scale $\sigma_{\rm panel} = \sqrt{(A_t+A_s)/(32\pi)}$ (with exact analytical rectangular self-term integration verified in ablation tests below), where $A_t$ and $A_s$ denote target and source panel areas.

Exploiting the four-fold cyclic symmetry ($C_4$) of the concentric ring sectors block-diagonalizes the dense $N_{\rm total} \times N_{\rm total}$ scattering matrix into four decoupled Bloch irreducible representations ($\ell_{\rm B} \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$) via the cyclic projection $\mathbf{M}^{(\ell_{\rm B})}(\kappa) = \sum_{q=0}^{3} e^{i\pi \ell_{\rm B} q/2}\mathbf{M}_{0,q}(\kappa)$, where $\mathbf{M}_{0,q}$ couples the fundamental sector to the $q$-th rotated sector and each decoupled block is factorized via dense pivoted LU decomposition (\texttt{scipy.linalg.lu\_factor}). This exact symmetry reduction decreases memory footprint by $16\times$ and accelerates LU factorization by $64\times$. We discretize the surfaces across three hierarchical mesh tiers: Standard ($656$ panels for $N=0,1$; $\RotCfourTightslotNtwoStandardThetaZerozerosixpzeroPanelCount{}$ panels for $N=2$ and 3-track controls; $\RotConezeroFractalNthreeStandardThetaZerozerothreepzeroPanelCount{}$ panels for 7-track $N=3$ and controls; up to $6{,}784$ total panels across 4 sectors for the 12-tooth / 24-lobe 3D Cantor configurations), High ($\RotCfourTightslotNtwoHighThetaZerozerosixpzeroPanelCount{}$ panels for $N=2$; $\RotConezeroFractalNthreeHighThetaZerozerothreepzeroPanelCount{}$ panels for $N=3$), and Ultra-High ($\RotCfourTightslotNtwoUltraHighThetaZerozerosixpzeroPanelCount{}$ panels for $N=2$). At $T = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoCdToleranceTemperatureK{}\text{ K}$, the continuous $\kappa$-integral is replaced by Matsubara summation over $\kappa_n = 2\pi n k_B T / (\hbar c)$ with prime weight $1/2$ at the static $n=0$ transverse-magnetic mode (evaluated at static regularization wavenumber $\kappa_0 = 10^{-6}\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$, while the static $n=0$ transverse-electric reflection vanishes in accordance with the Bohr--van Leeuwen theorem).

Beyond our three-solver rotary clutch comparisons, we validated the 3D Vector-Dyadic BEM solver against three canonical analytical and internal-consistency benchmarks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Analytical 1D Lifshitz parallel-plate benchmark}: Evaluating two parallel circular Drude gold disks ($R = 1.0\ \mu\text{m}$, area $A = \pi\ \mu\text{m}^2$) across separations $d \in \{100, 50, 30, 15\}\text{ nm}$ ($d/R \in \{0.100, 0.050, 0.030, 0.015\}$) on Standard, High, and Ultra-High meshes yields continuum-extrapolated BEM pressures of $\roundtwo{\BemValPlateBemContinuumDonezerozeronmPa}\text{ Pa}$ ($d=100\text{ nm}$, vs.\ exact 1D Lifshitz $\roundtwo{\BemValLifshitzExactDonezerozeronmPa}\text{ Pa}$, relative error $\roundtwo{\BemValPlateRelErrContinuumDonezerozeronmPct}\%$), $\roundtwo{\BemValPlateBemContinuumDfivezeronmPa}\text{ Pa}$ ($d=50\text{ nm}$, vs.\ exact $\roundtwo{\BemValLifshitzExactDfivezeronmPa}\text{ Pa}$, relative error $\roundtwo{\BemValPlateRelErrContinuumDfivezeronmPct}\%$), $\roundtwo{\BemValPlateBemContinuumDthreezeronmPa}\text{ Pa}$ ($d=30\text{ nm}$, vs.\ exact $\roundtwo{\BemValLifshitzExactDthreezeronmPa}\text{ Pa}$, relative error $\roundtwo{\BemValPlateRelErrContinuumDthreezeronmPct}\%$), and $\roundtwo{\BemValPlateBemContinuumDonefivenmPa}\text{ Pa}$ ($d=15\text{ nm}$, vs.\ exact $\roundtwo{\BemValLifshitzExactDonefivenmPa}\text{ Pa}$, relative error $\roundtwo{\BemValPlateRelErrContinuumDonefivenmPct}\%$), confirming monotonic convergence to the exact 1D Lifshitz limit as finite-disk edge diffraction ($O(d/R)$) vanishes.
    \item \emph{Energy-derivative versus trace-formula consistency}: Comparing the direct analytical trace formula in Eq.~\eqref{eq:methods_bem} against central finite-difference differentiation of the log-determinant Casimir energy $F_z^{\rm FD} = -[E_C(z_0 + \Delta z) - E_C(z_0 - \Delta z)]/(2\Delta z)$ ($\Delta z = \BemValCantorTraceVsFdStepNm{}\text{ nm}$) on Cantor-N2-HybridRZ yields $+\roundfour{\BemValCantorThetathreeTraceFzPn}\text{ pN}$ (trace) versus $+\roundfour{\BemValCantorThetathreeFdFzPn}\text{ pN}$ (finite difference) in the disengaged repulsive state ($\theta=3^\circ$, $\roundthree{\BemValCantorThetathreeTraceVsFdAgreementPct}\%$ agreement, relative discrepancy $\roundthree{\BemValCantorThetathreeTraceVsFdRelErrPct}\%$) and $\roundfour{\BemValCantorThetafoureightTraceFzPn}\text{ pN}$ (trace) versus $\roundfour{\BemValCantorThetafoureightFdFzPn}\text{ pN}$ (finite difference) in the engaged attractive state ($\theta=48^\circ$, $\roundfour{\BemValCantorThetafoureightTraceVsFdAgreementPct}\%$ agreement, relative discrepancy $\roundfour{\BemValCantorThetafoureightTraceVsFdRelErrPct}\%$).
    \item \emph{Direct $T=300\text{ K}$ Matsubara pole-truncation convergence}: Doubling the direct Matsubara pole sum ($\Delta\kappa = 2\pi k_B T/(\hbar c) = \roundthree{\BemValMatsubaraDeltaKappaPerUm}\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$) from $N_{\rm Matsubara} = \BemValMatsubaraCutoffNCount{}$ ($\kappa_{\max} = \roundtwo{\BemValMatsubaraKappaCutoffPerUm}\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$, $\hbar\omega_{\max} = \roundtwo{\BemValMatsubaraEnergyCutoffEv}\text{ eV}$) to $2N_{\rm Matsubara} = \BemValMatsubaraCutoffTwonCount{}$ ($\BemValMatsubaraPolesEvaluatedCount{}$ poles including $n=0$) on Cantor-N2-HybridRZ shifts $F_z(3^\circ)$ from $+\roundfour{\BemValMatsubaraCantorThetathreeFNPn}\text{ pN}$ to $+\roundfour{\BemValMatsubaraCantorThetathreeFTwonPn}\text{ pN}$ (tail residual $\roundtwo{\BemValMatsubaraCantorThetathreeTailRelPct}\%$) and $F_z(48^\circ)$ from $\roundfour{\BemValMatsubaraCantorThetafoureightFNPn}\text{ pN}$ to $\roundfour{\BemValMatsubaraCantorThetafoureightFTwonPn}\text{ pN}$ (tail residual $\roundtwo{\BemValMatsubaraCantorThetafoureightTailRelPct}\%$).

\subsection*{4-pass inclusion--exclusion 3D FDTD methodology}
To provide an independent time-domain electromagnetic benchmark in vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$), we integrate the Wick-rotated Maxwell stress tensor $\mathbf{T}$ over closed per-tooth vacuum bounding boxes $S_i$ surrounding each rotor tooth inside its radial stator slot\cite{rodriguez2009pra,oskooi2010,rodriguez2011}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
F_z &= \frac{\hbar}{\pi} \int_0^\infty d\xi \, \sum_{i} \oint_{S_i} d\mathbf{A} \cdot \langle \mathbf{T}(i\xi, \mathbf{x}) \rangle_z, \\
T_{zz} &= \frac{1}{2} \left[ \varepsilon_0 (E_z^2 - E_x^2 - E_y^2) + \mu_0 (H_z^2 - H_x^2 - H_y^2) \right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:methods_fdtd}
\end{equation}
To eliminate both the rotor's self-force and the uncoupled background scattering of the stator slot walls across the bounding-box faces, each discrete-cosine-transform (DCT) boundary moment is computed using a 4-pass inclusion--exclusion subtraction at identical Yee-grid alignment with subpixel permittivity averaging disabled (\texttt{eps\_averaging = False}) and Courant factor $0.3$:
\begin{equation}
F_z^{\rm net} = \bigl(F_z^{\rm both} - F_z^{\rm rotor}\bigr) - \bigl(F_z^{\rm stator} - F_z^{\rm vac}\bigr).
\label{eq:methods_four_pass}
\end{equation}
With $n_{\rm max}=1$ DCT moments across the $6$ bounding-box faces of each rotor tooth ($\FdtdMomentsPerToothPerPass{}$ moments per tooth per pass across $\FdtdInclusionExclusionPasses{}$ passes), the $N=0$ single-track geometry ($4$ teeth across the $4$ sectors) evaluates $\NaturemeeptaskzerozerooneMomentsPerConfig{}$ moments per pass ($\NaturemeeptaskzerozerooneTotalMoments{}$ total 4-pass moments), the $N=2$ and 3-track control geometries ($12$ teeth) evaluate $\NaturemeeptaskzerozerofourMomentsPerConfig{}$ moments per pass ($\NaturemeeptaskzerozerofourTotalMoments{}$ total 4-pass moments), and the $N=3$ 7-track Cantor geometry ($28$ teeth) evaluates $\RotConezeroFractalNthreeMomentsPerConfig{}$ moments per pass ($\RotConezeroFractalNthreeTotalMoments{}$ total 4-pass moments). All 4-pass FDTD MEEP simulations were executed across sharded MPI worker groups on the BigRed 200 supercomputer using the 6-pole Raki\'c Lorentz--Drude susceptibility model for gold\cite{rakic1998} in vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) and converted to SI piconewtons via $\hbar c / a^2 = 0.0316152677\text{ pN}\cdot\mu\text{m}^2$ at $a = 1\ \mu\text{m}$.

\subsection*{Wavenumber sub-band decomposition and stepped $(r,z)$ log-periodic stroke protocol}
To connect the discrete scaling spectrum $\omega_k = 2\pi k / \ln b$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier_running} to the 3D boundary-integral scattering formalism of Eq.~\eqref{eq:methods_bem}, we evaluate both a diagnostic radial-wall coupling norm $w_m(\kappa)$ and the exact signed axial force integrand $f_{z,m}(\kappa)$ across prefractal generations $m \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ in vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$). To test exact geometric clearance scaling without minimum-feature lithographic clamping, we analyze an exact-scaling coplanar $N=3$ Cantor ring ($b=3$, spanning $R \in [0.20, 1.55]\ \mu\text{m}$ with unconstrained minimum tooth width $w_{\rm min} = 5\text{ nm}$ so that radial slot-to-tooth clearances scale strictly as $g_{r,m} = g_{r,1} b^{-(m-1)} \in \{135, 45, 15\}\text{ nm}$ across $\Delta\theta_{\rm tooth} = 8^\circ$ teeth centred inside $\Delta\theta_{\rm slot} = 24^\circ$ stator apertures at $\theta = 12^\circ$). Let $\mathcal{P}_{\rm rot}^{(m)}$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\rm stat}^{(m)}$ denote the sets of radial-sidewall surface panels (\texttt{rotor\_radial\_wall} and \texttt{stator\_radial\_wall}) belonging to generation $m$. Isolating the generation-$m$ radial-sidewall block of the $C_4$-projected rotor--stator single-layer dyadic Green's tensor across $\kappa \in [1.5, 150]\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$ ($21$ logarithmically spaced points) defines the non-negative diagnostic coupling norm $w_m(\kappa)$ alongside the exact signed generation-$m$ trace integrand $f_{z,m}(\kappa) = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi}\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{P}^{(m)}}\!\bigl[(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{M}(\kappa))^{-1}\partial_{z_{\rm rotor}}\mathbf{M}(\kappa)\bigr]$:
\begin{equation}
w_m(\kappa) = \frac{1}{2}\kappa^6 \left( |r_{\rm TM}(\kappa)|^2 + |r_{\rm TE}(\kappa)|^2 \right) \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_{\rm rot}^{(m)}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}_{\rm stat}^{(m)}} \left| \bigl[\mathbf{G}_{\rm RS}^{(0)}(\kappa)\bigr]_{ij} \right|^2,
\label{eq:methods_spectral_subband}
\end{equation}
where $r_{\rm TM}(\kappa)$ and $r_{\rm TE}(\kappa)$ are the dispersive Drude gold Fresnel reflection weights evaluated at characteristic wavenumber $k_{\rm eff} = 1/\max(g_{\rm min}, 20\text{ nm})$. Because each generation-$m$ radial cavity filters evanescent modes via the Yukawa kernel $\exp(-\kappa |\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|) \sim \exp(-\kappa g_{r,m})$, $w_m(\kappa)$ peaks at $\kappa_{{\rm peak},m} \propto 1/g_{r,m} = b^{m-1}/g_{r,1}$, while the sum of the signed trace integrand $\sum_{m=1}^{3} \kappa f_{z,m}(\kappa)$ reproduces the total BEM axial force within machine precision ($<10^{-16}\text{ pN}\cdot\mu\text{m}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:clutch_log_periodicity}a). Panels in $\mathcal{P}_{\rm rot}^{(m)}$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\rm stat}^{(m)}$ are classified by matching the exact cylindrical vertex radii ($\min/\max \sqrt{x_v^2 + y_v^2}$ across all four vertices within $10^{-5}\ \mu\text{m}$ tolerance) rather than panel centroids, eliminating chord-sag radial shifts $r\cos(\Delta\phi/2)$ on finite-angle annular bridge panels ($R_{\max} = 1.55\ \mu\text{m}$); unassigned non-tooth panels are indexed to level $0$ and excluded from generation-specific sums.

To unfold these wavenumber sub-bands into a multi-stage force response along the vertical actuator coordinate $z_{\rm rotor}$, we construct the three-generation $(r,z)$-stepped Cantor clutch (Cantor-N3-SteppedRZ, code ID \texttt{Cantor\_N3\_SteppedRZ}, scale factor $b = \LogperSteppedRzBScale{}$, radial centres $R_c \in \{0.45, 0.85, 1.25\}\ \mu\text{m}$ ordered in Cantor flanking sequence $m \in \{3, 1, 2\}$). Both the radial cross-sectional dimensions and the vertical stator slot centres $z_{{\rm stage},m}$ are scaled geometrically by $b^{-(m-1)}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_m &= \ell_1 b^{-(m-1)}, \qquad
W_{{\rm slot},m} = 2.4\,\ell_m, \qquad
w_{{\rm tooth},m} = 1.1\,\ell_m, \\
H_{{\rm ring},m} &= 0.55\,\ell_m, \qquad
z_{{\rm stage},m} = z_{{\rm stage},1} b^{-(m-1)},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:methods_stepped_rz}
\end{equation}
with $\ell_1 = 160\text{ nm}$ ($\ell_m \in \{160.0, 64.0, 25.6\}\text{ nm}$) and $z_{{\rm stage},1} = -250\text{ nm}$ ($z_{{\rm stage},m} \in \{-250, -100, -40\}\text{ nm}$). We compare this geometrically stepped structure against both a non-geometric three-stage control with linearly spaced stator elevations ($z_{{\rm stage},m} \in \{-250, -145, -40\}\text{ nm}$) and a single-scale uniform control that assigns the middle-generation scale ($\ell_2 = 64.0\text{ nm}$, $z_{{\rm stage},2} = -100\text{ nm}$) identically to all three concentric tracks. Sweeping the vertical rotor displacement across $36$ logarithmically spaced depths $|z_{\rm rotor}| \in [25, 450]\text{ nm}$ ($\ln(|z_{\rm rotor}|/\ell_3) \in [-0.47, 2.42]$, $\theta = 3^\circ$, $\Delta\theta_{\rm tooth} = 15^\circ$, $\Delta\theta_{\rm slot} = 30^\circ$, $n_\xi = 12$ Gauss--Legendre quadrature nodes in 3D Vector-Dyadic BEM), we compensate the baseline inverse-square edge-fringing decay by forming $Y(x) = |z_{\rm rotor}|^2 F_z(z_{\rm rotor})$ as a function of logarithmic stroke $x = \ln(|z_{\rm rotor}| / |z_{{\rm stage},3}|)$. We fit $Y(x)$ simultaneously via a $7$-column ordinary least-squares design matrix combining a smooth quadratic baseline $Y_{\rm smooth}(x)$ and the two-harmonic log-periodic expansion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier_running} at the fixed geometric stage frequency $\omega_1 = 2\pi / \ln b = \roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzOmegaone}$:
\begin{equation}
Y_{\rm fit}(x) = \underbrace{c_0 + c_1 x + c_2 x^2}_{Y_{\rm smooth}(x)} + \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{2} \left[ a_k \cos(k \omega_1 x) + b_k \sin(k \omega_1 x) \right]}_{\delta Y_{\rm harm}(x)}, \qquad A_k = \sqrt{a_k^2 + b_k^2},
\label{eq:methods_logper_fit}
\end{equation}
from which the detrended modulation is evaluated as $\delta Y(x) = Y(x) - Y_{\rm smooth}(x)$ with harmonic coefficient of determination $R^2 = 1 - \sum_i [\delta Y(x_i) - \delta Y_{\rm harm}(x_i)]^2 / \sum_i [\delta Y(x_i) - \overline{\delta Y}]^2$ ($R^2 = \roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzRtwo}$, regression $F = \roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzFstatCantor}$, $p = 1.9 \times 10^{-4}$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} = \roundone{\LogperSteppedRzDeltaAicCantor}$, $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = \roundone{\LogperSteppedRzDeltaBicCantor}$ for the geometrically stepped Cantor clutch versus $R^2 = \roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzRtwoLinear}, \Delta\mathrm{AIC} = +\roundone{\LogperSteppedRzDeltaAicLinear}$ for the linearly stepped control and $R^2_{\rm ctrl} = \roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzRtwoUniform}$ for the single-stage uniform control; because the BEM data are deterministic, $F$, $p$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}$, and $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}$ quantify parametric model-fit quality against the competing geometric controls rather than random experimental noise). To verify that the scaling period is recovered without fixing $\omega_1$ a priori, we also sweep the trial frequency freely across $\omega \in [1.0, 14.0]$ ($1{,}301$-point grid plus continuous nonlinear least-squares refinement) using the unconstrained 1-harmonic joint Lomb--Scargle model $Y(x) = c_0 + c_1 x + c_2 x^2 + a_1 \cos(\omega x) + b_1 \sin(\omega x)$ (which avoids $2\omega$ subharmonic aliasing across the $\Delta x = 2.89$ stroke window) alongside a 2-harmonic nonlinear least-squares refinement on $\delta Y(x)$, extracting the unconstrained peak $\hat{\omega}_1 = \roundtwo{\LogperFreeOmegaoneCantor} \pm \roundtwo{\LogperFreeOmegaoneCininefiveHalfCantor}$ ($95\%\text{ CI } [\roundtwo{\LogperFreeOmegaoneCininefiveLowCantor}, \roundtwo{\LogperFreeOmegaoneCininefiveHighCantor}]$, $R^2_1(\hat{\omega}_1) = \roundtwo{\LogperFreeRtwoPeakCantor}$) and recovered scale factor $\hat{b} = \exp(2\pi/\hat{\omega}_1) = \roundtwo{\LogperFreeBHatCantor} \pm \roundtwo{\LogperFreeBHatCininefiveHalfCantor}$ ($95\%\text{ CI } [\roundtwo{\LogperFreeBHatCininefiveLowCantor}, \roundtwo{\LogperFreeBHatCininefiveHighCantor}]$; 2-harmonic refinement $\hat{\omega}_{1,\rm 2h} = \roundtwo{\LogperFreeOmegaoneTwohCantor} \pm \roundtwo{\LogperFreeOmegaoneTwohCininefiveHalfCantor}$, $\hat{b}_{\rm 2h} = \roundtwo{\LogperFreeBHatTwohCantor} \pm \roundtwo{\LogperFreeBHatTwohCininefiveHalfCantor}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:clutch_log_periodicity}c, inset).

In a rotary implementation, the $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ translational flexures suspend the vertically compliant frame while the $48^\circ$ in-plane rotation is driven by a decoupled outer rotary comb actuator or central torsional hub.

A proposed fabrication sequence compatible with standard SOI and damascene gold processing comprises four stages: (1) Starting from an SOI wafer with a single-crystal $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ device layer ($E_Y = \scinum{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityEYPa}\text{ Pa}$), the recessed isolation trench ($z \in [-650, -550]\text{ nm}$) and azimuthal landing pads ($z_{\rm pad,top} = -115\text{ nm}$, thickness $150\text{ nm}$) are patterned via $100\text{ keV}$ electron-beam lithography (EBL) in hydrogen silsesquioxane (HSQ) or PMMA resist, followed by Ti/Au seed deposition and damascene gold electroplating. (2) A sacrificial $\text{SiO}_2$ spacer is deposited by atomic-layer deposition (ALD) and planarized by chemical-mechanical polishing (CMP) to define the $15\text{ nm}$ vertical standoff between the landing pads ($z = -115\text{ nm}$) and rotor tooth tips ($z = -100\text{ nm}$), after which the $25\text{ nm}$-thick slotted Cantor stator aperture rings ($z \in [-25, 0]\text{ nm}$, including the $\ge 50\text{ nm}$ internal Cantor stator sub-ribs in F2rt-110 and F3-111) are patterned via overlay EBL aligned to sub-$5\text{ nm}$ interferometric fiducial marks. (3) Single-mask EBL (for the coplanar 2D $(r,\theta)$ hierarchy F2rt-110) or dual-exposure / grayscale EBL defines the stepped concentric rotor teeth ($\CantorNtwoHybridrzHGenoneNm{}\text{ nm}$ generation-1 and $\CantorNtwoHybridrzHGentwoNm{}\text{ nm}$ generation-2 teeth in Cantor-N2-HybridRZ, or $H_m \in \{90, 80, 70\}\text{ nm}$ across 24 azimuthal lobes per sector in F3-111), anchored to the four radial spider spokes ($z \in [350, 470]\text{ nm}$) and the $\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilitySpokeCount{}$-spoke single-crystal $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ flexure suspension. (4) The sacrificial oxide is removed using anhydrous vapor-phase hydrofluoric acid (vapor-HF) to prevent capillary stiction, followed by ultra-high-vacuum annealing and Kelvin-probe nulling of residual bias voltages below $V_{\rm crit}$.

\subsection*{Rotational energy barrier, Casimir torque and 300 K thermomechanical lock-in SNR}
To evaluate the angular energy barrier and axial-switching Casimir torque across $\theta \in [0^\circ, 48^\circ]$ at $T = 300\text{ K}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:nature_feasibility_stability}e), we compute the 3D Vector-Dyadic BEM Matsubara interaction energy $E_C(\theta)$ across a $10$-point angular grid $\theta \in \{0^\circ, 3^\circ, 6^\circ, 12^\circ, 18^\circ, 24^\circ, 30^\circ, 36^\circ, 42^\circ, 48^\circ\}$ for Cantor-N2-HybridRZ, Compact-N2, and Cantor-N3-7Track. Referencing the interaction energy to the open-slot centre $\Delta E_C(\theta) = E_C(\theta) - E_C(0^\circ)$, the axial-switching Casimir torque is obtained via second-order finite differences with respect to radian rotation angle:
\begin{equation}
\tau_\theta(\theta) = -\frac{\partial E_C(\theta)}{\partial \theta}, \qquad
W_{\rm switch}(48^\circ \to 3^\circ) = \bigl|E_C(3^\circ) - E_C(48^\circ)\bigr|.
\label{eq:methods_rotational_torque}
\end{equation}
As the rotor teeth cross the stator slot sidewalls around $\theta = \NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzRotationalEnergyBarrierThetaEnergyMinDeg{}^\circ\text{--}21^\circ$, $\Delta E_C(\theta)$ reaches an attractive minimum (barrier depth $\roundthree{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzRotationalEnergyBarrierSwitchingBarrierFromMinToDisengagedEv}\text{ eV}$ from minimum to disengaged and $\roundthree{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzRotationalEnergyBarrierSwitchingBarrierPeakToValleyEv}\text{ eV}$ peak-to-valley for Cantor-N2-HybridRZ; $\roundthree{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoRotationalEnergyBarrierSwitchingBarrierFromMinToDisengagedEv}\text{ eV}$ and $\roundthree{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoRotationalEnergyBarrierSwitchingBarrierPeakToValleyEv}\text{ eV}$ for Compact-N2), producing peak torque magnitudes $|\tau_\theta|_{\max} = \roundone{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzPeakTorquePnNm}\text{ pN}\cdot\text{nm}$ (Cantor-N2-HybridRZ), $\roundone{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoPeakTorquePnNm}\text{ pN}\cdot\text{nm}$ (Compact-N2), and $\roundone{\NatureFractalNthreeCantorseventrackPeakTorquePnNm}\text{ pN}\cdot\text{nm}$ (Cantor-N3-7Track) at $\theta = \NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzRotationalEnergyBarrierThetaPeakTorqueDeg{}^\circ$ and a net state-to-state switching work $W_{\rm switch} = \roundtwo{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzRotationalEnergyBarrierSwitchingWorkEv}\text{ eV}$ ($\roundthree{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzRotationalEnergyBarrierSwitchingWorkAj}\text{ aJ} \approx \roundone{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzRotationalEnergyBarrierSwitchingWorkOverKbtthreezerozero}\,k_B T_{300}$) for Cantor-N2-HybridRZ ($\roundtwo{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoRotationalEnergyBarrierSwitchingWorkEv}\text{ eV}$ or $\roundone{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoRotationalEnergyBarrierSwitchingWorkOverKbtthreezerozero}\,k_B T_{300}$ for Compact-N2).

For room-temperature ($T = \NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzThermomechanicalNoiseTemperatureK{}\text{ K}$) thermomechanical noise and lock-in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) analysis (Fig.~\ref{fig:nature_feasibility_stability}f), the effective axial modal mass $m_{\rm eff} = m_{\rm rotor,Au} + \frac{3}{8}m_{\rm spokes,Si}$ combines the 3D gold rotor mass ($\rho_{\rm Au} = 19{,}300\text{ kg}\,\text{m}^{-3}$, $m_{\rm rotor,Au} = \roundtwo{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzThermomechanicalNoiseMRotorGoldPg}\text{ pg}$ for Cantor-N2-HybridRZ and $\roundtwo{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoThermomechanicalNoiseMRotorGoldPg}\text{ pg}$ for Compact-N2) with the Rayleigh clamped-guided modal mass factor ($\frac{3}{8}$) of the four $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ suspension spokes ($\rho_{\rm Si} = 2{,}329\text{ kg}\,\text{m}^{-3}$). For each suspension regime with axial stiffness $k_{z,\rm mech}$, the fundamental axial resonance frequency $f_0$, RMS thermal displacement $\delta z_{\rm th}$, Langevin thermomechanical force noise spectral density $S_F^{1/2}$, and intrinsic lock-in signal-to-noise ratio $\mathrm{SNR}(\tau)$ over integration time $\tau$ are given by:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
f_0 &= \frac{\omega_0}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{k_{z,\rm mech}}{m_{\rm eff}}}, \qquad
\delta z_{\rm th} = \sqrt{\frac{k_B T}{k_{z,\rm mech}}}, \\
S_F^{1/2} &= \sqrt{\frac{4 k_B T k_{z,\rm mech}}{\omega_0 Q}}, \qquad
\mathrm{SNR}(\tau) = \frac{F_{z,300\rm K}^{\rm rep}}{S_F^{1/2} / \sqrt{\tau}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:methods_thermomechanical_snr}
\end{equation}
In Regime~I ($k_{z,\rm mech} = \roundone{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityKZMechPnPerNm}\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$), $\delta z_{\rm th} = \roundtwo{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzThermomechanicalNoiseThermalDzRegimeOneNm}\text{ nm}$ ($\roundtwo{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzThermomechanicalNoiseThermalDzRegimeTwoNm}\text{ nm}$ at $f_0 = \roundone{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzThermomechanicalNoiseResFreqRegimeTwoKhz}\text{ kHz}$ in Regime~II; $\roundtwo{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzThermomechanicalNoiseThermalDzRegimeThreeNm}\text{ nm}$ at $f_0 = \roundone{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzThermomechanicalNoiseResFreqRegimeThreeKhz}\text{ kHz}$ in Regime~III). At ultra-high-vacuum quality factors $Q \in \{10^4, 10^5\}$, lock-in detection yields $\mathrm{SNR}(\tau=1\text{ s}) = \roundzero{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzSnrOneSec}$ for Cantor-N2-HybridRZ ($\roundzero{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoSnrOneSec}$ for Compact-N2; $\roundzero{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzSnrOneSecQoneefive}$ and $\roundzero{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoSnrOneSecQoneefive}$ at $Q=10^5$) in Regime~I and $4{,}791\text{--}6{,}803$ in Regimes~II--III, while the repulsive vertical standoff gradient $\partial F_z/\partial z_0 = \roundthree{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzThermomechanicalNoiseDfzDzzeroPnPerNm}\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$ ($\roundthree{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoThermomechanicalNoiseDfzDzzeroPnPerNm}\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$ for Compact-N2) produces measurable positive resonant frequency shifts across all three suspension regimes.
