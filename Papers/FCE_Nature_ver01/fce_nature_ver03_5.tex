\abstract{%
The quantum fluctuation-induced Casimir force between neutral surfaces in vacuum is ordinarily attractive, governed by face-to-face surface areas and symmetry constraints that preclude repulsion in true vacuum. Although self-similar fractal boundaries are predicted to alter vacuum fluctuations across multiple length scales, observing a macroscopic fractal Casimir effect in three-dimensional cavities has been fundamentally impeded by Weyl's law, because parallel-plate surface overlap overwhelms fractal boundary contributions. In contrast, three-dimensional slot-tooth interdigitation eliminates vertical surface overlap and generates lateral fringing repulsion in true vacuum, opening the possibility of isolating and amplifying fractal vacuum forces. Here we show that combining slot-tooth interdigitation with a multi-scale prefractal Cantor hierarchy unlocks a zero-overlap regime in which fractal boundary recursion governs the macroscopic Casimir interaction. Our first-principles three-dimensional boundary-element and finite-difference time-domain calculations predict that self-similar Cantor rings amplify true-vacuum repulsive lift by $\CantorNtwoFlatzVsUniformBemStdRatio{}\times\text{--}\FractalNthreeVsUniformBemStdRatio{}\times$ over uniform periodic controls, whereas single-scale gratings remain limited by evanescent screening. Furthermore, in a concentric rotary quantum clutch, rotating the interdigitated teeth from open stator slots to recessed landing pads switches the axial force at zero electrical bias between fractal-amplified repulsion and conventional parallel-plate attraction. Together with log-periodic stroke oscillations, room-temperature electromechanical stability criteria, and cavity quantum electrodynamic readouts, these findings establish multi-scale geometry as a fundamental route to control quantum vacuum forces.}

The Casimir effect---the macroscopic force arising from boundary-modified quantum electromagnetic fluctuations\cite{casimir1948,bordag2009}---provides a direct mechanical link between quantum field theory and the geometry of matter at sub-100-nm scales. In ordinary three-dimensional (3D) cavities in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$), however, both the sign and the scaling of this interaction are tightly constrained. By the Kenneth--Klich no-go theorem\cite{kennethklich2006}, any two mirror-symmetric bodies composed of standard conductors or non-magnetic dielectrics in true vacuum mutually attract, while in the proximity-force approximation (PFA) facing parallel surfaces attract in proportion to their overlap area ($F_z^{\rm PFA} \propto -A_{\rm overlap}/d^4$). Although repulsive Casimir--Lifshitz forces have been realized through chemical liquid immersion\cite{munday2009}, and recent advances in rotational Casimir torques\cite{somers2018}, self-assembled microcavities\cite{munkhbat2021}, and nonlinear superconducting resonators\cite{casimir_drum2025,dejong2026} have expanded vacuum metrology, generating and switching amplified repulsive forces in true vacuum without liquid damping remains an open fundamental challenge\cite{rodriguez2011}.

Two seemingly distinct geometric strategies have been proposed to reshape vacuum forces without altering bulk material permittivities. First, in quantum fractals\cite{kempkes2019,xu2021} and self-similar Casimir stacks\cite{akkermans2010,shajesh2016,shajesh2017}, discrete scale invariance breaks continuous conformal symmetry. As formalized in a thermodynamically consistent trace framework incorporating semitransparent mode tunneling and conformal-parameter independence\cite{milton2004,milton2016,gordon2026prd}, a self-similar hierarchy induces a logarithmically running Casimir coefficient that generates a non-vanishing vacuum stress-energy trace anomaly. Yet in prior one-dimensional (1D) scalar models\cite{gordon2026prd}, this running coefficient remained an effective analytical parametrization rather than a first-principles 3D vector electromagnetic scattering result. More fundamentally, in standard 3D cavities with fractal boundaries (``fractal drums''), Weyl's law and Lapidus's theorem\cite{lapidus1991} dictate that the spectral mode count $N_\Omega(\lambda) = C_n |\Omega|\lambda^{n/2} + \mathcal{O}(\lambda^{D/2})$ is dominated by the leading Euclidean volume and surface-area terms $|\Omega|$, while in separation-dependent Casimir interactions any finite parallel-plate overlap area ($A_{\rm overlap} > 0$) produces strong normal attraction that masks subleading perimeter and hierarchical boundary contributions.

Second, Levin et al.\cite{levin2010} demonstrated that reflection symmetry can be broken geometrically in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) without fractals: when a metallic ridge interdigitates with a slotted aperture plate, transverse saddle-point fringing fields connecting the ridge tip to the upper slot sidewalls pull the ridge toward the slot mid-plane, yielding an axial repulsive restoring force along the vertical coordinate (accompanied by lateral Earnshaw instability that must be stabilized by a mechanical suspension)\cite{levin2010,rodriguez2011}. However, a single narrow ridge generates only sub-piconewton forces\cite{levin2010,rodriguez2011}, and scaling this repulsive interaction across a macroscopic 3D cavity has remained unresolved because widening a single tooth collapses the sidewall fringing gradient while repeating identical periodic slots suffers from mutual evanescent screening.

Here we unify these two phenomena by showing that 3D slot-tooth interdigitation and prefractal Cantor recursion are mutually essential counterparts of a macroscopic fractal Casimir effect (Figs.~\ref{fig:rotational_clutch_architecture} and \ref{fig:quantum_clutch_architecture_and_narrative} and Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:all_constellations_blender}). When concentric rotor teeth are aligned inside open stator slots ($\theta = 0^\circ\text{--}6^\circ$), vertical face-to-face surface overlap vanishes identically ($A_{\rm overlap} = 0$). This cancels the leading Euclidean PFA area attraction ($F_z^{\rm PFA} \equiv 0$), opening a zero-overlap fractal window in which multi-scale perimeter fringing and cross-track scattering recursion govern the macroscopic force. Whereas widening a single tooth collapses the sidewall gradient and repeating uniform periodic slots suffers from mutual evanescent screening, organizing the interdigitated tracks into a self-similar Cantor hierarchy distributes fringing fields across logarithmically separated wavenumber bands and amplifies true-vacuum repulsion across generations. Conversely, rotating the teeth over recessed stator landing pads ($\theta = 18^\circ\text{--}48^\circ$) restores face-to-face surface overlap, switching the cavity at zero electrical bias between fractal-amplified repulsion and conventional Euclidean attraction.

\section*{Zero-overlap fractal window and clutch}
\label{sec:architecture}

In an effective field theory of self-similar boundaries\cite{gordon2026prd}, a cavity hierarchy governed by a logarithmically running dimensionless Casimir coefficient $C(d_s, \ln(d/\ell_*))$---where $d_s$ is the spectral dimension, $d$ is the characteristic cavity scale, and $\ell_* = L b^{-N}$ is the $N$-th generation prefractal cutoff below outer scale $L$---generates an anisotropic vacuum stress-energy tensor with a non-vanishing integrated trace\cite{gordon2026prd}:
\begin{equation}
T^\mu{}_{\mu,\rm vac} = -\rho_{\rm vac} + 2P_\parallel + P_\perp = -\frac{\hbar c}{d^4} \,\partial_{\ln d}C\!\left(d_s, \ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right),
\label{eq:trace_running}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_{\rm vac}$ is the renormalized vacuum energy density and $P_\parallel$ and $P_\perp$ denote the transverse (in-plane) and normal (axial) Casimir pressures, respectively. To derive this running coefficient from first principles and connect it to 3D interdigitated cavities, we solve the transfer-matrix scattering recursion for an $N$-th generation pre-Cantor hierarchy $\mathcal{C}_N$ of scale factor $b$ (see Methods). Partitioning a self-similar boundary configuration of outer scale $d$ into its self-similar sub-blocks yields the functional recursion for the renormalized Casimir interaction energy per unit transverse area, $\Delta e_{\mathcal{C}_N}(d)$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta e_{\mathcal{C}_N}(d) = 2 \Delta e_{\mathcal{C}_{N-1}}(d/b) + \Delta e_{\rm cross}(d; \ell_*),
\label{eq:recursion_main}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta e_{\rm cross}$ captures the evanescent cross-talk between hierarchical sub-cavities across imaginary wavenumbers $\kappa = \xi/c$. Under the asymptotic fixed-point scaling of $\Delta e_{\rm cross}$ in the prefractal window $\ell_* \ll d \ll L$ (Methods), solving the discrete scale-invariant functional equation in $(3+1)$ dimensions yields a log-periodic Fourier expansion for the dimensionless Casimir coefficient:
\begin{equation}
C\!\left(d_s, \ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k \, e^{i \omega_k \ln(d/\ell_*)} = C_0(d_s) \left[ 1 + F_{\rm per}\!\left(\ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right) \right], \qquad \omega_k = \frac{2\pi k}{\ln b},
\label{eq:fourier_running}
\end{equation}
where $F_{\rm per}$ is multiplicatively periodic with period $\ln b$ ($b=3$ for middle-third Cantor geometry). Unlike a scale-invariant Euclidean cavity ($C = \text{const}$, $\partial_{\ln d} C = 0$), discrete self-similarity enforces both a non-zero logarithmic scale derivative $\partial_{\ln d} C \neq 0$ along the gap coordinate (manifesting in the stepped 3D clutch stroke and atomic or optomechanical transducers below) and multi-scale wavenumber partitioning across generations $N$.

Realizing the recursion of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:recursion_main}--\eqref{eq:fourier_running} as a macroscopic repulsive force in a 3D cavity requires overcoming two physical obstacles: Euclidean area dominance and mutual evanescent screening (Fig.~\ref{fig:rotational_clutch_architecture}a). First, whenever two 3D bodies share vertical face-to-face surface overlap ($A_{\rm overlap} > 0$), Weyl's law and parallel-plate attraction ($F_z^{\rm PFA} \propto -A_{\rm overlap}/d^4$) overwhelm the boundary recursion in Eq.~\eqref{eq:recursion_main}\cite{lapidus1991}. Second, even when vertical surface overlap is eliminated by interdigitating metallic teeth into open slots\cite{levin2010}, a uniform periodic grating of slot width $W_0$ couples every track to the same imaginary wavenumber band $\kappa \sim \pi/W_0$; adjacent slots then screen one another's transverse saddle-point fringing fields ($\Delta e_{\rm cross} < 0$). By contrast, organizing concentric interdigitated slots into a prefractal Cantor hierarchy with generation-scaled radial widths $W_k = W_1 b^{-(k-1)}$ and vertical depths $H_k$ distributes the 3D scattering log-determinant across logarithmically separated wavenumber bands $\kappa_k \sim b^{k-1}\pi/W_1$, mirroring the discrete scaling spectrum $\omega_k$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier_running}. Shorter-wavelength child tracks thus contribute constructive saddle-point repulsive lift in the near field with reduced screening of the primary slot.

We realize this 3D Weyl-filtering mechanism in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) using a concentric Cantor-ring rotary quantum clutch comprising a four-fold symmetric ($C_4$) gold rotor suspended above a stationary gold stator (Fig.~\ref{fig:rotational_clutch_architecture}b--d). Downward-protruding concentric rotor teeth interdigitate radially between thin upper stator aperture rings positioned above recessed azimuthal landing pads (see Methods for dimensions). In-plane rotation $\theta$ switches the cavity continuously between two distinct physical regimes without applied electrical bias. In the disengaged fractal perimeter state ($\theta = 0^\circ\text{--}6^\circ$; Fig.~\ref{fig:rotational_clutch_architecture}b,c), the rotor teeth sit centred inside open stator slots with zero vertical landing-pad overlap ($A_{\rm overlap} = 0$, $F_z^{\rm PFA} = 0$). Because the tooth tips extend below the stator aperture plane, transverse saddle-point fringing fields pull the rotor upward toward the slot mid-plane, generating repulsive lift ($F_z > 0$) governed entirely by 3D multi-scale sidewall scattering. Conversely, in the engaged Euclidean area state ($\theta = 18^\circ\text{--}48^\circ$; Fig.~\ref{fig:rotational_clutch_architecture}d), rotating the rotor aligns the bottom faces of the teeth directly over the recessed stator landing pads across a narrow vertical vacuum gap ($d = 15\text{ nm}$), where parallel-plate surface overlap ($A_{\rm overlap} > 0$) dominates over sidewall fringing and switches the axial force to strong normal attraction ($F_z < 0$).

To separate multi-scale Cantor recursion from single-slot interdigitation, periodic screening, and cylindrical-radius effects across a fixed annular footprint, we benchmark twelve core 3D cavity constellations spanning prefractal generations $N=0$ to $N=3$ alongside matched $N=2$ and $N=3$ non-fractal control families (Fig.~\ref{fig:rotational_clutch_architecture}e, Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:all_constellations_blender}, and Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:fractal_geometry_control}; full coordinates in Methods). The progression begins with single-track baselines ($N=0$) evaluated at both deep interdigitation and symmetric overhang, followed by a single wide first-generation Cantor slot ($N=1$). At the second generation ($N=2$, three concentric tracks), we compare uniform-depth Cantor rings ($H_1 = H_2 = 100\text{ nm}$) against exact-depth ($H_1 = 100\text{ nm}, H_2 = 33.3\text{ nm}$) and hybrid stepped $(r,z)$ Cantor rings ($H_1 = 100\text{ nm}, H_2 = 90\text{ nm}$), as well as compact rotational variants. We benchmark these $N=2$ hierarchies directly against three non-fractal controls of matched radial span and active tooth area: a single wide tooth, a uniform three-track periodic grating, and a size-matched monotonic array that arranges the exact $N=2$ track widths in increasing radial order rather than self-similar Cantor flanking order. Finally, we extend the hierarchy to the third-generation seven-track Cantor ring ($N=3$, $28$ teeth across four sectors), benchmark it against matched seven-track uniform, monotonic, and randomly permuted controls, and evaluate full-monolithic $N=2$ and $N=3$ structural assemblies incorporating support spokes, hub collars, and substrate trenches.

We calculate the 3D electromagnetic Casimir forces in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) using a $C_4$-symmetric 3D Vector-Dyadic boundary-element method (BEM) with dispersive Drude--Lorentz gold (Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum_clutch_architecture_and_narrative} and Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:fractal_geometry_control}). To validate our results, we compared these calculations against two independent solvers---4-pass 3D finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) simulations and 3D Scalar-Yukawa BEM\cite{oskooi2010,rodriguez2011}---and found quantitative agreement across all operating regimes (Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum_clutch_3solver_verification}, Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:quantum_clutch}, and Methods). Because unrounded $90^\circ$ metallic corners exhibit singular edge-current crowding ($\rho \sim r_{\rm edge}^{-1/3}$), we evaluate all comparative geometry benchmarks at matched \texttt{Standard} mesh density and verify in Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum_clutch_3solver_verification}a and Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:quantum_clutch} that introducing a physical $r_{\rm edge} = 5\text{ nm}$ lithographic corner radius regularizes the BEM mesh-refinement slope while preserving the Cantor-over-uniform amplification ratio ($1.37\times\text{--}1.43\times$ across all mesh tiers).

On the single-track baseline ($N=0$), deep interdigitation of the rotor tooth inside the open stator slot at $\theta=3^\circ$ ($z_{\rm tip} = -100\text{ nm}$, extending $75\text{ nm}$ below the stator ring bottom) generates an upward repulsive force of $+\roundtwo{\SingletoothTrackzeroBemThetathreeFZPn}\text{ pN}$, which switches to strong parallel-plate attraction of $\roundtwo{\SingletoothTrackzeroBemThetafoureightFZPn}\text{ pN}$ when rotated over the landing pad at $\theta=48^\circ$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum_clutch_architecture_and_narrative}a,b). Retracting the tooth upward to $z_{\rm tip} = -55\text{ nm}$ so that its top face overhangs the stator upper plane by $+45\text{ nm}$ reverses the vertical asymmetry across the slot mid-plane and flips the $\theta=3^\circ$ open-slot force from repulsion to attraction ($\roundtwo{\SingletoothTrackzeroOverhangBemThetathreeFZPn}\text{ pN}$), confirming that axial lift originates from saddle-point sidewall fringing\cite{levin2010}.

Single-scale interdigitation, however, fails to scale across a wide annular cavity (Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum_clutch_architecture_and_narrative}c--e and Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:fractal_geometry_control}). Widening a single tooth across the radial span moves the stator slot sidewalls far from the tooth centre, collapsing the transverse fringing gradient and eliminating open-slot repulsion ($F_z = \roundtwo{\CtrlSinglewideBemThetathreeFZPn}\text{ pN}$ at $\theta=3^\circ$). Similarly, the single wide first-generation Cantor slot ($N=1$) yields only $+\roundtwo{\FractalNoneFundringBemThetathreeFZPn}\text{ pN}$ at $\theta=3^\circ$. When the annulus is subdivided into a three-track uniform periodic grating of equal active tooth area ($A_{\rm tooth} = \roundthree{\FractalCtrlUniformperiodicActiveToothAreaUmtwo}\ \mu\text{m}^2$, edge perimeter $P_{\rm edge} = \roundtwo{\FractalCtrlUniformperiodicEdgePerimeterUm}\ \mu\text{m}$), all slots share a single width and couple to the same evanescent wavenumber band; mutual screening between adjacent tracks limits $\theta=3^\circ$ repulsion to $+\roundtwo{\CtrlUniformperiodicBemThetathreeFZPn}\text{ pN}$ ($\roundtwo{\NatureCtrlUniformperiodicFRepPnPerToothAreaUmtwo}\text{ pN}\,\mu\text{m}^{-2}$, or $\roundthree{\FractalCtrlUniformperiodicFRepPnPerUm}\text{ pN}\,\mu\text{m}^{-1}$ per unit perimeter). By contrast, flanking the central primary slot with two narrower second-generation child tracks at matched tooth area and perimeter ($A_{\rm tooth} = \roundthree{\FractalCantorFlatzActiveToothAreaUmtwo}\ \mu\text{m}^2$, $P_{\rm edge} = \roundtwo{\FractalCantorFlatzEdgePerimeterUm}\ \mu\text{m}$) in the flat-depth $N=2$ Cantor ring elevates $\theta=3^\circ$ repulsive lift to $+\roundtwo{\CantorNtwoFlatzBemThetathreeFZPn}\text{ pN}$ ($\roundtwo{\NatureCantorNtwoFlatzFRepPnPerToothAreaUmtwo}\text{ pN}\,\mu\text{m}^{-2}$, $\roundthree{\FractalCantorFlatzFRepPnPerUm}\text{ pN}\,\mu\text{m}^{-1}$)---a $\CantorNtwoFlatzVsUniformBemStdRatio{}\times$ amplification over the uniform periodic grating.

Comparing the $N=2$ Cantor hierarchies against the size-matched monotonic control isolates the role of 3D self-similarity versus cylindrical radius (Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum_clutch_architecture_and_narrative}d,e). \subsection*{Transfer-matrix derivation of the log-periodic running Casimir coefficient}
To derive the scale-dependent Casimir coefficient $C(d_s, \ln(d/\ell_*))$ in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:trace_running}--\eqref{eq:fourier_running} from first principles rather than postulating it phenomenologically\cite{gordon2026prd}, we evaluate the reduced Euclidean Green's function $g(z, z'; \kappa)$ at imaginary wavenumber $\kappa = \sqrt{\zeta^2 + \mathbf{k}_\perp^2}$ across an $N$-th generation pre-Cantor stack $\mathcal{C}_N$ of semitransparent barriers $V(z) = \lambda_0 \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_N} \delta(z - z_i)$ on $[0, d]$:
\begin{equation}
\left[ -\frac{\partial^2}{\partial z^2} + \kappa^2 + V(z) \right] g(z, z'; \kappa) = \delta(z - z').
\end{equation}
In the homogeneous vacuum intervals between barriers, $g(z, z'; \kappa) = A_j e^{-\kappa z} + B_j e^{\kappa z}$, where wave amplitudes across barrier $z_i$ are linked by the single-plate transfer matrix $\mathbf{M}_i(\kappa)$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}_i(\kappa) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - \frac{\lambda_0}{2\kappa} & -\frac{\lambda_0}{2\kappa} e^{2\kappa z_i} \\ \frac{\lambda_0}{2\kappa} e^{-2\kappa z_i} & 1 + \frac{\lambda_0}{2\kappa} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{M}_{\rm global}(\kappa) = \prod_{i \in \mathcal{C}_N} \mathbf{M}_i(\kappa).
\end{equation}
For any finite coupling $\lambda_0 < \infty$, high-frequency modes ($\kappa \gg \lambda_0$) tunnel across barriers with transmission probability $|t(\kappa)|^2 = 4\kappa^2 / (4\kappa^{2} + \lambda_0^2) > 0$, establishing global thermalization across all sub-cavities at temperature $T$ before taking the strong-coupling limit\cite{gordon2026prd}. Partitioning the $N$-th generation Cantor stack (scale ratio $b=3$) into its left and right $(N-1)$-th generation sub-stacks yields the functional interaction energy recursion $\Delta e_{\mathcal{C}_N}(d) = 2\Delta e_{\mathcal{C}_{N-1}}(d/b) + \Delta e_{\rm cross}(d; \ell_*)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:recursion_main}. In $(3+1)$-dimensional spacetime, the interaction energy per unit area carries scaling dimension $[\text{length}]^{-3}$, so that under spatial dilation $d \to d/b$ the homogeneous scale-invariant part satisfies $\Delta e_{\rm hom}(d/b) = b^3 \Delta e_{\rm hom}(d)$, while discrete self-similarity across generations $N \to \infty$ imposes periodicity in $\ln(d/\ell_*)$ with period $\ln b$. Expanding the general solution in complex scaling frequencies $\omega_k = 2\pi k / \ln b$ ($k \in \mathbb{Z}$) yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier_running}:
\begin{equation}
e(d) = \frac{\hbar c}{d^3} \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k \, e^{i \frac{2\pi k}{\ln b} \ln(d/\ell_*)} = \frac{\hbar c}{d^3} C\!\left(d_s, \ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right).
\end{equation}
At finite temperature $T$, compactifying imaginary time with period $\beta = \hbar/(k_B T)$ replaces the continuous $\zeta$-integral with the Matsubara sum over $\zeta_n = 2\pi n k_B T/\hbar$, generalizing $C$ to the bivariate function $C_T(d_s, \ln(d/\ell_*), d/\lambda_T)$ with thermal wavelength $\lambda_T = \hbar c / (2\pi k_B T)$\cite{gordon2026prd}.

\subsection*{3D boundary-element method and $C_4$ cyclic Bloch reduction}
In three dimensions, the transfer-matrix log-determinant generalizes to the boundary-integral scattering formula along the imaginary wavenumber axis $\kappa = \xi/c$ in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$):
\begin{equation}
E_C = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi} \int_0^\infty d\kappa \, \ln\det\bigl(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}(\kappa)\bigr),
\qquad
F_z = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi} \int_0^\infty d\kappa \, \mathrm{Tr}\!\left[\bigl(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}(\kappa)\bigr)^{-1} \frac{\partial \mathbf{M}(\kappa)}{\partial z_{\rm rotor}}\right],
\label{eq:methods_bem}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{M}(\kappa) = \mathbf{D}_{\rm diag}^{-1}(\kappa)\mathbf{V}_{\rm off}(\kappa)$ couples discretized surface panels across the rotor and stator, weighted by the dispersive Drude dielectric permittivity of gold ($\omega_p = 9.03\text{ eV}$, $\gamma = 0.053\text{ eV}$). In the 3D Scalar-Yukawa formulation, panel interactions are governed by the scalar Green's function $G_0(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';\kappa) = \exp(-\kappa |\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|)/(4\pi |\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|)$, whereas the full 3D Vector-Dyadic formulation couples tangential surface currents via the dyadic electromagnetic Green's tensor $\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';\kappa) = (\mathbf{I} - \kappa^{-2}\nabla\nabla')G_0(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';\kappa)$. Coincident and near-field panel integrals are regularized at the sub-panel quadrature length scale $\sigma_{\rm panel} = \sqrt{(A_t+A_s)/(32\pi)}$, where $A_t$ and $A_s$ denote target and source panel areas.

Exploiting the four-fold cyclic symmetry ($C_4$) of the concentric ring sectors block-diagonalizes the dense $N_{\rm total} \times N_{\rm total}$ scattering matrix into four decoupled Bloch irreducible representations ($\ell_{\rm B} \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$) via the cyclic projection $\mathbf{M}^{(\ell_{\rm B})}(\kappa) = \sum_{q=0}^{3} e^{i\pi \ell_{\rm B} q/2}\mathbf{M}_{0,q}(\kappa)$, where $\mathbf{M}_{0,q}$ couples the fundamental sector to the $q$-th rotated sector and each decoupled block is factorized via dense pivoted LU decomposition (\texttt{scipy.linalg.lu\_factor}). This exact symmetry reduction decreases memory footprint by $16\times$ and accelerates LU factorization by $64\times$. We discretize the surfaces across three hierarchical mesh tiers: \texttt{Standard} ($656$ panels for $N=0,1$; $\RotCfourTightslotNtwoStandardThetaZerozerosixpzeroPanelCount{}$ panels for $N=2$ and 3-track controls; $\RotConezeroFractalNthreeStandardThetaZerozerothreepzeroPanelCount{}$ panels for $N=3$ and 7-track controls), \texttt{High} ($\RotCfourTightslotNtwoHighThetaZerozerosixpzeroPanelCount{}$ panels for $N=2$; $\RotConezeroFractalNthreeHighThetaZerozerothreepzeroPanelCount{}$ panels for $N=3$), and \texttt{Ultra-High} ($\RotCfourTightslotNtwoUltraHighThetaZerozerosixpzeroPanelCount{}$ panels for $N=2$). At $T = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoCdToleranceTemperatureK{}\text{ K}$, the continuous $\kappa$-integral is replaced by exact Matsubara summation over $\kappa_n = 2\pi n k_B T / (\hbar c)$ with prime weight $1/2$ at the static $n=0$ transverse-magnetic mode (the static $n=0$ transverse-electric reflection vanishes by the Bohr--van Leeuwen theorem).

\subsection*{4-pass inclusion--exclusion 3D FDTD methodology}
To provide an independent time-domain electromagnetic benchmark in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$), we integrate the Wick-rotated Maxwell stress tensor $\mathbf{T}$ over closed per-tooth vacuum bounding boxes $S_i$ surrounding each rotor tooth inside its radial stator slot\cite{oskooi2010,rodriguez2011}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
F_z &= \frac{\hbar}{\pi} \int_0^\infty d\xi \, \sum_{i} \oint_{S_i} d\mathbf{A} \cdot \langle \mathbf{T}(i\xi, \mathbf{x}) \rangle_z, \\
T_{zz} &= \frac{1}{2} \left[ \varepsilon_0 (E_z^2 - E_x^2 - E_y^2) + \mu_0 (H_z^2 - H_x^2 - H_y^2) \right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:methods_fdtd}
\end{equation}
To eliminate both the rotor's self-force and the uncoupled background scattering of the stator slot walls across the bounding-box faces, each discrete-cosine-transform (DCT) boundary moment is computed using a 4-pass inclusion--exclusion subtraction at identical Yee-grid alignment with subpixel permittivity averaging disabled (\texttt{eps\_averaging = False}) and Courant factor $0.3$:
\begin{equation}
F_z^{\rm net} = \bigl(F_z^{\rm both} - F_z^{\rm rotor}\bigr) - \bigl(F_z^{\rm stator} - F_z^{\rm vac}\bigr).
\label{eq:methods_four_pass}
\end{equation}
With $n_{\rm max}=1$ DCT moments across the $6$ bounding-box faces of each rotor tooth ($\FdtdMomentsPerToothPerPass{}$ moments per tooth per pass across $\FdtdInclusionExclusionPasses{}$ passes), the $N=0$ single-track geometry ($4$ teeth across the $4$ sectors) evaluates $\NaturemeeptaskzerozerooneMomentsPerConfig{}$ moments per pass ($\NaturemeeptaskzerozerooneTotalMoments{}$ total 4-pass moments), the $N=2$ and 3-track control geometries ($12$ teeth) evaluate $\NaturemeeptaskzerozerofourMomentsPerConfig{}$ moments per pass ($\NaturemeeptaskzerozerofourTotalMoments{}$ total 4-pass moments), and the $N=3$ 7-track Cantor geometry ($28$ teeth) evaluates $\RotConezeroFractalNthreeMomentsPerConfig{}$ moments per pass ($\RotConezeroFractalNthreeTotalMoments{}$ total 4-pass moments). All 4-pass FDTD MEEP simulations were executed across sharded MPI worker groups on the BigRed 200 supercomputer using the 6-pole Raki\'c Lorentz--Drude susceptibility model for gold in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) and converted to SI piconewtons via $\hbar c / a^2 = 0.0316152677\text{ pN}\cdot\mu\text{m}^2$ at $a = 1\ \mu\text{m}$.

\subsection*{Wavenumber sub-band decomposition and stepped $(r,z)$ log-periodic stroke protocol}
To connect the discrete scaling spectrum $\omega_k = 2\pi k / \ln b$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier_running} to the 3D boundary-integral scattering formalism of Eq.~\eqref{eq:methods_bem}, we decompose both the radial-wall coupling operator and the exact signed axial force integrand across prefractal generations $m \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$). To evaluate exact geometric clearance scaling without minimum-feature lithographic clamping, we analyze an exact-scaling coplanar $N=3$ Cantor ring ($b=3$, spanning $R \in [0.20, 1.55]\ \mu\text{m}$ with unconstrained minimum tooth width $w_{\rm min} = 5\text{ nm}$ so that radial slot-to-tooth clearances scale strictly as $g_{r,m} = g_{r,1} b^{-(m-1)} \in \{135, 45, 15\}\text{ nm}$ across $\Delta\theta_{\rm tooth} = 8^\circ$ teeth centred inside $\Delta\theta_{\rm slot} = 24^\circ$ stator apertures at $\theta = 12^\circ$). Let $\mathcal{P}_{\rm rot}^{(m)}$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\rm stat}^{(m)}$ denote the sets of radial-sidewall surface panels (\texttt{rotor\_radial\_wall} and \texttt{stator\_radial\_wall}) belonging to generation $m$. Isolating the generation-$m$ radial-sidewall block $\mathbf{G}_{{\rm rad},m}^{(0)}(\kappa)$ of the $C_4$-projected rotor--stator single-layer dyadic Green's tensor across $\kappa \in [1.5, 150]\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$ ($21$ logarithmically spaced points) defines the strictly non-negative generation-$m$ spectral coupling density $w_m(\kappa)$ alongside the exact signed generation-$m$ trace integrand $f_{z,m}(\kappa) = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi}\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{P}^{(m)}}\!\bigl[(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{M}(\kappa))^{-1}\partial_{z_{\rm rotor}}\mathbf{M}(\kappa)\bigr]$:
\begin{equation}
w_m(\kappa) = \frac{1}{2}\kappa^6 \left( |r_{\rm TM}(\kappa)|^2 + |r_{\rm TE}(\kappa)|^2 \right) \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_{\rm rot}^{(m)}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}_{\rm stat}^{(m)}} \left| \bigl[\mathbf{G}_{\rm RS}^{(0)}(\kappa)\bigr]_{ij} \right|^2,
\label{eq:methods_spectral_subband}
\end{equation}
where $r_{\rm TM}(\kappa)$ and $r_{\rm TE}(\kappa)$ are the dispersive Drude gold Fresnel reflection weights evaluated at characteristic wavenumber $k_{\rm eff} = 1/\max(g_{\rm min}, 20\text{ nm})$. Because each generation-$m$ radial cavity filters evanescent modes via the Yukawa kernel $\exp(-\kappa |\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|) \sim \exp(-\kappa g_{r,m})$, the spectral density $w_m(\kappa)$ peaks at $\kappa_{{\rm peak},m} \propto 1/g_{r,m} = b^{m-1}/g_{r,1}$, yielding logarithmic peak spacing $\Delta\ln\kappa_{\rm peak} \approx \ln b$ across generations (Fig.~\ref{fig:clutch_log_periodicity}a). Panels in $\mathcal{P}_{\rm rot}^{(m)}$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\rm stat}^{(m)}$ are classified by matching the exact cylindrical vertex radii ($\min/\max \sqrt{x_v^2 + y_v^2}$ across all four vertices within $10^{-5}\ \mu\text{m}$ tolerance) rather than panel centroids, eliminating chord-sag radial shifts $r\cos(\Delta\phi/2)$ on finite-angle annular bridge panels ($R_{\max} = 1.55\ \mu\text{m}$); unassigned non-tooth panels are indexed to level $0$ and excluded from generation-specific sums.

To transform these wavenumber sub-bands into real-space log-periodic force oscillations along the vertical actuator coordinate $z_{\rm rotor}$, we construct the three-generation $(r,z)$-self-similar stepped Cantor clutch (\texttt{Cantor\_N3\_SteppedRZ}, scale factor $b = \LogperSteppedRzBScale{}$, radial centres $R_c \in \{0.45, 0.85, 1.25\}\ \mu\text{m}$ ordered in Cantor flanking sequence $m \in \{3, 1, 2\}$). Both the radial cross-sectional dimensions and the vertical stator slot centres $z_{{\rm stage},m}$ are scaled geometrically by $b^{-(m-1)}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_m &= \ell_1 b^{-(m-1)}, \qquad
W_{{\rm slot},m} = 2.4\,\ell_m, \qquad
w_{{\rm tooth},m} = 1.1\,\ell_m, \\
H_{{\rm ring},m} &= 0.55\,\ell_m, \qquad
z_{{\rm stage},m} = z_{{\rm stage},1} b^{-(m-1)},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:methods_stepped_rz}
\end{equation}
with $\ell_1 = 160\text{ nm}$ ($\ell_m \in \{160.0, 64.0, 25.6\}\text{ nm}$) and $z_{{\rm stage},1} = -250\text{ nm}$ ($z_{{\rm stage},m} \in \{-250, -100, -40\}\text{ nm}$). We benchmark this geometrically stepped hierarchy against both a non-geometric three-stage control with linearly spaced stator elevations ($z_{{\rm stage},m} \in \{-250, -145, -40\}\text{ nm}$) and a single-scale uniform control that assigns the middle-generation scale ($\ell_2 = 64.0\text{ nm}$, $z_{{\rm stage},2} = -100\text{ nm}$) identically to all three concentric tracks. Sweeping the vertical rotor displacement across $36$ logarithmically spaced depths $|z_{\rm rotor}| \in [25, 450]\text{ nm}$ ($\ln(|z_{\rm rotor}|/\ell_3) \in [-0.47, 2.42]$, $\theta = 3^\circ$, $\Delta\theta_{\rm tooth} = 15^\circ$, $\Delta\theta_{\rm slot} = 30^\circ$, $n_\xi = 12$ Gauss--Legendre quadrature nodes in 3D Vector-Dyadic BEM), we compensate the baseline inverse-square edge-fringing decay by forming $Y(x) = |z_{\rm rotor}|^2 F_z(z_{\rm rotor})$ as a function of logarithmic stroke $x = \ln(|z_{\rm rotor}| / |z_{{\rm stage},3}|)$. Subtracting a smooth quadratic baseline sequentially over a non-integer number of log-periods can absorb power at the fundamental log-periodic frequency; we therefore fit $Y(x)$ simultaneously via a $7$-column ordinary least-squares design matrix combining the smooth quadratic baseline $Y_{\rm smooth}(x)$ and the two-harmonic log-periodic expansion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier_running} at the fixed theoretical scaling frequency $\omega_1 = 2\pi / \ln b = \roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzOmegaone}$:
\begin{equation}
Y_{\rm fit}(x) = \underbrace{c_0 + c_1 x + c_2 x^2}_{Y_{\rm smooth}(x)} + \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{2} \left[ a_k \cos(k \omega_1 x) + b_k \sin(k \omega_1 x) \right]}_{\delta Y_{\rm harm}(x)}, \qquad A_k = \sqrt{a_k^2 + b_k^2},
\label{eq:methods_logper_fit}
\end{equation}
from which the detrended modulation is evaluated as $\delta Y(x) = Y(x) - Y_{\rm smooth}(x)$ with harmonic coefficient of determination $R^2 = 1 - \sum_i [\delta Y(x_i) - \delta Y_{\rm harm}(x_i)]^2 / \sum_i [\delta Y(x_i) - \overline{\delta Y}]^2$ ($R^2 = \roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzRtwo}$, $F = 7.92$, $p = 1.9 \times 10^{-4}$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} = -10.8$, $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = -12.1$ vs.\ $R^2_{\rm ctrl} = \roundtwo{\LogperSteppedRzRtwoUniform}$).

\subsection*{Electrostatic patch budget, flexure scaling and proposed SOI fabrication}
To quantify electrostatic patch-potential tolerances at $T = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoCdToleranceTemperatureK{}\text{ K}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:nature_feasibility_stability}a--d and Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:nems_feasibility}), we extract the rotor--stator mutual-capacitance gradient $|\partial C_{\rm el}/\partial z|$ from the $\kappa \to 0$ Laplace limit of the 3D BEM solver, obtaining $|\partial C_{\rm el}/\partial z| = \roundfour{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoPatchPotentialAbsDcDzPnPerMvtwo}\text{ pN}\,\text{mV}^{-2}$ ($\scinum{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoPatchPotentialDcDzFPerM}\text{ F}\,\text{m}^{-1}$) for \texttt{Rot\_C8\_Compact\_N2} and $\roundfour{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzPatchPotentialAbsDcDzPnPerMvtwo}\text{ pN}\,\text{mV}^{-2}$ ($\scinum{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzPatchPotentialDcDzFPerM}\text{ F}\,\text{m}^{-1}$) for \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}. Balancing parasitic electrostatic attraction $F_{z,\rm el} = -\frac{1}{2}|\partial C_{\rm el}/\partial z|V_{\rm rms}^2$ against room-temperature gold Casimir repulsion $F_{z,300\rm K}^{\rm rep}$ defines the critical uncompensated RMS voltage $V_{\rm crit} = \sqrt{2 F_{z,300\rm K}^{\rm rep}/|\partial C_{\rm el}/\partial z|}$ ($\roundone{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoPatchPotentialVCritMv}\text{ mV}$ and $\roundone{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzPatchPotentialVCritMv}\text{ mV}$, respectively).

Because an $N_s = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilitySpokeCount{}$-spoke single-crystal $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ clamped-guided suspension ($E_Y = \scinum{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityEYPa}\text{ Pa}$) obeys the anisotropic scaling laws $k_{z,\rm mech} = N_s E_Y w_{\rm spoke} t_{\rm spoke}^3 / L_{\rm spoke}^3$ and $k_{r,\rm mech} / k_{z,\rm mech} = (w_{\rm spoke} / t_{\rm spoke})^2$, the mechanical suspension can be tailored across three operational regimes bounded by the engaged-state ($\theta=48^\circ$, $d_0=15\text{ nm}$, power-law exponent $\eta=3.5$) nonlinear vertical pull-in stiffness $k_{z,\rm crit}^{\rm pull\text{-}in} = \frac{(\eta+1)^{\eta+1}}{\eta^\eta}\frac{|F_{z,0}^{\rm att}|}{d_0} = \roundtwo{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityKZCritPullInPnPerNm}\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$ for \texttt{Rot\_C8\_Compact\_N2} ($\roundtwo{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzLateralStabilityKZCritPullInPnPerNm}\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$ for \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}; Fig.~\ref{fig:nature_feasibility_stability}c,d and Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:nems_feasibility}): (i) an isometric gap-locked frequency-transducer regime ($k_{z,\rm mech} = \roundone{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityKZMechPnPerNm}\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1} \gg k_{z,\rm crit}^{\rm pull\text{-}in}$, $k_{r,\rm mech} = \roundone{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityKRMechPnPerNm}\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$), which overcomes the radial Casimir stiffness magnitude ($\roundthree{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityOriginEvenEnergyEstimatePnPerNm}\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$ for \texttt{Rot\_C8\_Compact\_N2}; $\roundthree{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzLateralStabilityKRCasimirPnPerNm}\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$ for \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}) with lateral safety factors of $\roundzero{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityLateralStabilitySafetyFactor}\times$ and $\roundzero{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzLateralStabilityLateralStabilitySafetyFactor}\times$ and vertical pull-in safety factors of $\roundone{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityVerticalPullInSafetyFactor}\times$ and $\roundone{\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzLateralStabilityVerticalPullInSafetyFactor}\times$; (ii) an intermediate compliant nanometre-stroke regime ($k_{z,\rm mech} = \roundthree{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityNmActuatorKZMechPnPerNm}\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$, $k_{r,\rm mech} = \roundtwo{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityNmActuatorKRMechPnPerNm}\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$); and (iii) a high-stroke contact-release clutch regime ($k_{z,\rm mech} < k_{z,\rm crit}^{\rm pull\text{-}in}$) using either $10\times$ longer spokes ($L_{\rm spoke} = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilitySoftClutchLSpokeUm{}\ \mu\text{m}$) or a compact $50\text{ nm}$ SOI flexure ($L_{\rm spoke} = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityCompactClutchLSpokeUm{}\ \mu\text{m}$, $t_{\rm spoke} = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityCompactClutchTSpokeUm{}\ \mu\text{m}$) with self-consistent basin equilibrium lift $\Delta z_{\rm eq} = F_{z,0}^{\rm rep}/(k_{z,\rm mech} + F_{z,0}^{\rm rep}/\Delta z_{\rm basin})$.

A proposed fabrication sequence compatible with standard SOI and damascene gold processing comprises four stages: (1) Starting from an SOI wafer with a single-crystal $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ device layer ($E_Y = \scinum{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityEYPa}\text{ Pa}$), the recessed isolation trench ($z \in [-650, -550]\text{ nm}$) and azimuthal landing pads ($z_{\rm pad,top} = -115\text{ nm}$, thickness $150\text{ nm}$) are patterned via $100\text{ keV}$ electron-beam lithography (EBL) in hydrogen silsesquioxane (HSQ) or PMMA resist, followed by Ti/Au seed deposition and damascene gold electroplating. (2) A sacrificial $\text{SiO}_2$ spacer is deposited by atomic-layer deposition (ALD) and planarized by chemical-mechanical polishing (CMP) to define the $15\text{ nm}$ vertical standoff between the landing pads ($z = -115\text{ nm}$) and rotor tooth tips ($z = -100\text{ nm}$), after which the $25\text{ nm}$-thick slotted Cantor stator aperture rings ($z \in [-25, 0]\text{ nm}$) are patterned via overlay EBL aligned to sub-$5\text{ nm}$ interferometric fiducial marks. (3) Dual-exposure or grayscale EBL defines the stepped concentric rotor teeth ($\CantorNtwoHybridrzHGenoneNm{}\text{ nm}$ generation-1 and $\CantorNtwoHybridrzHGentwoNm{}\text{ nm}$ generation-2 teeth in \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}), anchored to the four radial spider spokes ($z \in [350, 470]\text{ nm}$) and the $\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilitySpokeCount{}$-spoke single-crystal $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ flexure suspension. (4) The sacrificial oxide is removed using anhydrous vapor-phase hydrofluoric acid (vapor-HF) to prevent capillary stiction, followed by ultra-high-vacuum annealing and Kelvin-probe nulling of residual patch potentials below $V_{\rm crit}$.

\subsection*{Rotational energy barrier, Casimir torque and 300 K thermomechanical lock-in SNR}
To evaluate the angular energy barrier and axial-switching Casimir torque across $\theta \in [0^\circ, 48^\circ]$ at $T = 300\text{ K}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:nature_feasibility_stability}e), we compute the 3D Vector-Dyadic BEM Matsubara interaction energy $E_C(\theta)$ across a $10$-point angular grid $\theta \in \{0^\circ, 3^\circ, 6^\circ, 12^\circ, 18^\circ, 24^\circ, 30^\circ, 36^\circ, 42^\circ, 48^\circ\}$ for \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}, \texttt{Rot\_C8\_Compact\_N2}, and \texttt{Fractal\_N3\_Cantor7Track}. Referencing the interaction energy to the open-slot centre $\Delta E_C(\theta) = E_C(\theta) - E_C(0^\circ)$, the axial-switching Casimir torque is obtained via second-order finite differences with respect to radian rotation angle, $\tau_\theta(\theta) = -\partial E_C(\theta)/\partial\theta$, with state-to-state switching work $W_{\rm switch}(48^\circ \to 3^\circ) = |E_C(3^\circ) - E_C(48^\circ)|$. As the rotor teeth cross the stator slot sidewalls around $\theta = 18^\circ\text{--}21^\circ$, $\Delta E_C(\theta)$ reaches an attractive minimum of $-0.345\text{ eV}$ (\texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}), $-0.290\text{ eV}$ (\texttt{Rot\_C8\_Compact\_N2}), and $-0.758\text{ eV}$ (\texttt{Fractal\_N3\_Cantor7Track}), producing peak torque magnitudes $|\tau_\theta|_{\max} = 285\text{--}332\text{ pN}\cdot\text{nm}$ at $\theta = 12^\circ$ and a net switching work $W_{\rm switch} = 0.23\text{ eV}$ ($0.036\text{ aJ} \approx 8.7\,k_B T_{300}$) for \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}.

For room-temperature ($T = 300\text{ K}$) thermomechanical noise and lock-in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) analysis (Fig.~\ref{fig:nature_feasibility_stability}f), the effective axial modal mass $m_{\rm eff} = m_{\rm rotor,Au} + \frac{3}{8}m_{\rm spokes,Si}$ combines the 3D gold rotor mass ($\rho_{\rm Au} = 19{,}300\text{ kg}\,\text{m}^{-3}$, $m_{\rm rotor,Au} = 4.65\text{ pg}$ for \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ} and $4.90\text{ pg}$ for \texttt{Rot\_C8\_Compact\_N2}) with the Rayleigh clamped-guided modal mass factor ($\frac{3}{8}$) of the four $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ suspension spokes ($\rho_{\rm Si} = 2{,}329\text{ kg}\,\text{m}^{-3}$). For each suspension regime with axial stiffness $k_{z,\rm mech}$, the fundamental axial resonance frequency is $f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{k_{z,\rm mech}/m_{\rm eff}}$, the RMS thermal displacement is $\delta z_{\rm th} = \sqrt{k_B T / k_{z,\rm mech}}$, the Langevin thermomechanical force noise spectral density is $S_F^{1/2} = \sqrt{4 k_B T k_{z,\rm mech}/(\omega_0 Q)}$, and the lock-in signal-to-noise ratio over integration time $\tau$ is $\mathrm{SNR}(\tau) = F_{z,300\rm K}^{\rm rep}\sqrt{\tau}/S_F^{1/2}$. In Regime~I ($k_{z,\rm mech} = \roundone{\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityKZMechPnPerNm}\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$), $m_{\rm eff} = 7.77\text{ pg}$, $f_0 = 2{,}001\text{ kHz}$, and $\delta z_{\rm th} = 0.18\text{ nm}$ ($3.16\text{ nm}$ at $f_0 = 924\text{ kHz}$ in Regime~II; $5.50\text{ nm}$ at $f_0 = 614\text{ kHz}$ in Regime~III), yielding $S_F^{1/2}(Q=10^4) = 1.28\text{ fN}\,\text{Hz}^{-1/2}$ and $\mathrm{SNR}(\tau = 1\text{ s}) = 1{,}155\text{--}1{,}307$ in Regime~I ($4{,}791\text{--}6{,}803$ in Regimes~II--III).

\subsection*{Cavity QED atomic transducers and optomechanical readout formulations}
For a ground-state hydrogen atom with static polarizability $\alpha(0)/(4\pi\varepsilon_0) = \frac{9}{2}a_0^3$ located at distance $z$ from a prefractal boundary in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$), the retarded Casimir--Polder potential is modulated by the log-periodic coefficient of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier_running}:
\begin{equation}
U_{\rm CP}(z) = -\frac{3\hbar c \, \alpha(0)}{32\pi^2 \varepsilon_0 z^4} \left[ 1 + C\!\left(d_s, \ln\frac{z}{\ell_*}\right) \right].
\end{equation}
The spontaneous emission rate of the $2p \to 1s$ transition ($\omega_{12}$) relative to free space $\Gamma_0$ follows the fractal local density of states scaling $\Gamma_{2p\to 1s}/\Gamma_0 \approx (L_s \omega_{12}/c)^{d_s - 3} G(\ln(\omega_{12}L_s/c))$ across characteristic cavity scale $L_s$, where $G$ is a log-periodic modulation of period $\ln b$. Similarly, the geometric modification to the non-relativistic Bethe Lamb shift is obtained by integrating the boundary-modified virtual mode density $\Delta\rho_{\rm frac}(k, d_s, \ell_*)$:
\begin{equation}
\delta E_{\rm Lamb} = \frac{2\alpha_{\rm EM}}{3\pi m_e^2 c^2} \sum_n |\langle 2s | \mathbf{p} | n \rangle|^2 (E_n - E_{2s}) \int_0^\infty \frac{dk}{k} \, \frac{\Delta\rho_{\rm frac}(k, d_s, \ell_*)}{E_n - E_{2s} + \hbar c k}.
\end{equation}
At $z = 50\text{ nm}$ inside a $d = 100\text{ nm}$ prefractal gap, the baseline flat-plate Casimir--Polder potential $|U_{\rm CP}^{\rm flat}| \approx \scinum{\AtomicCqedCpBaseEv}\text{ eV}$ is shifted by $\Delta U_{\rm CP} \approx 2.51 \times 10^{-10}\text{ eV}$ ($\sim \AtomicCqedCpShiftKhz{}\text{ kHz}$), while the $2p\to 1s$ ($\lambda = 121.6\text{ nm}$) spontaneous emission rate varies by $\AtomicCqedPurcellLifetimeVariationMin{}\%\text{--}\AtomicCqedPurcellLifetimeVariationMax{}\%$ and the Bethe Lamb shift reaches $\delta E_{\rm Lamb} \approx \scinum{\AtomicCqedLambShiftDeltaE}\text{ eV}$ ($\delta\nu_{\rm Lamb} \approx \AtomicCqedLambShiftDeltaNu{}\text{ kHz}$). When the prefractal stator is coupled to a superconducting drum resonator of effective mass $m_{\rm eff}$ and bare resonance frequency $\omega_{m,0}$ in a microwave optomechanical cavity\cite{casimir_drum2025}, the scale-dependent Casimir pressure $P_\perp(d)$ induces a softening frequency shift $\Delta\omega_m(d) \approx -\frac{A_\perp}{2 m_{\rm eff} \omega_{m,0}} \frac{\partial P_\perp(d)}{\partial d}$, enabling direct reconstruction of $\partial_{\ln d}C$ and $\partial_{\ln d}^2 C$ from the mechanical frequency-versus-gap curve.
