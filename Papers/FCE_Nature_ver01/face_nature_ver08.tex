\noindent\textbf{Quantum electromagnetic fluctuations in vacuum ordinarily pull neutral surfaces together, governed by mirror-symmetry constraints and parallel-plate overlap\cite{casimir1948,kennethklich2006}. Repulsive Casimir forces have been measured in liquids\cite{munday2009}, and interdigitated metallic ridges in vacuum are predicted to experience weak upward fringing forces when facing surface overlap vanishes\cite{levin2010,rodriguez2011}. However, scaling vacuum repulsion across micrometre-scale cavities is hindered because wide ridges lose sidewall proximity whereas dense periodic gratings screen adjacent slots. Here we report three-dimensional boundary-element calculations, complemented by qualitative time-domain sign checks, predicting that a multiscale concentric ring architecture amplifies and mechanically switches axial Casimir forces in vacuum. Combining Cantor-inspired radial slot subdivision with azimuthal segmentation and axial stepping suppresses parallel-plate attraction in the open-slot state, yielding $+15.5$ to $+19.8$ piconewtons of upward repulsion across refined mesh tiers with five-nanometre corner rounding. This multiscale geometry increases repulsion $1.25\text{--}1.55$-fold in radial hierarchies over uniform gratings and exceeds equal-sub-slot controls under a fixed primary-slot budget by $21\text{--}73$ per cent, while matching unconstrained non-fractal arrays engineered to share identical fifteen-nanometre clearances and sidewall area. Rotating the teeth over recessed landing pads switches the force to parallel-plate attraction at zero electrical bias, defining a theoretical blueprint for nanomechanical vacuum-force control.}

% Opening paragraphs (Nature Articles omit an explicit "Introduction" heading)
The Casimir effect---the force arising from boundary-modified quantum electromagnetic fluctuations\cite{casimir1948,bordag2009}---links quantum electrodynamics to nanomechanical engineering below $100\text{ nm}$. In vacuum (background permittivity $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bg}}=1.0$), both the sign and magnitude of this interaction are tightly constrained: mirror-symmetric conductors or non-magnetic dielectrics mutually attract\cite{kennethklich2006}, and in the proximity-force approximation (PFA) facing parallel surfaces attract in proportion to their projected overlap area ($F_{z,\mathrm{face}}^{\mathrm{PFA}} \propto -A_{\mathrm{overlap}}/d^4$). Although repulsive Casimir--Lifshitz forces have been measured via chemical liquid immersion\cite{munday2009}, and experiments on rotational Casimir torques\cite{somers2018}, self-assembled microcavities\cite{munkhbat2021}, and nonlinear superconducting resonators\cite{dejong2026} have expanded vacuum metrology, theoretically designing and mechanically switching piconewton-scale repulsive Casimir forces in vacuum without liquid damping remains an open challenge\cite{rodriguez2011}.

Two geometric concepts motivate an approach that avoids altering bulk material permittivities. First, in quantum fractals\cite{kempkes2019,xu2021} and self-similar plate stacks\cite{akkermans2010,shajesh2016,shajesh2017}, multiscale boundaries break continuous scale symmetry\cite{milton2004,milton2016,gordon2026prd}. Yet in standard three-dimensional (3D) cavities, Weyl's law and spectral asymptotics\cite{lapidus1991} dictate that bulk volume and facing-surface-area contributions dominate whenever $A_{\mathrm{overlap}} > 0$, masking subleading perimeter and hierarchical boundary terms. Second, Levin et al.\cite{levin2010} predicted that reflection symmetry can be broken geometrically in vacuum without fractals: an elongated metallic ridge centred above a slotted aperture plate experiences transverse saddle-point fringing fields that pull the ridge tip toward the slot mid-plane, producing an upward repulsive restoring force along the vertical coordinate (accompanied by lateral Earnshaw instability requiring mechanical stabilization)\cite{levin2010,rodriguez2011}. However, a single narrow ridge generates only sub-piconewton forces, widening a single tooth collapses the sidewall fringing gradient, and repeating identical periodic slots suffers from mutual evanescent screening.

Here we present a theoretical and numerical investigation combining 3D slot-tooth interdigitation with finite-generation ($N \le 3$) Cantor-inspired multiscale ring hierarchies across radial, azimuthal, and axial coordinates $(r, \theta, z)$ in vacuum (Figs.~1--4, Extended Data Figs.~1--5, and Extended Data Tables~\ref{tab:fractal_geometry_control}--\ref{tab:nems_feasibility}). When concentric gold rotor teeth sit centred inside open stator slots ($\theta = 0^\circ\text{--}6^\circ$), projected vertical face-to-face surface overlap vanishes ($A_{\mathrm{overlap}} = 0$, $F_{z,\mathrm{face}}^{\mathrm{PFA}} = 0$), isolating lateral sidewall fringing and cross-track scattering. Conversely, rotating the rotor in-plane over recessed stator landing pads ($\theta = 18^\circ\text{--}48^\circ$) restores parallel-plate overlap across a narrow vertical gap, switching the predicted axial force at zero electrical bias between perimeter-driven repulsion and parallel-plate attraction.

\section*{Multiscale rotary switch architecture}

As a conceptual motivation, an idealized one-dimensional (1D) scalar transfer-matrix model of a pre-Cantor barrier stack with scale factor $b$ suggests that self-similar cavity partitioning introduces a logarithmically running Casimir coefficient $C(d_s, \ln(d/\ell_*))$ with discrete scaling frequencies $\omega_k = 2\pi k / \ln b$ (Methods, Eqs.~\ref{eq:trace_running}--\ref{eq:fourier_running})\cite{gordon2026prd}. Although this 1D scalar parametrization is not an electromagnetic derivation for a 3D vector ring, it provides a useful design heuristic suggesting that multiscale slot widths and vertical step depths can partition evanescent coupling across distinct spatial scales while reducing mutual screening between adjacent tracks (Fig.~1a).

We implement this zero-face-overlap concept in a modelled four-fold symmetric ($C_4$, $90^\circ$ sector period) concentric gold rotor--stator switch in vacuum (Fig.~1b--d and Methods). Downward-protruding concentric rotor teeth interdigitate radially between $25\text{ nm}$-thick upper stator aperture rings ($z \in [-25, 0]\text{ nm}$) suspended above recessed azimuthal stator landing pads ($z_{\mathrm{pad,top}} = -115\text{ nm}$). In the disengaged open-slot state ($\theta = 0^\circ\text{--}6^\circ$; Fig.~1b,c), the tooth tips extend below the stator aperture plane with $A_{\mathrm{overlap}} = 0$, so transverse saddle-point sidewall fringing pulls the rotor upward ($F_z > 0$). In the engaged landing-pad state ($\theta = 18^\circ\text{--}48^\circ$; Fig.~1d), aligning the bottom faces of the teeth over the recessed landing pads across a $15\text{ nm}$ minimum vertical gap restores $A_{\mathrm{overlap}} > 0$ and switches the axial force to normal attraction ($F_z < 0$). Across our progression from a single-track baseline ($N=0$) to a 3D polar $(r,\theta,z)$ multiscale architecture (F3-111; Fig.~1e, Extended Data Fig.~1, and Extended Data Tables~\ref{tab:fractal_geometry_control} and \ref{tab:unified_3d_fractal_factorial}), we benchmark each hierarchy against explicitly defined non-fractal control families to separate multiscale architectural effects from near-field perimeter and clearance packing.

\section*{Force amplification and error bounds}

We evaluate 3D Casimir forces in vacuum using a $C_4$-symmetric 3D Vector-Dyadic boundary-element method (BEM) with dispersive Drude gold\cite{reid2013pra,rakic1998} (Fig.~2, Extended Data Fig.~2, Extended Data Tables~\ref{tab:fractal_geometry_control}--\ref{tab:quantum_clutch}, and Methods). On parallel circular Drude gold disks ($R = 1.0\ \mu\text{m}$), continuum-extrapolated BEM pressures converge monotonically toward exact 1D Lifshitz theory as $d/R \to 0$, with relative error decreasing from $4.04\%$ at $d=100\text{ nm}$ to $0.28\%$ at $d=15\text{ nm}$ ($-2872.43\text{ Pa}$ versus exact $-2880.44\text{ Pa}$), while central finite-difference differentiation $-\partial E_C/\partial z_{\mathrm{rotor}}$ ($\Delta z = 0.5\text{ nm}$) matches the analytical trace derivative to $99.964\%$ at $\theta=3^\circ$ and $99.994\%$ at $\theta=48^\circ$ (Methods). Independent 4-pass inclusion--exclusion 3D finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) simulations\cite{rodriguez2009pra,oskooi2010} and diagnostic 3D Scalar-Yukawa BEM independently confirm positive open-slot repulsion ($\theta=0^\circ\text{--}6^\circ$) and sign reversal to strong attraction over the landing pads ($\theta=18^\circ\text{--}48^\circ$). However, because $15\text{ nm}$ clearances span only $0.60\text{--}1.20$ Yee voxels at feasible Cartesian FDTD resolutions ($R=40\text{--}80\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$) without subpixel averaging and expanding the boundary discrete-cosine-transform (DCT) basis from $n_{\max}=1$ to $n_{\max}=2$ shifts the open-slot force by $+42.6\%$, FDTD serves strictly as a qualitative sign check rather than a quantitative validation (Methods). Consequently, all quantitative force predictions rely on the 3D Vector-Dyadic BEM solver and require an explicit numerical uncertainty budget.

Specifically, unrounded $90^\circ$ metallic corners exhibit singular edge-current crowding ($\rho \sim r_{\mathrm{edge}}^{-1/3}$) that causes raw sharp-edge BEM forces to increase with panel density. Introducing a physical lithographic corner radius ($r_{\mathrm{edge}}=5\text{ nm}$ or $10\text{ nm}$) regularizes this edge singularity: on the stepped $N=2$ Cantor ring (Cantor-N2-HybridRZ), refining across Standard, High, and Ultra-High meshes yields $+1.46 \to +1.42 \to +1.73\text{ pN}$ versus $+1.07 \to +1.03 \to +1.21\text{ pN}$ on the uniform three-track grating, maintaining a stable $1.37\text{--}1.42$-fold Cantor-over-uniform ratio across all mesh tiers (Extended Data Fig.~2a and Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:quantum_clutch}). For the 3D $N=3$ architecture (F3-111), evaluating three nested physical-resolution tiers that enforce matched local panel dimensions and aspect ratios ($\overline{\mathrm{AR}} \approx 5.1\text{--}6.8$, up to $14{,}464$ panels; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:unified_3d_fractal_factorial} and Methods) yields $+15.5 \to +16.2 \to +19.8\text{ pN}$ at $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=5\text{ nm}$ and $+11.4 \to +12.8 \to +15.4\text{ pN}$ at $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=10\text{ nm}$ (compared with $+18.55\text{ pN}$ on the baseline sharp-edge Standard mesh). Because refinement from Tier~2 to Tier~3 still produces a $20\text{--}22\%$ upward shift without demonstrating a flat asymptotic plateau, and replacing single-pole Drude gold with a 6-pole Lorentz--Drude susceptibility\cite{rakic1998} shifts forces by $9.7\text{--}16.1\%$ (whereas frequency-quadrature and Matsubara truncation errors remain below $0.85\%$; Methods), absolute force magnitudes carry an estimated $20\text{--}25\%$ numerical discretization and dispersion uncertainty at fixed corner radius. Comparative geometry ratios evaluated at matched physical resolution, by contrast, remain consistent across tiers.

At matched Standard surface discretization, single-scale interdigitation fails to scale across wide annular cavities (Fig.~2a--e and Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:fractal_geometry_control}). On the single-track baseline ($N=0$), deep interdigitation ($z_{\mathrm{tip}} = -100\text{ nm}$) generates $+0.71\text{ pN}$ of upward repulsion at $\theta=3^\circ$, switching to $-1.72\text{ pN}$ at $\theta=48^\circ$ or flipping to $-0.36\text{ pN}$ under symmetric overhang ($z_{\mathrm{tip}} = -55\text{ nm}$), consistent with saddle-point sidewall fringing\cite{levin2010}. Widening a single tooth across $R \in [0.30, 1.35]\ \mu\text{m}$ collapses the sidewall gradient ($F_z = -0.02\text{ pN}$), while subdividing that annulus into a three-track uniform periodic grating ($A_{\mathrm{tooth}} = 0.328\ \mu\text{m}^2$, $P_{\mathrm{edge}} = 8.22\ \mu\text{m}$) yields $+1.20\text{ pN}$ ($3.65\text{ pN}\,\mu\text{m}^{-2}$) due to mutual screening. Flanking the central slot with narrower child tracks at identical tooth area and edge perimeter elevates repulsion $1.25$-fold to $+1.49\text{ pN}$ ($4.56\text{ pN}\,\mu\text{m}^{-2}$) in the uniform-depth $N=2$ Cantor ring and $1.39$-fold to $+1.66\text{ pN}$ ($5.06\text{ pN}\,\mu\text{m}^{-2}$) in the stepped $(r,z)$ hybrid ring (or $14.67\text{ pN}\,\mu\text{m}^{-2}$ in Compact-N2). In the seven-track ($N=3$, $R \in [0.30, 1.65]\ \mu\text{m}$) radial family, the 1D radial Cantor ring (F0-000, $+3.18\text{ pN}$) exceeds the seven-track uniform grating ($+2.06\text{ pN}$) $1.55$-fold, with monolithic spider spokes and substrate trenches perturbing forces by less than $3.3\%$ (Fig.~2f). However, ordering the same seven radial track widths monotonically from inner to outer radius (Ctrl-Monotonic-7Track) yields $+4.39\text{ pN}$ ($+38\%$ above F0-000), because the $5.5:1$ cylindrical radius ratio rewards packing the four narrowest $50\text{ nm}$ slots at small radii while the coarse $450\text{ nm}$ central slot contributes almost zero force ($-0.0034\text{ pN}$; Extended Data Fig.~3 and Methods).

\section*{Spectral bands and staged stroke tuning}

Imaginary-wavenumber and vertical-stroke decompositions clarify where the 1D scaling heuristic aids 3D design and where exact fractal scaling breaks down (Fig.~3 and Methods). In an exact-scaling coplanar $N=3$ Cantor ring ($b=3$, radial clearances $g_{r,m} \in \{135, 45, 15\}\text{ nm}$), the three radial clearance generations partition into three distinct imaginary-wavenumber coupling bands peaking at $\hat{\kappa}_1 = 14.98 \pm 0.11\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$, $\hat{\kappa}_2 = 28.26 \pm 0.23\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$, and $\hat{\kappa}_3 = 46.74 \pm 0.46\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$, whereas the uniform periodic grating exhibits a single band (Fig.~3a). Because finite vertical ring thickness ($H = 100\text{ nm}$) and gold plasma skin depth ($\delta_{\mathrm{skin}} \approx 22\text{ nm} > g_{r,3}$) regularize near-field decay, the empirical logarithmic peak spacings $\Delta\ln\kappa_{21} = 0.63 \pm 0.01$ and $\Delta\ln\kappa_{32} = 0.50 \pm 0.01$ are compressed and non-uniform relative to $\ln 3 = 1.099$. Information-criterion model comparison strongly rejects exact $\ln 3$ log-periodic spacing ($\chi^2_{\mathrm{peak}} = 7415.3$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} = -1661.6$ in favour of an unrestricted generation-resolved multiband fit; Methods). Furthermore, $85.7\%$ ($+2.72\text{ pN}$ of $+3.18\text{ pN}$) of F0-000's axial repulsion originates from the narrowest $15\text{ nm}$-clearance band ($\kappa_3$), confirming that coarse radial generations are starved of sidewall proximity.

Staggering three stator stages geometrically in both radius and vertical stage depth ($b=2.5$, $z_{\mathrm{stage},m} \in \{250, 100, 40\}\text{ nm}$) unfolds these generations along the positive vertical stroke coordinate $z_d = -z_{\mathrm{rotor}} > 0$ (Fig.~3b,c). Although vertical staging trades single-gap peak force ($+0.75\text{ pN}$ maximum) for stroke breadth, it sustains continuous open-slot repulsion ($F_z > 0$) across an eight-fold vertical range ($z_d \in [45, 360]\text{ nm}$), including sub-$100\text{ nm}$ depths where a single-stage uniform control flips to attraction ($-0.90\text{ pN}$ at $z_d = 73\text{ nm}$). Detrended $z_d^2 F_z$ modulations follow geometric staging at $\omega_1 = 2\pi/\ln b = 6.86$ ($R^2 = 0.53$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} = -18.6$) far more closely than linearly spaced ($R^2 = 0.18$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} = +1.4$) or single-stage uniform ($R^2 = 0.01$) controls. Nevertheless, an unconstrained free-frequency sweep recovers an empirical scale factor $\hat{b} = 2.65 \pm 0.14$ ($95\%$ confidence interval $[2.52, 2.79]$, excluding nominal $b=2.5$; Fig.~3c, inset). Thus, the 3D results support geometry-dependent multiband force tuning rather than an exact 3D log-periodic scaling law.

\section*{Three-dimensional polar staging}

Guided by the spectral diagnosis that coarse radial slots lack near-field coupling, we generalize the $N=3$ cavity across $(r, \theta, z)$ via a $2^3$ factorial hierarchy $(u_r, u_\theta, u_z) \in \{0,1\}^3$ spanning $R \in [0.30, 1.65]\ \mu\text{m}$ above $z_{\mathrm{pad,top}} = -115\text{ nm}$ (Fig.~4, Extended Data Fig.~4, Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:unified_3d_fractal_factorial}, and Methods). First, recursive middle-third radial subdivision ($u_r=1$) partitions the $450\text{ nm}$ Generation-1 slot and two $150\text{ nm}$ Generation-2 slots into $50\text{ nm}$ sub-slots separated by five solid internal Cantor stator sub-ribs ($[50, 150, 50]\text{ nm}$ and $50\text{ nm}$), housing twelve $20\text{ nm}$ rotor sub-teeth per sector at $g_r = 15\text{ nm}$ radial clearance. Second, generation-aligned azimuthal splitting ($u_\theta=1$) divides each sub-tooth into two lobes ($24$ lobes per sector), introducing radial-azimuthal fringing corners. Third, linear axial stepping ($u_z=1$) staggers tooth heights ($H_m \in \{90, 80, 70\}\text{ nm}$) across primary generations while maintaining $\ge 15\text{ nm}$ clearances (verified across all $19/19$ primary designs by automated 3D mesh audits; Methods).

Combining all three upgrades in F3-111 $(1,1,1)$ ($A_{\mathrm{tooth}} = 0.3049\ \mu\text{m}^2$) elevates open-slot repulsion on the Standard sharp-edge mesh from $+3.18\text{ pN}$ in F0-000 to $+9.09\text{ pN}$ in radial-only F1r-100, $+13.54\text{ pN}$ in single-mask planar F2rt-110, and $+18.55\text{ pN}$ ($60.83\text{ pN}\,\mu\text{m}^{-2}$), switching to $-19.98\text{ pN}$ over the landing pads at $\theta=48^\circ$ (Fig.~4a,b). Across nested physical-resolution Tiers~1--3, F3-111 maintains a $4.8\text{--}4.9$-fold gain over F0-000 and a $7.8\text{--}8.4$-fold gain over flat uniform C1-Uni000 at $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=5\text{ nm}$ ($4.4\text{--}4.6$-fold and $7.3\text{--}8.1$-fold at $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=10\text{ nm}$), with an $+8.4\text{--}15.1\%$ super-additive excess above the sum of single-axis upgrades (Methods). Spectrally, subdividing the coarse slots into $15\text{ nm}$-clearance sub-cavities brings all three generation integrands $\kappa f_{z,m}(\kappa)$ into a shared near-field wavenumber window ($\hat{\kappa}_1 = 9.51 \pm 0.18$, $\hat{\kappa}_2 = 9.88 \pm 0.19$, and $\hat{\kappa}_3 = 10.27 \pm 0.20\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$, rejecting $\ln 3$ spacing with $\chi^2_{\mathrm{peak}} = 6098.2$; Fig.~4d), reactivating the central $450\text{ nm}$ slot from $-0.0034\text{ pN}$ to $+5.4\text{ pN}$.

Comparing F3-111 against two distinct non-fractal control regimes establishes the exact scope of this performance gain (Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:unified_3d_fractal_factorial} and Methods):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Fixed primary-slot budget with standard $15\text{ nm}$ equal-sub-slot ribs (Controls C6--C10 and M4)}: When non-fractal 12-tooth / 24-lobe controls space $12$ equal slots uniformly or linearly (C6-Uni12, C7-Lin12) or subdivide the monotonic, shuffled, or Cantor 7-slot layouts into equal-width sub-slots separated by standard $15\text{ nm}$ ribs (C8-Mono12, C9-Shuf12, C10-OutPhase12; harmonic-mean clearance $g_{r,\mathrm{harm}} = 20.0\text{--}24.5\text{ nm}$), they yield $+13.97$ to $+15.17\text{ pN}$ on the Standard sharp-edge mesh ($+13.66$ to $+15.35\text{ pN}$ at exact $0.3049\ \mu\text{m}^2$ iso-area). F3-111 exceeds all five primary controls by $+22.3\%$ to $+32.8\%$ nominal ($+20.8\%$ to $+35.8\%$ iso-area), leads the strongest primary control C9-Shuf12 across nested rounded-corner Tiers~1--3 by $+31.6\% \to +51.0\% \to +63.9\%$ at $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=5\text{ nm}$ ($+15.5 \to +16.2 \to +19.8\text{ pN}$ versus $+11.7 \to +10.7 \to +12.1\text{ pN}$) and $+31.3\% \to +62.8\% \to +72.7\%$ at $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=10\text{ nm}$, and surpasses maximum-height equal-sub-slot optimization (M4-OptMaxHZ12-IsoF3: $+12.70\text{ pN}$ sharp; $+10.95\text{ pN}$ at $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=5\text{ nm}$). Exact common-ratio $b=3$ angular and axial scaling variants (F3-ExactZ-StepB3, F3-ExactTheta-GapB3, F3-Exact3D-IsoB3) similarly retain $+17.23$ to $+19.73\text{ pN}$ sharp ($+14.36$ to $+16.70\text{ pN}$ at $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=5\text{ nm}$).
  \item \emph{Unconstrained clearance- and perimeter-matched $15\text{ nm}$ trench arrays (Controls M1--M3)}: Physically, a four-feature linear regression in horizontal edge perimeter $P_{\mathrm{edge}}$, vertical sidewall area $A_{\mathrm{sidewall}}$, tooth volume, and proximity-weighted sidewall area $\sum A_{\mathrm{wall},r}/g_r$ accounts for $R^2 = 0.823$ of the variance across the $19$ primary designs. Because F3-111's wider internal stator sub-ribs ($50\text{--}150\text{ nm}$) narrow every sub-slot to $50\text{ nm}$, F3-111 achieves larger $P_{\mathrm{edge}}$ ($34.33\ \mu\text{m}$ versus $24.58\text{--}27.12\ \mu\text{m}$) and $A_{\mathrm{sidewall}}$ ($2.75\ \mu\text{m}^2$ versus $1.91\text{--}2.11\ \mu\text{m}^2$) while locking every sub-tooth to $g_r = 15\text{ nm}$. When non-fractal 12-trench arrays are explicitly constructed with widened ribs so as to match F3-111 in twelve $50\text{ nm}$ trenches, $20\text{ nm}$ sub-teeth, $g_r = 15\text{ nm}$ clearance, $P_{\mathrm{edge}} = 34.33\ \mu\text{m}$, and $A_{\mathrm{sidewall}} = 2.75\ \mu\text{m}^2$, they reach $+18.68\text{ pN}$ (M1-Uni12-g15), $+19.37\text{ pN}$ (M2-Mono12-g15), and $+19.54\text{ pN}$ (M3-Shuf12-g15) on the sharp mesh, and $+15.58$, $+15.73$, and $+15.87\text{ pN}$ on the equal-panel-count $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=5\text{ nm}$ mesh (versus $+15.30\text{ pN}$ for F3-111).
\end{enumerate}

These clearance-matched controls slightly exceed F3-111 by $0.7\text{--}5.3\%$ (sharp) and $1.8\text{--}3.7\%$ ($r_{\mathrm{edge}}=5\text{ nm}$) due to outer-radius trench packing. Consequently, fractality does not produce a general force enhancement over unconstrained $15\text{ nm}$ trench arrays; rather, total open-slot repulsion is governed by near-field edge perimeter, vertical sidewall area, and $15\text{ nm}$ clearance, whereas the Cantor-inspired architecture embeds those $15\text{ nm}$ sub-cavities inside multiscale primary slots using mechanically wider ($\ge 50\text{ nm}$) internal stator sub-ribs.

\section*{Projected room-temperature feasibility}

Because no fabricated device or force measurement is reported in this work, we evaluate room-temperature ($T = 300\text{ K}$) nanoelectromechanical system (NEMS) operation strictly as model projections to identify the critical experimental parameters required for future realization (Extended Data Fig.~5, Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:nems_feasibility}, and Methods). Direct Matsubara summation at $T = 300\text{ K}$ ($0.45\text{--}0.83\%$ pole-truncation tail residual) projects that Drude gold retains $+1.66\text{ pN}$ (Cantor-N2-HybridRZ), $+1.48\text{ pN}$ (Compact-N2), and $+18.55\text{ pN}$ (F3-111, Standard sharp mesh; $+15.5\text{--}19.8\text{ pN}$ across $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=5\text{ nm}$ physical tiers) of open-slot repulsion at $\theta=3^\circ$, switching to $-3.01$, $-4.29$, and $-19.98\text{ pN}$ of parallel-plate attraction at $\theta=48^\circ$ (Extended Data Fig.~5a). Mechanically rotating between states via an external rotary comb actuator traverses an attractive sidewall-crossing well at $\theta = 18^\circ\text{--}21^\circ$ with a projected barrier of $0.286\text{--}0.323\text{ eV}$ ($0.23\text{ eV}$ endpoint difference) and peak Casimir torques of $222.2\text{--}386.7\text{ pN}\cdot\text{nm}$ across $N=2$ and $N=3$ (Extended Data Fig.~5e).

Three practical constraints will govern experimental feasibility. First, from BEM mutual-capacitance gradients $|\partial C_{\mathrm{el}}/\partial z|$, uniform residual electrostatic bias cancels predicted room-temperature Casimir repulsion above $V_{\mathrm{crit}} = 90.2\text{--}99.9\text{ mV}$ ($92.1\text{--}101.7\text{ mV}$ under $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=5\text{ nm}$ Ultra-High BEM; Extended Data Fig.~5b). Although Kelvin-probe compensation routinely nulls spatially averaged contact potential differences below $5\text{ mV}$, spatially varying work-function patch potentials on polycrystalline gold ($10\text{--}30\text{ mV}$ when grain sizes approach the $15\text{ nm}$ clearances) cannot be nulled by a single global bias and will require experimental characterization on small-grain or epitaxial gold films. Second, suppressing lateral Earnshaw snap-in across $15\text{ nm}$ clearances requires high radial-to-axial stiffness ratios; Euler--Bernoulli and finite-element beam models project that a four-spoke single-crystal $\mathrm{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ clamped-guided flexure ($k_{z,\mathrm{mech}} = 121.1\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$, $k_{r,\mathrm{mech}} = 1030.5\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$ in isometric Regime~I) exceeds destabilizing radial Casimir stiffnesses ($0.31\text{--}3.50\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$) by $295\text{--}3299$-fold and vertical pull-in thresholds by $4.5\text{--}55.6$-fold, yielding projected $300\text{ K}$ thermomechanical lock-in signal-to-noise ratios above $7 \times 10^3$ in $1\text{ s}$ at assumed ultra-high-vacuum quality factors $Q = 10^4\text{--}10^5$, or $3.2\text{--}12.6\text{ nm}$ static repulsive lifts in compliant Regimes~II--III (Extended Data Fig.~5c,f). Third, realizing $15\text{ nm}$ radial and axial gaps without capillary stiction requires sub-$5\text{ nm}$ interferometric overlay electron-beam lithography and vapor-phase HF release, though modelled forces vary smoothly across $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=5\text{--}10\text{ nm}$ rounding and $\pm 10\text{ nm}$ critical-dimension etch biases (Extended Data Fig.~5d and Methods).

In summary, our 3D electromagnetic calculations predict that multiscale interdigitated ring cavities can suppress parallel-plate attraction in the open-slot state to generate piconewton-scale vacuum repulsion and switch to normal attraction over recessed landing pads at zero electrical bias. While unconstrained $15\text{ nm}$-clearance non-fractal trench arrays match or slightly exceed the peak repulsive force of F3-111---demonstrating that near-field sidewall area and $15\text{ nm}$ clearance rather than fractality per se determine peak force magnitude---Cantor-inspired $(r,\theta,z)$ staging provides a versatile multiscale switch architecture that outperforms equal-sub-slot controls under a fixed primary-slot budget and enables multi-stage vertical stroke modulation. Independent quantitative verification using conformal sub-voxel or higher-order boundary-integral solvers, followed by nanofabrication and surface-potential metrology on low-patch-potential gold NEMS structures, will be essential to test these predictions experimentally.

\subsection*{1D transfer-matrix recursion and motivating log-periodic scaling heuristic}
To motivate our multiscale cavity design heuristically\cite{gordon2026prd}, consider an idealized 1D scalar system parameterized by a logarithmically running dimensionless Casimir coefficient $C(d_s, \ln(d/\ell_*))$---where $d_s$ is an effective spectral dimension, $d$ is the separation scale, and $\ell_* = L b^{-N}$ is the $N$-th generation prefractal cutoff below outer scale $L$---satisfying the macroscopic vacuum stress-energy trace identity\cite{gordon2026prd}:
\begin{equation}
T^\mu{}_{\mu,\mathrm{vac}} = -\rho_{\mathrm{vac}} + 2P_\parallel + P_\perp = -\frac{\hbar c}{d^4} \,\partial_{\ln d}C\!\left(d_s, \ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right),
\label{eq:trace_running}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_{\mathrm{vac}}$, $P_\parallel$, and $P_\perp$ denote the renormalized interaction energy density and transverse and normal Casimir pressures. For the 1D reduced Euclidean Green's function $g(z, z'; \kappa)$ at imaginary wavenumber $\kappa = \sqrt{(\xi/c)^2 + \mathbf{k}_\perp^2}$ across an $N$-th generation pre-Cantor stack $\mathcal{C}_N$ of semitransparent delta-function barriers $V(z) = \lambda_0 \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_N} \delta(z - z_i)$ on $[0, d]$, wave amplitudes across barrier $z_i$ satisfy $[-\partial_z^2 + \kappa^2 + V(z)]g(z,z';\kappa) = \delta(z-z')$ and are linked by single-plate transfer matrices $\mathbf{M}_i(\kappa)$ with global product $\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{global}}(\kappa) = \prod_{i \in \mathcal{C}_N} \mathbf{M}_i(\kappa)$. Finite coupling $\lambda_0 < \infty$ permits high-frequency mode tunnelling ($|t(\kappa)|^2 = 4\kappa^2/(4\kappa^2 + \lambda_0^2) > 0$), preserving global thermal equilibrium across sub-cavities prior to taking strong-coupling limits\cite{gordon2026prd}. Partitioning $\mathcal{C}_N$ (scale ratio $b$) into its two $(N-1)$-th generation sub-stacks yields the interaction energy recursion per unit area:
\begin{equation}
\Delta e_{\mathcal{C}_N}(d) = 2 \Delta e_{\mathcal{C}_{N-1}}(d/b) + \Delta e_{\mathrm{cross}}(d; \ell_*),
\label{eq:recursion_main}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta e_{\mathrm{cross}}$ represents inter-cavity evanescent cross-talk. Under the idealized fixed-point assumption that $\Delta e_{\mathrm{cross}}$ scales homogeneously as $d^{-3}$ up to multiplicatively periodic corrections in $\ell_* \ll d \ll L$, the discrete recursion admits the log-periodic Fourier expansion:
\begin{equation}
C\!\left(d_s, \ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k \, e^{i \omega_k \ln(d/\ell_*)} = C_0(d_s) \left[ 1 + F_{\mathrm{per}}\!\left(\ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right) \right], \qquad \omega_k = \frac{2\pi k}{\ln b},
\label{eq:fourier_running}
\end{equation}
with $e(d) = (\hbar c / d^3)C(d_s, \ln(d/\ell_*))$, generalized at temperature $T$ via Matsubara frequencies $\xi_n = 2\pi n k_B T/\hbar$ ($\kappa_n = \xi_n/c$) to $C_T(d_s, \ln(d/\ell_*), d/\lambda_T)$ with $\lambda_T = \hbar c/(2\pi k_B T)$\cite{gordon2026prd}. Equations~\eqref{eq:trace_running}--\eqref{eq:fourier_running} serve strictly as a 1D motivating heuristic; all 3D electromagnetic predictions below are computed directly via 3D BEM and FDTD without assuming values for $d_s$ or $C_k$.

\subsection*{3D boundary-element solver, $C_4$ Bloch reduction, FDTD sign check and error budget}
Casimir interaction energies $E_C$ and axial forces $F_z$ in vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bg}}=1.0$) are evaluated via the boundary-integral log-determinant along $\kappa = \xi/c$\cite{reid2013pra}:
\begin{equation}
E_C = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi} \int_0^\infty d\kappa \, \ln\det\bigl(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}(\kappa)\bigr),
\qquad
F_z = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi} \int_0^\infty d\kappa \, \mathrm{Tr}\!\left[\bigl(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}(\kappa)\bigr)^{-1} \frac{\partial \mathbf{M}(\kappa)}{\partial z_{\mathrm{rotor}}}\right],
\label{eq:methods_bem}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{M}(\kappa) = -\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{diag}}^{-1}(\kappa)\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{off}}(\kappa)$ subtracts isolated rigid-body self-energies identically at each $\kappa$. Piecewise-constant collocation panels use single-pole Drude gold ($\omega_p = 9.03\text{ eV}$, $\gamma = 0.053\text{ eV}$)\cite{rakic1998}, coupling two tangential surface-current unknowns per panel via $\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';\kappa) = (\mathbf{I} - \kappa^{-2}\nabla\nabla')G_0(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';\kappa)$ with $G_0 = \exp(-\kappa |\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|)/(4\pi |\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|)$ and sub-panel regularization $\sigma_{\mathrm{panel}} = \sqrt{(A_t+A_s)/(32\pi)}$. Cyclic $C_4$ symmetry block-diagonalizes the matrix into four Bloch representations ($\ell_{\mathrm{B}} \in \{0,1,2,3\}$) via $\mathbf{M}^{(\ell_{\mathrm{B}})}(\kappa) = \sum_{q=0}^{3} e^{i\pi \ell_{\mathrm{B}} q/2}\mathbf{M}_{0,q}(\kappa)$, reducing memory $16$-fold and dense pivoted LU factorization cost $64$-fold across Standard ($656$ panels for $N=0,1$; $1{,}504$ for $N=2$; $3{,}168$ for 7-track $N=3$; $6{,}784$ across four sectors for 12-tooth 3D $N=3$), High ($3{,}120$ for $N=2$; $6{,}576$ for $N=3$), and Ultra-High ($6{,}016$ for $N=2$) meshes. At $T = 300\text{ K}$, Matsubara summation over $\kappa_n = 2\pi n k_B T/(\hbar c)$ applies weight $1/2$ at the static $n=0$ transverse-magnetic mode ($\kappa_0 = 10^{-6}\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$, with vanishing static transverse-electric reflection).

Canonical BEM validation checks confirm: (1) monotonic parallel-disk ($R = 1.0\ \mu\text{m}$) continuum convergence to exact 1D Lifshitz pressures across $d \in \{100, 50, 30, 15\}\text{ nm}$ ($-5.43$ vs.\ $-5.66\text{ Pa}$ [$4.04\%$], $-58.09$ vs.\ $-58.70\text{ Pa}$ [$1.03\%$], $-311.98$ vs.\ $-313.32\text{ Pa}$ [$0.43\%$], and $-2872.43$ vs.\ $-2880.44\text{ Pa}$ [$0.28\%$]); (2) trace versus central finite-difference ($\Delta z = 0.5\text{ nm}$) agreement on Cantor-N2-HybridRZ to $99.964\%$ at $\theta=3^\circ$ ($+1.6570$ vs.\ $+1.6564\text{ pN}$) and $99.9942\%$ at $\theta=48^\circ$ ($-3.0100$ vs.\ $-3.0098\text{ pN}$); (3) $T=300\text{ K}$ Matsubara pole convergence upon doubling $N_{\mathrm{Matsubara}}$ from $64$ to $128$ poles ($0.45\%$ tail residual at $\theta=3^\circ$, $+1.6492 \to +1.6566\text{ pN}$; $0.83\%$ at $\theta=48^\circ$, $-2.9867 \to -3.0116\text{ pN}$); (4) Gauss--Legendre quadrature drift of $0.0024\%$ above $n_\xi = 24$ ($+6.2713\text{ pN}$ at $n_\xi=24$ vs.\ $+6.2712\text{ pN}$ at $n_\xi=64$, PEC); and (5) dielectric dispersion sensitivity of $9.72\%$ at $\theta=3^\circ$ ($+1.8180$ vs.\ $+1.6570\text{ pN}$) and $16.14\%$ at $\theta=48^\circ$ ($-3.4957$ vs.\ $-3.0100\text{ pN}$) when replacing single-pole Drude gold with 6-pole Raki\'c Lorentz--Drude gold\cite{rakic1998}.

For the 3D $N=3$ architectures, three nested physical-resolution tiers---Tier~1 ($h_{r,\mathrm{tooth}}=15\text{ nm}, h_{r,\mathrm{slot}}=45\text{ nm}, \Delta\phi_{\mathrm{lobe}}=3.0^\circ, h_z=110\text{ nm}$), Tier~2 ($10\text{ nm}, 30\text{ nm}, 2.25^\circ, 75\text{ nm}$), and Tier~3 ($7.5\text{ nm}, 22.5\text{ nm}, 1.8^\circ, 55\text{ nm}$; $\overline{\mathrm{AR}} \approx 5.1\text{--}6.8$)---yield $+15.5 \to +16.2 \to +19.8\text{ pN}$ ($6{,}056 \to 8{,}720 \to 11{,}360$ panels) at $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=5\text{ nm}$ and $+11.4 \to +12.8 \to +15.4\text{ pN}$ ($6{,}864 \to 10{,}304 \to 14{,}464$ panels) at $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=10\text{ nm}$ for F3-111, with single-axis upgrades F1r-100 ($+11.8 \to +11.8 \to +14.5\text{ pN}$), F1t-010 ($+4.5 \to +5.2 \to +5.4\text{ pN}$), and F1z-001 ($+4.4 \to +4.3 \to +5.5\text{ pN}$) giving a linear additive sum of $+14.3 \to +14.5 \to +17.2\text{ pN}$ at $r_{\mathrm{edge}}=5\text{ nm}$. In the engaged state ($\theta=48^\circ$, $d=15\text{ nm}$), $h = N_{\mathrm{panels}}^{-1/2} \to 0$ extrapolation gives $-37.23\text{ pN}$ (Compact-N2) and $-43.54\text{ to }-65.33\text{ pN}$ (TightSlot-N2). \subsection*{Spectral band decomposition and stepped $(r,z)$ stroke regression}
On the exact-scaling coplanar $N=3$ ring ($b=3$, $R \in [0.20, 1.55]\ \mu\text{m}$, $w_{\min}=5\text{ nm}$, $g_{r,m} \in \{135, 45, 15\}\text{ nm}$, $\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{tooth}}=8^\circ$ inside $24^\circ$ slots), we evaluate the radial-sidewall coupling norm
\begin{equation}
w_m(\kappa) = \frac{1}{2}\kappa^6 \left( |r_{\mathrm{TM}}(\kappa)|^2 + |r_{\mathrm{TE}}(\kappa)|^2 \right) \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{rot}}^{(m)}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{stat}}^{(m)}} \left| \bigl[\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{RS}}^{(0)}(\kappa)\bigr]_{ij} \right|^2
\label{eq:methods_spectral_subband}
\end{equation}
and signed trace integrand $f_{z,m}(\kappa) = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi}\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{P}^{(m)}}[(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{M}(\kappa))^{-1}\partial_{z_{\mathrm{rotor}}}\mathbf{M}(\kappa)]$ (summing to $F_z$ within $<10^{-16}\text{ pN}\cdot\mu\text{m}$ using exact vertex-radius classification) across a $91$-point logarithmic grid $\kappa \in [1.5, 150]\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$ plus $21$-point parabolic peak refinement (and a $76$-point grid across $\kappa \in [0.5, 40]\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$ for F0-000 and F3-111). Comparing three nested Gaussian log-wavenumber models via AIC, BIC, and $\chi^2_{\mathrm{peak}} = \sum_{m=1}^{3}(\ln\hat{\kappa}_m - \mu_m)^2/\sigma_{\ln\hat{\kappa}_m}^2$ rejects exact $\ln 3$ spacing on both the coplanar $N=3$ ring ($R^2 = 0.9125$, $\chi^2_{\mathrm{peak}} = 7415.3$, vs.\ compressed periodic $b_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1.76$, $R^2 = 0.9993$, $\chi^2_{\mathrm{peak}} = 41.4$, and unrestricted multiband $R^2 = 0.9998$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} = -1661.6$) and F3-111 ($R^2 = 0.8131$, $\chi^2_{\mathrm{peak}} = 6098.2$, vs.\ unrestricted $R^2 = 0.9958$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} = -888.9$).

For the vertically stepped switch (Cantor-N3-SteppedRZ, $b=2.5$, $R_c \in \{0.45, 0.85, 1.25\}\ \mu\text{m}$ in sequence $m \in \{3,1,2\}$, $\ell_m = 160\text{ nm} \times b^{-(m-1)}$, $W_{\mathrm{slot},m} = 2.4\ell_m$, $w_{\mathrm{tooth},m} = 1.1\ell_m$, $H_{\mathrm{ring},m} = 0.55\ell_m$, $z_{\mathrm{stage},m} = 250\text{ nm} \times b^{-(m-1)} \in \{250, 100, 40\}\text{ nm}$), sweeping $36$ logarithmic depths $z_d \in [25, 450]\text{ nm}$ ($\theta=3^\circ$, $n_\xi=12$) and fitting $Y(x) = z_d^2 F_z$ ($x = \ln(z_d/z_{\mathrm{stage},3})$) via $Y_{\mathrm{fit}}(x) = c_0 + c_1 x + c_2 x^2 + \sum_{k=1}^{2}[a_k\cos(k\omega_1 x) + b_k\sin(k\omega_1 x)]$ at $\omega_1 = 2\pi/\ln b = 6.86$ yields $A_1 = (2.58 \pm 0.46)\times 10^{-3}\text{ pN}\cdot\mu\text{m}^2$ ($R^2 = 0.53$, $F = 7.92$, $p = 1.92\times 10^{-4}$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} = -18.6$, $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = -12.2$, quantifying deterministic parametric fit quality against linear [$R^2 = 0.18$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} = +1.4$] and uniform [$R^2 = 0.01$] controls). Unconstrained Lomb--Scargle regression across $\omega \in [1.0, 14.0]$ recovers $\hat{\omega}_1 = 6.46 \pm 0.35$ ($95\%\text{ CI }[6.11, 6.80]$, $R^2_1 = 0.59$) and $\hat{b} = 2.65 \pm 0.14$ ($95\%\text{ CI }[2.52, 2.79]$; 2-harmonic $\hat{b}_{2\mathrm{h}} = 2.62 \pm 0.15$).

\subsection*{Modelled NEMS electrostatics, flexure mechanics, SNR projections and fabrication route}
From the $\kappa \to 0$ Laplace limit of the 3D BEM solver ($|\partial C_{\mathrm{el}}/\partial z| = 0.0003, 0.0004, \text{and }0.0037\text{ pN}\,\text{mV}^{-2}$ for Compact-N2, Cantor-N2-HybridRZ, and F3-111), balancing $F_{z,\mathrm{el}} = -\frac{1}{2}|\partial C_{\mathrm{el}}/\partial z|V_{\mathrm{rms}}^2$ against Standard-mesh $300\text{ K}$ repulsion defines uniform-bias thresholds $V_{\mathrm{crit}} = 94.1, 90.2, \text{and }99.9\text{ mV}$. Four-spoke single-crystal $\mathrm{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ clamped-guided flexures ($E_Y = 1.69\times 10^{11}\text{ Pa}$, $k_{z,\mathrm{mech}} = N_s E_Y w_{\mathrm{spoke}} t_{\mathrm{spoke}}^3/L_{\mathrm{spoke}}^3$, $k_{r,\mathrm{mech}}/k_{z,\mathrm{mech}} = (w_{\mathrm{spoke}}/t_{\mathrm{spoke}})^2$) are modelled across three regimes relative to nonlinear pull-in stiffness $k_{z,\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{pull\text{-}in}} = \frac{(\eta+1)^{\eta+1}}{\eta^\eta}\frac{|F_{z,0}^{\mathrm{att}}|}{d_0}$ ($d_0 = 15\text{ nm}$, $\eta = 3.5$; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:nems_feasibility}): isometric Regime~I ($L_{\mathrm{spoke}} = 15\ \mu\text{m}$, $k_{z,\mathrm{mech}} = 121.1\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$, $k_{r,\mathrm{mech}} = 1030.5\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$, $\delta z_{\mathrm{th}} = 0.18\text{ nm}$), nanometre-stroke Regime~II ($k_{z,\mathrm{mech}} = 0.415\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$, $k_{r,\mathrm{mech}} = 11.82\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$, $\delta z_{\mathrm{th}} = 3.16\text{ nm}$), and contact-release Regime~III ($k_{z,\mathrm{mech}} = 0.137\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$, $L_{\mathrm{spoke}} = 60\ \mu\text{m}$, $t_{\mathrm{spoke}} = 0.05\ \mu\text{m}$, $\delta z_{\mathrm{th}} = 5.50\text{ nm}$). Combining gold rotor mass ($m_{\mathrm{rotor,Au}} = 3.05, 5.59, \text{and }7.71\text{ pg}$ for Compact-N2, Cantor-N2-HybridRZ, and F3-111) with $\frac{3}{8}m_{\mathrm{spokes,Si}}$ (verified within $\le 0.22\%$ by a $64$-element Hermite cubic Euler--Bernoulli finite-element solver; $f_0 = 627.6\text{ kHz}$ for Cantor-N2-HybridRZ and $556.5\text{ kHz}$ for F3-111 in Regime~I), Langevin force noise $S_F^{1/2} = \sqrt{4 k_B T k_{z,\mathrm{mech}}/(\omega_0 Q)}$ at $300\text{ K}$ and assumed $Q \in \{10^4, 10^5\}$ yields $\mathrm{SNR}(1\text{ s}) = 7{,}232\text{--}7{,}345$ ($N=2$) and $77{,}432$ (F3-111; $245{,}000$ at $Q=10^5$, $|\partial F_z/\partial z_0| = 1.16\text{ pN}\,\text{nm}^{-1}$). A proposed four-stage SOI process uses $100\text{ keV}$ electron-beam-lithography damascene gold landing pads, atomic-layer-deposited and planarized sacrificial $\mathrm{SiO}_2$ ($15\text{ nm}$ standoff), sub-$5\text{ nm}$ interferometrically aligned stator rings and rotor teeth, and anhydrous vapor-HF release followed by ultra-high-vacuum annealing and Kelvin-probe nulling.
