\begin{abstract}
Attractive Casimir forces between proximate surfaces in vacuum pose a fundamental stiction barrier for nanoelectromechanical systems (NEMS). While reflection-symmetric conductors in vacuum mutually attract, three-dimensional interdigitation of metallic rotor teeth into slotted stator apertures in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) generates an axial repulsive Casimir restoring force ($F_z > 0$) driven by transverse saddle-point fringing fields. Here we disentangle the geometric origin of this true-vacuum repulsion from the role of multi-scale fractal hierarchy and introduce a complete engineering design for the \emph{Gold Concentric Cantor-Ring Rotary Quantum Clutch}. We show that while a single interdigitated tooth ($N=0$) already produces aperture repulsion, simply widening a single tooth across an annular rotor collapses the lateral fringing gradient and eliminates repulsion ($F_z = \CtrlSinglewideBemThetathreeFZPn{}\text{ pN}$). Organizing concentric interdigitated rings into a prefractal Cantor hierarchy ($N=2, 3$) with scale-matched radial widths and stepped vertical insertion depths preserves nanoscale slot proximity across the entire annulus, amplifying repulsive lift by $>28\times$ in 4-pass 3D Finite-Difference Time-Domain (FDTD) MEEP simulations relative to uniform periodic controls and increasing rotational switching swing across generations. Using three independent first-principles electromagnetic solvers---3D Vector-Dyadic Boundary Element Method (BEM), 3D Scalar-Yukawa BEM, and 4-pass inclusion--exclusion 3D FDTD MEEP on the BigRed 200 supercomputer---we demonstrate unpowered rotary switching between disengaged slot-aligned repulsion ($\theta=0^\circ\text{--}6^\circ$, reaching $+\CantorNtwoHybridrzBemHighFZPn{}\text{ pN}$ in High-tier Vector-Dyadic BEM and $+\CantorNtwoHybridrzMeepRfivezeroFZPn{}\text{ pN}$ in 4-pass MEEP for \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}, and $+\RotConezeroFractalNthreeHighThetaZerozerothreepzeroVectorDyadicFZPn{}\text{ pN}$ in High-tier BEM for $N=3$) and engaged landing-pad attraction ($\theta=18^\circ\text{--}48^\circ$, $F_z < 0$). Finally, we establish a silicon-on-insulator (SOI) nanofabrication and stability blueprint at $T = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoCdToleranceTemperatureK{}\text{ K}$, quantifying room-temperature Matsubara dispersion, mutual-capacitance patch-potential bounds ($V_{\rm crit} = \NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzPatchPotentialVCritMv{}\text{--}\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoPatchPotentialVCritMv{}\text{ mV}$), lateral Earnshaw stabilization via a $\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilitySpokeCount{}$-spoke single-crystal $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ flexure suspension, and robustness across lithographic corner-rounding and critical-dimension ($\Delta\text{CD}$) etch tolerances.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Casimir effect---the macroscopic force arising from boundary-modified quantum electromagnetic vacuum fluctuations \cite{casimir1948,bordag2009}---becomes the dominant mechanical interaction at sub-100-nanometer separations. In conventional planar and micromechanical architectures operating in vacuum, Casimir forces are strongly attractive ($F_z \propto -d^{-4}$), causing irreversible pull-in stiction and permanent device failure in nanoelectromechanical switches, rotary actuators, and ultra-compliant sensors \cite{rodriguez2011}. By the Kenneth--Klich no-go theorem \cite{kennethklich2006}, any two mirror-symmetric bodies composed of ordinary conductors or non-magnetic dielectrics in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) mutually attract, ruling out repulsive restoring forces between planar or reflection-symmetric surfaces without chemical liquid immersion.

However, Levin, McCauley, Rodriguez, Reid, and Johnson demonstrated that reflection symmetry can be broken geometrically in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$): when an elongated metallic ridge approaches or interdigitates with a slotted metallic aperture plate, transverse electric-field lines connecting the ridge tip to the upper sidewalls of the slot create a saddle-point energy landscape that pulls the ridge back toward the slot mid-plane, yielding a repulsive axial Casimir restoring force ($F_z > 0$) along the vertical axis \cite{levin2010,rodriguez2011}. In parallel, theoretical studies of self-similar and prefractal Casimir cavities have shown that multiscale boundary hierarchies reshape vacuum mode spectra and interaction energies across length scales \cite{shajesh2016,shajesh2017,gordon2026prd}.

Translating geometric vacuum repulsion into a practical, dynamically switchable NEMS actuator requires answering a fundamental physical question: \emph{what is the precise mechanistic role of tooth interdigitation versus multi-scale fractal geometry?} Specifically, repulsion in true vacuum is not generated by fractality per se; it is an electromagnetic consequence of lateral slot-tooth interdigitation \cite{levin2010}. Yet a single narrow tooth generates sub-piconewton forces, and naively widening a single tooth to cover a larger actuator footprint separates the opposing slot sidewalls, destroys the transverse fringing-field gradient, and eliminates the repulsive force entirely.

In this work, we resolve this scaling bottleneck by introducing, benchmarking, and engineering the \emph{Gold Concentric Cantor-Ring Rotary Quantum Clutch} in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$). Our core contributions form a single, unified physical and engineering narrative:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Disentangling Interdigitation Repulsion from Multi-Scale Cantor Amplification}: Through a six-step controlled numerical progression, we prove that axial repulsion ($F_z > 0$) originates from the vertical interdigitation of rotor teeth inside open stator slots ($\theta = 0^\circ\text{--}6^\circ$), flipping to attraction when a tooth is retracted above the vertical equilibrium plane ($z_{\rm tip} = -55\text{ nm}$ overhang control) or rotated over recessed landing pads ($\theta = 18^\circ\text{--}48^\circ$). Crucially, while widening a single track (\texttt{Ctrl\_SingleWide}) collapses repulsion to attraction ($\CtrlSinglewideBemThetathreeFZPn{}\text{ pN}$), partitioning the annular rotor into a prefractal Cantor hierarchy ($N=0 \to N=2 \to N=3$) with scale-matched radial widths and stepped vertical insertion depths (\texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}) preserves high-gradient nanoscale sidewall gaps across the entire disk, amplifying both repulsive lift and rotational switching swing beyond uniform periodic arrays (\texttt{Ctrl\_UniformPeriodic}).
    \item \textbf{Three-Solver First-Principles Verification in True Vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$)}: We benchmark the rotary clutch across three independent 3D solvers: 3D Vector-Dyadic BEM (\texttt{Standard}, \texttt{High}, and \texttt{Ultra\_High} meshes, plus full-monolithic passive-support validation), 3D Scalar-Yukawa BEM, and 4-pass inclusion--exclusion 3D FDTD MEEP simulations on the BigRed 200 supercomputer ($R=40, 50, 60$ Yee-grid convergence), contrasting all three against the 1D Lifshitz proximity-force approximation (PFA).
    \item \textbf{Room-Temperature ($T = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoCdToleranceTemperatureK{}\text{ K}$) SOI NEMS Engineering Blueprint}: We provide a complete fabrication and stability specification for building the device on a silicon-on-insulator (SOI) platform, quantifying finite-temperature Matsubara dispersion of dispersive Drude Gold, 3D BEM mutual-capacitance patch-potential voltage budgets ($V_{\rm crit}$), lateral stabilization against Earnshaw's theorem via a $\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilitySpokeCount{}$-spoke single-crystal $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ flexure suspension, and force margins across realistic electron-beam lithography (EBL) corner-rounding ($r_{\rm edge}=5\text{--}10\text{ nm}$) and critical-dimension ($\Delta\text{CD} \in [-10, +10]\text{ nm}$) etch tolerances.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Architecture of the Gold Concentric Cantor-Ring Rotary Quantum Clutch}
\label{sec:architecture}

\subsection{Rotational Switching Between Slot-Aligned Repulsion and Landing-Pad Attraction}

Unlike static lithographic structures whose Casimir interaction is fixed once fabricated, the \emph{Gold Concentric Cantor-Ring Rotary Quantum Clutch} operates entirely in \textbf{true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$)} and switches its axial force sign dynamically via in-plane rotation $\theta$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum_clutch_architecture_and_narrative}). The device comprises a concentric Gold rotor suspended above a stationary Gold stator with four-fold cyclic symmetry ($C_4$, $90^\circ$ sector period). In the baseline vertical configuration, downward-protruding concentric rotor teeth ($z_{\rm tooth} \in [-100, 0]\text{ nm}$, height $H_{\rm teeth} = 100\text{ nm}$) interdigitate radially between thin upper stator aperture rings ($z_{\rm stator} \in [-25, 0]\text{ nm}$, membrane thickness $t_{\rm membrane} = 25\text{ nm}$) while suspended above recessed azimuthal stator landing pads ($z_{\rm pad,top} = -115\text{ nm}$, thickness $t_{\rm pad} = 150\text{ nm}$).

Rotating the rotor through angle $\theta$ modulates the azimuthal overlap between the rotor teeth and the recessed stator landing pads, switching the axial Casimir force $F_z$ continuously between two operational states:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Disengaged / Slot-Aligned State ($\theta = 0^\circ\text{--}6^\circ$)}: The rotor teeth sit centered inside the open angular apertures between stator bridge segments with zero vertical landing-pad overlap ($A_{\rm overlap} = 0$). Because the rotor tooth tips ($z_{\rm tip} = -100\text{ nm}$) extend $75\text{ nm}$ below the bottom face of the upper stator ring ($z = -25\text{ nm}$), non-monotonic transverse fringing fields coupling the rotor tooth sidewalls to the upper stator slot walls pull the rotor upward toward the stator slot mid-plane ($z = -12.5\text{ nm}$), generating a positive, repulsive axial restoring force ($F_z > 0$).
    \item \textbf{Engaged / Landing-Pad State ($\theta = 18^\circ\text{--}48^\circ$)}: Rotating the rotor moves the teeth out of the open aperture slots and aligns the horizontal bottom faces of the rotor teeth ($z = -100\text{ nm}$) directly over the horizontal top surfaces of the recessed stator landing pads ($z = -115\text{ nm}$) across a narrow $d = 15\text{ nm}$ vertical vacuum gap. In this regime, direct face-to-face parallel-plate coupling dominates over lateral sidewall fringing, switching the net axial interaction to strong normal Casimir attraction ($F_z < 0$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Prefractal Cantor Radial-and-Vertical Hierarchy versus Non-Fractal Controls}

To isolate how radial multi-scale subdivision affects repulsive lift and rotational switching swing across a fixed annular footprint ($R \in [R_{\rm min}, R_{\rm max}]$), we construct a systematic hierarchy of prefractal Cantor ring architectures alongside matched non-fractal control geometries:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Single-Track Baseline ($N=0$, \texttt{SingleTooth\_Track0} / \texttt{Fractal\_N0\_SingleTrack})}: A single concentric rotor tooth ring ($R_{\rm tooth} \in [0.2325, 0.3175]\ \mu\text{m}$, width $w_{\rm tooth} = 85\text{ nm}$) centered inside a single stator slot ($R_{\rm slot} \in [0.20, 0.35]\ \mu\text{m}$, width $W_{\rm slot} = 150\text{ nm}$, lateral sidewall gap $g_r = 32.5\text{ nm}$), used to benchmark fundamental single-tooth interdigitation repulsion, vertical overhang sign reversal ($z_{\rm tip} \in [-110, -55]\text{ nm}$), and single-track angular switching.
    \item \textbf{Fundamental Single-Slot Cantor Ring ($N=1$, \texttt{Fractal\_N1\_FundRing})}: The first Cantor generation over the full annular domain $R \in [0.20, 1.35]\ \mu\text{m}$, removing the central third to form a single wide slot ($W_{\rm slot} = 383.3\text{ nm}$, tooth width $w_{\rm tooth} = 210.8\text{ nm}$, lateral gap $g_r = 86.3\text{ nm}$).
    \item \textbf{Second-Generation 3-Track Cantor Hierarchies ($N=2$, \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_FlatZ}, \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}, \texttt{Rot\_C8\_Compact\_N2}, and \texttt{Rot\_C4\_TightSlot\_N2})}: Two-level middle-third Cantor subdivision generating three concentric interdigitated tracks---a primary generation-1 central track flanked by two narrower generation-2 child tracks. In \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_FlatZ}, all three tracks share uniform vertical tooth depth $z_{\rm tip} = -100\text{ nm}$. In \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}, the vertical insertion depth is scale-matched to the radial hierarchy ($z_{\rm tip,1} = -110\text{ nm}$ with $H_{\rm teeth,1} = 110\text{ nm}$ on the central generation-1 track, and $z_{\rm tip,2} = -100\text{ nm}$ with $H_{\rm teeth,2} = 100\text{ nm}$ on the flanking generation-2 tracks), optimizing the vertical-to-radial aspect ratio across scales. The compact NEMS variants \texttt{Rot\_C8\_Compact\_N2} and \texttt{Rot\_C4\_TightSlot\_N2} compress the $N=2$ Cantor hierarchy into a compact radial envelope ($R \in [0.20, 0.90]\ \mu\text{m}$) with $35\text{--}60\text{ nm}$ lateral slot clearances.
    \item \textbf{Third-Generation 7-Track Cantor Hierarchy ($N=3$, \texttt{Fractal\_N3\_Cantor7Track} / \texttt{Rot\_C10\_Fractal\_N3})}: Three-level Cantor subdivision containing $2^3 - 1 = 7$ concentric interdigitated rotor tracks (one generation-1 track, two generation-2 tracks, and four narrow generation-3 tracks, yielding $4 \times 7 = 28$ rotor teeth across the four sectors), maximizing total sidewall perimeter across the annulus.
    \item \textbf{Matched Non-Fractal Controls (\texttt{Ctrl\_SingleWide}, \texttt{Ctrl\_UniformPeriodic}, and \texttt{Ctrl\_SizeMatched\_Monotonic})}: To test whether multi-track subdivision and Cantor scale distribution are physically necessary, we compare the $N=2$ Cantor designs against: (i) \texttt{Ctrl\_SingleWide}, a single wide rotor tooth matching the total radial span; (ii) \texttt{Ctrl\_UniformPeriodic}, a 3-track periodic grating with three identical equal-width teeth and slots matching the total active area of $N=2$; and (iii) \texttt{Ctrl\_SizeMatched\_Monotonic}, a 3-track non-symmetric control in which the exact track widths of the $N=2$ Cantor set are rearranged in monotonic radial order rather than self-similar flanking order.
\end{itemize}


\section{True-Vacuum 3D BEM and 4-Pass 3D FDTD Numerical Methodology}
\label{sec:fdtd_methodology}

To evaluate the electromagnetic Casimir interaction across these 3D interdigitated architectures in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) without relying on proximity approximations, we employ three independent first-principles solvers: (1) 3D Vector-Dyadic Boundary Element Method (BEM), (2) 3D Scalar-Yukawa BEM, and (3) 4-pass inclusion--exclusion 3D Finite-Difference Time-Domain (FDTD) MEEP simulations \cite{oskooi2010,rodriguez2011}.

\subsection{3D Boundary Element Scattering Formalism and Matsubara Summation}

In the surface-integral scattering formulation along the imaginary wavenumber axis $\kappa = \xi/c$, the Casimir interaction energy $E_C$ and axial force $F_z = -\partial E_C / \partial z_{\rm rotor}$ between the rotor and stator surfaces in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) are given by:
\begin{equation}
E_C = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi} \int_0^\infty d\kappa \, \ln\det\bigl(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}(\kappa)\bigr),
\qquad
F_z = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi} \int_0^\infty d\kappa \, \mathrm{Tr}\!\left[\bigl(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}(\kappa)\bigr)^{-1} \frac{\partial \mathbf{M}(\kappa)}{\partial z_{\rm rotor}}\right],
\label{eq:bem_logdet_force}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{M}(\kappa) = \mathbf{D}_{\rm diag}^{-1}(\kappa)\mathbf{V}_{\rm off}(\kappa)$ couples boundary panels across the rotor and stator using the dyadic Yukawa Green's kernel $\exp(-\kappa R)/(4\pi R)$ weighted by the dispersive Drude dielectric response of Gold ($\omega_p = 9.03\text{ eV}$, $\gamma = 0.053\text{ eV}$) in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$). Exploiting the four-fold cyclic symmetry ($C_4$) of the concentric ring sectors reduces the dense $N_{\rm total} \times N_{\rm total}$ system into four decoupled Bloch irreps ($m \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$), yielding a $16\times$ reduction in memory and a $64\times$ speedup in LU factorization. At finite temperature $T = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoCdToleranceTemperatureK{}\text{ K}$, the continuous $\kappa$-integral is replaced by the exact Matsubara summation over $\kappa_n = 2\pi n k_B T / (\hbar c)$ with prime weight $1/2$ at the static $n=0$ transverse-magnetic mode (where the static transverse-electric reflection vanishes in accordance with the Bohr--van Leeuwen theorem).

\subsection{4-Pass Inclusion--Exclusion 3D FDTD Stress-Tensor Integration}

To benchmark the 3D BEM predictions against full-wave time-domain electrodynamics in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$), we integrate the Wick-rotated Maxwell stress tensor $\mathbf{T}$ over closed per-tooth vacuum bounding boxes $S_i$ surrounding each rotor tooth inside its radial stator slot \cite{oskooi2010,rodriguez2011}:
\begin{equation}
F_z = \frac{\hbar}{\pi} \int_0^\infty d\xi \, \sum_{i} \oint_{S_i} d\mathbf{A} \cdot \langle \mathbf{T}(i\xi, \mathbf{x}) \rangle_z,
\qquad
T_{zz} = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \varepsilon_0 (E_z^2 - E_x^2 - E_y^2) + \mu_0 (H_z^2 - H_x^2 - H_y^2) \right].
\label{eq:fdtd_force_integral}
\end{equation}
To eliminate both the rotor's self-force and the uncoupled background scattering of the stator slot walls across the stress-box faces, each discrete-cosine-transform (DCT) boundary moment is evaluated using a rigorous \textbf{4-pass inclusion--exclusion subtraction} at identical Yee grid alignment (\texttt{eps\_averaging = False}, \texttt{Courant = 0.3}):
\begin{equation}
F_z^{\rm net} = \bigl(F_z^{\rm both} - F_z^{\rm rotor}\bigr) - \bigl(F_z^{\rm stator} - F_z^{\rm vac}\bigr).
\label{eq:four_pass_subtraction}
\end{equation}
All 4-pass MEEP simulations are executed across sharded MPI worker groups on the BigRed 200 supercomputer using the 6-pole Raki\'c Lorentz-Drude susceptibility model for Gold in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) and converted to SI piconewtons via $\hbar c / a^2 = 0.0316152677\text{ pN}\cdot\mu\text{m}^2$ at $a = 1\ \mu\text{m}$.

\section{NEMS Engineering Blueprint, Fabrication Process Flow, and 6-DOF Stability}
\label{sec:experimental}

\subsection{Silicon-on-Insulator (SOI) Nanofabrication Process Flow}

To realize the Gold Concentric Cantor-Ring Rotary Quantum Clutch in hardware, the device is designed around standard silicon-on-insulator (SOI) and damascene Gold nanofabrication processes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Bottom Stator Landing Pads and Isolation Trench}: Starting from an SOI substrate with a single-crystal $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ device layer ($E_Y = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityEYPa{}\text{ Pa}$), the recessed stator trench ($z \in [-650, -550]\text{ nm}$) and azimuthal landing pads ($z_{\rm pad,top} = -115\text{ nm}$, thickness $150\text{ nm}$) are patterned by high-resolution 100 keV electron-beam lithography (EBL) in hydrogen silsesquioxane (HSQ) or PMMA resist, followed by Ti/Au seed deposition and damascene Gold electroplating.
    \item \textbf{Sacrificial Spacer Planarization and Slotted Cantor Stator Membrane}: A sacrificial $\text{SiO}_2$ spacer layer is deposited by atomic-layer deposition (ALD) and chemical-mechanical polishing (CMP) to define the exact $15\text{ nm}$ vertical standoff between the landing pads ($z = -115\text{ nm}$) and the rotor tooth tips ($z = -100\text{ nm}$). The $25\text{ nm}$-thick slotted Cantor stator aperture rings ($z \in [-25, 0]\text{ nm}$, minimum slot clearances $25\text{--}60\text{ nm}$) are then patterned via overlay EBL aligned to sub-5 nm interferometric fiducial marks.
    \item \textbf{Stepped Concentric Rotor Teeth and $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ Flexure Suspension}: For the hybrid $(r,z)$ Cantor rotor (\texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}), a two-step grayscale or dual-exposure EBL mold defines the $110\text{ nm}$ primary generation-1 tooth and $100\text{ nm}$ flanking generation-2 teeth, anchored above to the radial spider spokes ($z \in [350, 470]\text{ nm}$) and a $\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilitySpokeCount{}$-spoke single-crystal $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ flexure suspension ($L_{\rm spoke} = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityLSpokeUm{}\ \mu\text{m}$, $w_{\rm spoke} = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityWSpokeUm{}\ \mu\text{m}$, $t_{\rm spoke} = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityTSpokeUm{}\ \mu\text{m}$).
    \item \textbf{Dry Vapor-HF Release and Kelvin-Probe Potential Nulling}: To prevent capillary stiction during release, the sacrificial oxide is removed using anhydrous vapor-phase hydrofluoric acid (vapor-HF) followed by ultra-high-vacuum annealing. Electrical contact to the rotor hub and stator sectors enables active Kelvin-probe nulling of residual work-function patch potentials well below the critical voltage threshold $V_{\rm crit}$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have established the physical mechanism, multi-scale fractal optimization, three-solver electromagnetic verification, and room-temperature NEMS engineering blueprint of the \emph{Gold Concentric Cantor-Ring Rotary Quantum Clutch} in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$).

The key findings of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Interdigitation Origin of Repulsion versus Multi-Scale Cantor Amplification}: True-vacuum axial Casimir repulsion ($F_z > 0$) originates from 3D slot-tooth interdigitation ($\theta=0^\circ\text{--}6^\circ$) and reverses sign under vertical overhang ($z_{\rm tip} = -55\text{ nm}$) or rotary alignment over recessed landing pads ($\theta=18^\circ\text{--}48^\circ$). While widening a single tooth (\texttt{Ctrl\_SingleWide}) collapses lateral fringing fields and eliminates repulsion ($\CtrlSinglewideBemThetathreeFZPn{}\text{ pN}$), subdividing the annulus into a prefractal Cantor hierarchy ($N=2, 3$) with scale-matched $(r,z)$ tooth depths (\texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}) preserves nanoscale sidewall proximity across the disk, amplifying repulsive lift ($+\CantorNtwoHybridrzBemHighFZPn{}\text{ pN}$ in High-tier BEM and $+\CantorNtwoHybridrzMeepRfivezeroFZPn{}\text{ pN}$ in 4-pass MEEP for $N=2$; $+\RotConezeroFractalNthreeHighThetaZerozerothreepzeroVectorDyadicFZPn{}\text{ pN}$ in High-tier BEM for $N=3$) and rotational switching swing beyond uniform periodic arrays.
    \item \textbf{Three-Solver Multi-Resolution Convergence}: Across \texttt{Standard}, \texttt{High}, and \texttt{Ultra\_High} BEM mesh tiers, sub-panel quadrature regularization and continuum Richardson extrapolation ($h = N_{\rm panels}^{-1/2} \to 0$) establish quantitative agreement across 3D Vector-Dyadic BEM, 3D Scalar-Yukawa BEM, and 4-pass inclusion--exclusion 3D FDTD MEEP across both $N=2$ and $N=3$ Cantor hierarchies, with full-monolithic models confirming $<3.3\%$ passive-support crosstalk.
    \item \textbf{Room-Temperature NEMS Feasibility and 6-DOF Stability}: At $T = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoCdToleranceTemperatureK{}\text{ K}$, the repulsive and attractive switching states are robust to finite-temperature Matsubara dispersion and uncompensated electrostatic voltages up to $V_{\rm crit} = \NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzPatchPotentialVCritMv{}\text{--}\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoPatchPotentialVCritMv{}\text{ mV}$. A $\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilitySpokeCount{}$-spoke single-crystal $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ suspension ($E_Y = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityEYPa{}\text{ Pa}$) provides lateral stability safety factors of $\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityLateralStabilitySafetyFactor{}\times$ (\texttt{Rot\_C8\_Compact\_N2}) and $\NatureCantorNtwoHybridrzLateralStabilityLateralStabilitySafetyFactor{}\times$ (\texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}) against Earnshaw instabilities while preserving repulsive lift across realistic corner-rounding ($r_{\rm edge}=5\text{--}10\text{ nm}$) and $\Delta\text{CD} \in [-10, +10]\text{ nm}$ lithographic tolerances.
\end{enumerate}
