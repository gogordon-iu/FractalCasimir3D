\noindent\textbf{%
Quantum vacuum fluctuations generate strong attractive Casimir forces between proximate surfaces, causing irreversible stiction in nanoelectromechanical systems (NEMS)\cite{casimir1948,rodriguez2011}. While self-similar boundaries theoretically induce scale-dependent vacuum anomalies\cite{shajesh2016,gordon2026prd}, observing a macroscopic fractal Casimir effect in three-dimensional (3D) cavities is fundamentally impeded by Weyl's law: face-to-face surface overlap produces a Euclidean area force that buries fractal boundary modifications inside subleading spectral remainders\cite{lapidus1991}. Conversely, three-dimensional slot-tooth interdigitation eliminates vertical surface overlap and generates true-vacuum axial repulsion\cite{levin2010}, yet single-scale periodic teeth suffer severe evanescent screening across wide actuators. Here we show that interdigitated slot alignment opens a zero-proximity-force window in which multi-scale Cantor boundary recursion becomes the leading electromagnetic interaction, amplifying true-vacuum repulsive lift by more than 28-fold over uniform periodic controls and enabling a dynamically switchable rotary quantum clutch. Using 3D Vector-Dyadic and Scalar-Yukawa boundary element methods alongside 4-pass 3D finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) simulations, we demonstrate unpowered rotational switching between fractal-amplified slot repulsion ($\theta = 0^\circ\text{--}6^\circ$, reaching $+\CantorNtwoHybridrzBemHighFZPn{}\text{ pN}$ in BEM and $+\CantorNtwoHybridrzMeepRfivezeroFZPn{}\text{ pN}$ in FDTD for $N=2$, and $+\RotConezeroFractalNthreeHighThetaZerozerothreepzeroVectorDyadicFZPn{}\text{ pN}$ for $N=3$) and Euclidean landing-pad attraction ($\theta = 18^\circ\text{--}48^\circ$, $F_z < 0$). Coupled with cavity quantum electrodynamic, optomechanical, and room-temperature ($300\text{ K}$) silicon-on-insulator stability blueprints, our results establish multi-scale vacuum engineering for stiction-free nanomachines.}

The Casimir effect---the macroscopic mechanical force arising from boundary-modified quantum electromagnetic fluctuations\cite{casimir1948,bordag2009}---dominates nanoscale device mechanics below $100\text{ nm}$. By the Kenneth--Klich no-go theorem\cite{kennethklich2006}, any two mirror-symmetric bodies composed of ordinary conductors or non-magnetic dielectrics in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) mutually attract ($F_z \propto -A_{\rm overlap}/d^4$). While repulsive Casimir--Lifshitz forces have been realized via chemical liquid immersion\cite{munday2009}, and rotational Casimir torques\cite{somers2018}, self-assembled microcavities\cite{munkhbat2021}, and nonlinear superconducting resonators\cite{casimir_drum2025,dejong2026} have expanded vacuum metrology, achieving tunable repulsive restoring forces in true vacuum remains a central challenge for stiction-free NEMS.

Two seemingly distinct theoretical pathways have been proposed to reshape vacuum forces through geometry alone. First, in quantum fractals\cite{kempkes2019,xu2021} and self-similar Casimir stacks\cite{akkermans2010,shajesh2016,shajesh2017}, discrete scale invariance (DSI) breaks conformal symmetry. As recently formalized in an effective trace framework for self-similar systems\cite{gordon2026prd} (which established thermodynamic compatibility via semitransparent mode tunneling and conformal-parameter $\xi$-independence\cite{milton2004,milton2016}), any plate-like system governed by a logarithmically running Casimir coefficient $C(d_s, \ln(d/\ell_*))$ generates an anisotropic stress-energy tensor with a non-vanishing integrated vacuum trace\cite{gordon2026prd}:
\begin{equation}
T^\mu{}_{\mu,\rm vac} = -\rho_{\rm vac} + 2P_\parallel + P_\perp = -\frac{\hbar c}{d^4} \,\partial_{\ln d}C\!\left(d_s, \ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right),
\label{eq:trace_running}
\end{equation}
where $d_s$ is the spectral dimension and $\ell_* = L b^{-N}$ is the prefractal cutoff scale. However, in previous one-dimensional scalar models\cite{gordon2026prd}, $C(d_s, \ln(d/\ell_*))$ remained a postulated ansatz rather than a first-principles 3D electromagnetic scattering result. Moreover, in standard 3D Euclidean cavities with fractal boundaries (``fractal drums''), Lapidus's theorem\cite{lapidus1991} dictates that the eigenvalue counting function $N_\Omega(\lambda) = C_n |\Omega|\lambda^{n/2} + \mathcal{O}(\lambda^{D/2})$ is dominated by the leading Euclidean Weyl volume/area term $|\Omega|$, burying fractal boundary anomalies inside subleading remainders.

Second, Levin et al.\cite{levin2010} demonstrated that reflection symmetry can be broken geometrically in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) without fractals: when a metallic ridge interdigitates with a slotted aperture plate, transverse saddle-point fringing fields connecting the ridge tip to the upper slot sidewalls pull the ridge toward the slot mid-plane, yielding an axial repulsive restoring force ($F_z > 0$)\cite{levin2010,rodriguez2011}. Yet a single narrow ridge produces sub-piconewton forces, and naively widening a single ridge across an actuator separates the opposing slot sidewalls and destroys the repulsive gradient.

Here we unify these two phenomena by showing that 3D slot-tooth interdigitation and prefractal Cantor hierarchy are mutually essential counterparts of a macroscopic fractal Casimir effect. In the \emph{Gold Concentric Cantor-Ring Rotary Quantum Clutch} (Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum_clutch_architecture_and_narrative}), aligning interdigitated rotor teeth with open stator slots ($\theta = 0^\circ\text{--}6^\circ$) sets the vertical face-to-face surface overlap to zero ($A_{\rm overlap} = 0$). This identically cancels the leading Euclidean proximity-force approximation (PFA) area term ($F_z^{\rm PFA} \equiv 0$), opening a ``Zero-PFA Fractal Window'' in which multi-scale perimeter fringing and cross-track scattering recursion become the leading physical interaction. Conversely, rotating the rotor over recessed stator landing pads ($\theta = 18^\circ\text{--}48^\circ$) restores $A_{\rm overlap} > 0$, dynamically switching the cavity from the fractal perimeter-dominated repulsive phase into the Euclidean area-dominated attractive phase.

\section*{The zero-PFA fractal window and clutch}
\label{sec:architecture}

To derive the running Casimir coefficient $C$ from first principles and link it to 3D interdigitated electrodynamics, we first solve the transfer-matrix scattering recursion for an $N$-th generation pre-Cantor hierarchy $\mathcal{C}_N$ of scale factor $b$ (see Methods). Partitioning a self-similar boundary configuration of outer span $d$ into its self-similar sub-blocks yields the functional interaction energy recursion
\begin{equation}
\Delta e_{\mathcal{C}_N}(d) = 2 \Delta e_{\mathcal{C}_{N-1}}(d/b) + \Delta e_{\rm cross}(d; \ell_*),
\label{eq:recursion_main}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta e_{\rm cross}$ captures the evanescent cross-talk between hierarchical sub-cavities across imaginary wavenumbers $\kappa = \xi/c$. As shown in Methods, solving the discrete scale-invariant functional equation in $(3+1)$ dimensions yields a log-periodic Fourier expansion for the dimensionless Casimir coefficient:
\begin{equation}
C\!\left(d_s, \ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k \, e^{i \omega_k \ln(d/\ell_*)} = C_0(d_s) \left[ 1 + F_{\rm per}\!\left(\ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right) \right], \qquad \omega_k = \frac{2\pi k}{\ln b},
\label{eq:fourier_running}
\end{equation}
where $F_{\rm per}$ is a multiplicatively periodic function of period $\ln b$ ($b=3$ for middle-third Cantor geometry). Unlike a scale-invariant Euclidean system ($C = \text{const}$, $\partial_{\ln d} C = 0$), Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier_running} shows that discrete self-similarity forces a non-zero logarithmic scale derivative $\partial_{\ln d} C \neq 0$, sourcing the integrated vacuum trace in Eq.~\eqref{eq:trace_running}.

In a 3D cylindrical NEMS actuator ($R \in [R_{\rm min}, R_{\rm max}]$), however, observing this multi-scale boundary physics requires suppressing the leading Euclidean parallel-plate attraction. The Gold Concentric Cantor-Ring Rotary Quantum Clutch achieves this in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) using a four-fold cyclic ($C_4$, $90^\circ$ sector period) Gold rotor suspended above a stationary Gold stator (Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum_clutch_architecture_and_narrative}). Downward-protruding concentric rotor teeth ($z_{\rm tooth} \in [-100, 0]\text{ nm}$, height $H_{\rm teeth} = 100\text{ nm}$) interdigitate radially between thin upper stator aperture rings ($z_{\rm stator} \in [-25, 0]\text{ nm}$, thickness $t_{\rm membrane} = 25\text{ nm}$) above recessed azimuthal stator landing pads ($z_{\rm pad,top} = -115\text{ nm}$, thickness $t_{\rm pad} = 150\text{ nm}$).

In-plane rotation $\theta$ switches the clutch continuously between two universality regimes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Disengaged Fractal Perimeter State ($\theta = 0^\circ\text{--}6^\circ$)}: The rotor teeth sit centered inside open angular stator apertures with zero vertical landing-pad overlap ($A_{\rm overlap} = 0$, $F_z^{\rm PFA} = 0$). Because the tooth tips ($z_{\rm tip} = -100\text{ nm}$) extend $75\text{ nm}$ below the stator ring bottom ($z = -25\text{ nm}$), transverse saddle-point fringing fields pull the rotor upward toward the slot mid-plane ($z = -12.5\text{ nm}$), generating repulsive lift ($F_z > 0$) governed entirely by 3D sidewall scattering.
    \item \textbf{Engaged Euclidean Area State ($\theta = 18^\circ\text{--}48^\circ$)}: Rotating the rotor aligns the bottom faces of the rotor teeth ($z = -100\text{ nm}$) directly over the recessed stator landing pads ($z = -115\text{ nm}$) across a $d = 15\text{ nm}$ vertical vacuum gap. Here, Euclidean face-to-face parallel-plate overlap ($A_{\rm overlap} > 0$) dominates over sidewall fringing, switching the axial force to strong normal attraction ($F_z < 0$).
\end{enumerate}

To rigorously separate the role of single-tooth interdigitation from multi-scale Cantor hierarchy across a fixed annular span, we benchmark a six-step progression of prefractal Cantor rings ($N=0, 1, 2, 3$) against matched non-fractal controls (see Methods for exact radial coordinates): (i) the single-track baseline ($N=0$, \texttt{SingleTooth\_Track0}, tooth width $w_{\rm tooth} = 85\text{ nm}$, lateral gap $g_r = 32.5\text{ nm}$); (ii) the first-generation single wide Cantor slot ($N=1$, \texttt{Fractal\_N1\_FundRing}, $W_{\rm slot} = 383.3\text{ nm}$, $g_r = 86.3\text{ nm}$); (iii) the second-generation 3-track Cantor hierarchies ($N=2$) comparing uniform vertical tooth depth (\texttt{Cantor\_N2\_FlatZ}, $z_{\rm tip} = -100\text{ nm}$), scale-matched radial-and-vertical $(r,z)$ depth (\texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}, where the primary generation-1 track extends to $z_{\rm tip,1} = -110\text{ nm}$ and flanking generation-2 tracks sit at $z_{\rm tip,2} = -100\text{ nm}$), and compact NEMS variants (\texttt{Rot\_C8\_Compact\_N2} and \texttt{Rot\_C4\_TightSlot\_N2}); (iv) the third-generation 7-track Cantor hierarchy ($N=3$, \texttt{Fractal\_N3\_Cantor7Track} / \texttt{Rot\_C10\_Fractal\_N3} with $2^3 - 1 = 7$ concentric tracks and $28$ teeth across four sectors); and (v) three non-fractal controls---\texttt{Ctrl\_SingleWide} (a single wide tooth spanning the annulus), \texttt{Ctrl\_UniformPeriodic} (an equal-area 3-track periodic grating with identical tooth and slot widths), and \texttt{Ctrl\_SizeMatched\_Monotonic} (the exact track widths of $N=2$ rearranged in monotonic radial order rather than self-similar Cantor flanking order).

\section*{Atomic CQED and optomechanical readouts}
\label{sec:cqed_optomech}

Beyond direct nanomechanical actuation, the scale-dependent vacuum inside a prefractal Cantor cavity ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) produces distinct thermodynamic, cavity quantum electrodynamic (CQED), and optomechanical signatures (Methods). In a ``Conformal Breathing Box'' Gedankenexperiment, subjecting a prefractal cavity to a uniform dilation $\lambda$ breaks the featureless $E(\lambda) = E_0/\lambda$ scaling of a conformally invariant cavity; the non-conformal expansion work directly integrates the trace anomaly of Eq.~\eqref{eq:trace_running}, $\Delta W_{\rm non\text{-}conformal} = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} T^\mu{}_\mu \, dV$.

At the microscopic scale, a ground-state hydrogen atom placed at the center ($z = 50\text{ nm}$) of a $d = 100\text{ nm}$ prefractal gap experiences three measurable QED modifications (Methods): (i) a log-periodic modulation of the retarded Casimir--Polder potential\cite{casimirpolder1948}, shifting the baseline flat-plate interaction ($|U_{\rm CP}^{\rm flat}| \approx 2.51 \times 10^{-9}\text{ eV}$) by $\Delta U_{\rm CP} \approx 2.51 \times 10^{-10}\text{ eV}$ ($\sim 60.77\text{ kHz}$), well within the $<10^{-12}\text{ eV}$ resolution of modern Rydberg and optical-tweezer spectroscopy\cite{safronova2018}; (ii) a fractal Purcell modification\cite{purcell1946} of the Lyman-$\alpha$ ($2p \to 1s$, $\lambda = 121.6\text{ nm}$) spontaneous emission rate $\Gamma_{2p\to 1s}/\Gamma_0$ of $10\%\text{--}50\%$, exceeding the $0.1\%$ precision of fluorescence lifetime imaging\cite{lodahl2015}; and (iii) a geometric Bethe Lamb shift\cite{bethe1947} of $\delta\nu_{\rm Lamb} \approx 2.4\text{ kHz}$ ($\delta E_{\rm Lamb} \approx 9.93 \times 10^{-12}\text{ eV}$), exceeding the $<10\text{ Hz}$ uncertainty of two-photon $1S\text{--}2S$ hydrogen spectroscopy\cite{parthey2011}. Furthermore, coupling the prefractal stator to a superconducting microwave drum resonator\cite{casimir_drum2025} maps the non-zero logarithmic running $\partial_{\ln d}C$ directly onto the Duffing mechanical softening shift $\Delta\omega_m(d) \propto -\partial_d^2 E_C(d)$, providing a single-photon optomechanical readout of the running Casimir coefficient.

\subsection*{Transfer-matrix derivation of the log-periodic running Casimir coefficient}
To derive the scale-dependent Casimir coefficient $C(d_s, \ln(d/\ell_*))$ in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:trace_running}--\eqref{eq:fourier_running} from first principles rather than postulating it phenomenologically\cite{gordon2026prd}, we evaluate the reduced Euclidean Green's function $g(z, z'; \kappa)$ at imaginary wavenumber $\kappa = \sqrt{\zeta^2 + \mathbf{k}_\perp^2}$ across an $N$-th generation pre-Cantor stack $\mathcal{C}_N$ of semitransparent barriers $V(z) = \lambda_0 \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_N} \delta(z - z_i)$ on $[0, d]$:
\begin{equation}
\left[ -\frac{\partial^2}{\partial z^2} + \kappa^2 + V(z) \right] g(z, z'; \kappa) = \delta(z - z').
\end{equation}
In the homogeneous vacuum intervals between barriers, $g(z, z'; \kappa) = A_j e^{-\kappa z} + B_j e^{\kappa z}$, where wave amplitudes across barrier $z_i$ are linked by the single-plate transfer matrix $\mathbf{M}_i(\kappa)$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}_i(\kappa) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - \frac{\lambda_0}{2\kappa} & -\frac{\lambda_0}{2\kappa} e^{2\kappa z_i} \\ \frac{\lambda_0}{2\kappa} e^{-2\kappa z_i} & 1 + \frac{\lambda_0}{2\kappa} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{M}_{\rm global}(\kappa) = \prod_{i \in \mathcal{C}_N} \mathbf{M}_i(\kappa).
\end{equation}
For any finite coupling $\lambda_0 < \infty$, high-frequency modes ($\kappa \gg \lambda_0$) tunnel across barriers with transmission probability $|t(\kappa)|^2 = 4\kappa^2 / (4\kappa^2 + \lambda_0^2) > 0$, establishing global thermalization across all sub-cavities at temperature $T$ before taking the strong-coupling limit\cite{gordon2026prd}. Partitioning the $N$-th generation Cantor stack (scale ratio $b=3$) into its left and right $(N-1)$-th generation sub-stacks yields the functional interaction energy recursion $\Delta e(d) = 2\Delta e(d/b) + \Delta e_{\rm cross}(d)$. In $(3+1)$-dimensional spacetime, the interaction energy per unit area carries scaling dimension $[\text{length}]^{-3}$, so that under spatial dilation $d \to d/b$ the homogeneous scale-invariant part satisfies $\Delta e_{\rm hom}(d/b) = b^3 \Delta e_{\rm hom}(d)$, while the discrete self-similarity across generations $N \to \infty$ imposes periodicity in $\ln(d/\ell_*)$ with period $\ln b$. Expanding the general solution in complex scaling frequencies $\omega_k = 2\pi k / \ln b$ ($k \in \mathbb{Z}$) yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier_running}:
\begin{equation}
e(d) = \frac{\hbar c}{d^3} \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k \, e^{i \frac{2\pi k}{\ln b} \ln(d/\ell_*)} = \frac{\hbar c}{d^3} C\!\left(d_s, \ln\frac{d}{\ell_*}\right).
\end{equation}
At finite temperature $T$, compactifying imaginary time with period $\beta = \hbar/(k_B T)$ replaces the continuous $\zeta$-integral with the Matsubara sum over $\zeta_n = 2\pi n k_B T/\hbar$, generalizing $C$ to the bivariate function $C_T(d_s, \ln(d/\ell_*), d/\lambda_T)$ with $\lambda_T = \hbar c / (2\pi k_B T)$\cite{gordon2026prd}.

\subsection*{3D Boundary Element Method (BEM) and $C_4$ cyclic Bloch reduction}
In three dimensions, the transfer-matrix log-determinant generalizes to the surface-integral scattering formula along the imaginary wavenumber axis $\kappa = \xi/c$ in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$):
\begin{equation}
E_C = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi} \int_0^\infty d\kappa \, \ln\det\bigl(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}(\kappa)\bigr),
\qquad
F_z = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi} \int_0^\infty d\kappa \, \mathrm{Tr}\!\left[\bigl(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}(\kappa)\bigr)^{-1} \frac{\partial \mathbf{M}(\kappa)}{\partial z_{\rm rotor}}\right],
\label{eq:methods_bem}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{M}(\kappa) = \mathbf{D}_{\rm diag}^{-1}(\kappa)\mathbf{V}_{\rm off}(\kappa)$ couples discretized surface panels across the rotor and stator via the dyadic Yukawa Green's kernel $\exp(-\kappa R)/(4\pi R)$ weighted by the dispersive Drude dielectric permittivity of Gold ($\omega_p = 9.03\text{ eV}$, $\gamma = 0.053\text{ eV}$) in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$). We evaluate Eq.~\eqref{eq:methods_bem} using both the full 3D Vector-Dyadic electromagnetic kernel and the 3D Scalar-Yukawa kernel across three hierarchical mesh tiers: \texttt{Standard} ($\RotCfourTightslotNtwoStandardThetaZerozerosixpzeroPanelCount{}$ panels), \texttt{High} ($\RotCfourTightslotNtwoHighThetaZerozerosixpzeroPanelCount{}$ panels), and \texttt{Ultra\_High} ($\RotCfourTightslotNtwoUltraHighThetaZerozerosixpzeroPanelCount{}$ panels). Exploiting the four-fold cyclic symmetry ($C_4$) of the concentric ring sectors block-diagonalizes the dense $N_{\rm total} \times N_{\rm total}$ system into four decoupled Bloch irreducible representations ($m \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$), reducing memory footprint by $16\times$ and accelerating LU factorization by $64\times$. At $T = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoCdToleranceTemperatureK{}\text{ K}$, the continuous $\kappa$-integral is evaluated via exact Matsubara summation over $\kappa_n = 2\pi n k_B T / (\hbar c)$ with prime weight $1/2$ at the static $n=0$ transverse-magnetic (TM) mode (the static $n=0$ transverse-electric reflection vanishes in accordance with the Bohr--van Leeuwen theorem).

\subsection*{4-pass inclusion--exclusion 3D FDTD MEEP methodology}
To provide an independent time-domain electromagnetic benchmark in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$), we integrate the Wick-rotated Maxwell stress tensor $\mathbf{T}$ over closed per-tooth vacuum bounding boxes $S_i$ surrounding each rotor tooth inside its radial stator slot\cite{oskooi2010,rodriguez2011}:
\begin{equation}
F_z = \frac{\hbar}{\pi} \int_0^\infty d\xi \, \sum_{i} \oint_{S_i} d\mathbf{A} \cdot \langle \mathbf{T}(i\xi, \mathbf{x}) \rangle_z,
\qquad
T_{zz} = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \varepsilon_0 (E_z^2 - E_x^2 - E_y^2) + \mu_0 (H_z^2 - H_x^2 - H_y^2) \right].
\label{eq:methods_fdtd}
\end{equation}
To eliminate both the rotor's self-force and the uncoupled background scattering of the stator slot walls across the bounding-box faces, each discrete-cosine-transform (DCT) boundary moment is computed using a 4-pass inclusion--exclusion subtraction at identical Yee-grid alignment (\texttt{eps\_averaging = False}, \texttt{Courant = 0.3}):
\begin{equation}
F_z^{\rm net} = \bigl(F_z^{\rm both} - F_z^{\rm rotor}\bigr) - \bigl(F_z^{\rm stator} - F_z^{\rm vac}\bigr).
\label{eq:methods_four_pass}
\end{equation}
All 4-pass MEEP simulations were executed across sharded MPI worker groups on the BigRed 200 supercomputer using the 6-pole Raki\'c Lorentz--Drude susceptibility model for Gold in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$) and converted to SI piconewtons via $\hbar c / a^2 = 0.0316152677\text{ pN}\cdot\mu\text{m}^2$ at $a = 1\ \mu\text{m}$.

\subsection*{Radial and vertical coordinates of the Cantor hierarchy and control geometries}
In the baseline single-track configuration ($N=0$, \texttt{SingleTooth\_Track0}), a single concentric rotor tooth ($R_{\rm tooth} \in [0.2325, 0.3175]\ \mu\text{m}$, width $w_{\rm tooth} = 85\text{ nm}$, $z_{\rm tip} = -100\text{ nm}$) sits centered inside a single stator slot ($R_{\rm slot} \in [0.20, 0.35]\ \mu\text{m}$, width $W_{\rm slot} = 150\text{ nm}$, lateral gap $g_r = 32.5\text{ nm}$). In the first-generation single-slot ring ($N=1$, \texttt{Fractal\_N1\_FundRing}), middle-third subdivision across $R \in [0.20, 1.35]\ \mu\text{m}$ yields $W_{\rm slot} = 383.3\text{ nm}$, $w_{\rm tooth} = 210.8\text{ nm}$, and $g_r = 86.3\text{ nm}$. In the second-generation 3-track hierarchies ($N=2$, \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_FlatZ} and \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}), a primary generation-1 central track is flanked by two narrower generation-2 child tracks; \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_FlatZ} uses uniform $z_{\rm tip} = -100\text{ nm}$, whereas \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ} scales the vertical insertion depth with the radial generation ($z_{\rm tip,1} = -110\text{ nm}$ on generation 1 and $z_{\rm tip,2} = -100\text{ nm}$ on generation 2). The compact NEMS variants \texttt{Rot\_C8\_Compact\_N2} and \texttt{Rot\_C4\_TightSlot\_N2} compress the $N=2$ hierarchy into $R \in [0.20, 0.90]\ \mu\text{m}$ with $35\text{--}60\text{ nm}$ lateral slot clearances, and the third-generation $N=3$ design (\texttt{Fractal\_N3\_Cantor7Track} / \texttt{Rot\_C10\_Fractal\_N3}) spans $R \in [0.15, 0.95]\ \mu\text{m}$ with $2^3 - 1 = 7$ concentric tracks ($28$ teeth across the four sectors).

\subsection*{Cavity QED atomic transducers and optomechanical readout formulations}
For a ground-state hydrogen atom with static polarizability $\alpha(0)/(4\pi\varepsilon_0) = \frac{9}{2}a_0^3$ located at distance $z$ from a prefractal boundary in true vacuum ($\varepsilon_{\rm bg}=1.0$), the retarded Casimir--Polder potential is modulated by the log-periodic coefficient:
\begin{equation}
U_{\rm CP}(z) = -\frac{3\hbar c \, \alpha(0)}{32\pi^2 \varepsilon_0 z^4} \left[ 1 + C\!\left(d_s, \ln\frac{z}{\ell_*}\right) \right].
\end{equation}
The spontaneous emission rate of the $2p \to 1s$ transition ($\omega_{12}$) relative to free space $\Gamma_0$ follows the local density of states scaling $\Gamma_{2p\to 1s}/\Gamma_0 \approx (L_s \omega_{12}/c)^{d_s - 3} G(\ln(\omega_{12}L_s/c))$, and the geometric modification to the non-relativistic Bethe Lamb shift is evaluated by integrating the boundary-modified virtual mode density $\Delta\rho_{\rm frac}(k, d_s, \ell_*)$:
\begin{equation}
\delta E_{\rm Lamb} = \frac{2\alpha_{\rm EM}}{3\pi m_e^2 c^2} \sum_n |\langle 2s | \mathbf{p} | n \rangle|^2 (E_n - E_{2s}) \int_0^\infty \frac{dk}{k} \, \frac{\Delta\rho_{\rm frac}(k, d_s, \ell_*)}{E_n - E_{2s} + \hbar c k}.
\end{equation}
When the prefractal stator is coupled to a superconducting drum resonator of effective mass $m_{\rm eff}$ and bare resonance frequency $\omega_{m,0}$ in a microwave optomechanical cavity\cite{casimir_drum2025}, the scale-dependent Casimir pressure induces a softening frequency shift $\Delta\omega_m(d) \approx -\frac{A_\perp}{2 m_{\rm eff} \omega_{m,0}} \frac{\partial P_\perp(d)}{\partial d}$, enabling direct reconstruction of $\partial_{\ln d}C$ and $\partial_{\ln d}^2 C$ from the mechanical frequency-versus-gap curve.

\subsection*{Silicon-on-insulator (SOI) nanofabrication protocol}
Fabrication of the Gold Concentric Cantor-Ring Rotary Quantum Clutch is designed around standard SOI and damascene Gold processing: (1) Starting from an SOI wafer with a single-crystal $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ device layer ($E_Y = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityEYPa{}\text{ Pa}$), the recessed isolation trench ($z \in [-650, -550]\text{ nm}$) and azimuthal landing pads ($z_{\rm pad,top} = -115\text{ nm}$, thickness $150\text{ nm}$) are patterned via $100\text{ keV}$ EBL in hydrogen silsesquioxane (HSQ) or PMMA resist, followed by Ti/Au seed deposition and damascene Gold electroplating. (2) A sacrificial $\text{SiO}_2$ spacer is deposited by atomic-layer deposition (ALD) and planarized by chemical-mechanical polishing (CMP) to define the exact $15\text{ nm}$ vertical standoff between the landing pads ($z = -115\text{ nm}$) and rotor tooth tips ($z = -100\text{ nm}$), after which the $25\text{ nm}$-thick slotted Cantor stator aperture rings ($z \in [-25, 0]\text{ nm}$) are patterned via overlay EBL aligned to sub-$5\text{ nm}$ interferometric fiducial marks. (3) Dual-exposure or grayscale EBL defines the stepped concentric rotor teeth ($110\text{ nm}$ generation-1 and $100\text{ nm}$ generation-2 teeth in \texttt{Cantor\_N2\_HybridRZ}), anchored to the four radial spider spokes ($z \in [350, 470]\text{ nm}$) and the $\NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilitySpokeCount{}$-spoke single-crystal $\text{Si}\langle 110\rangle$ flexure suspension ($L_{\rm spoke} = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityLSpokeUm{}\ \mu\text{m}$, $w_{\rm spoke} = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityWSpokeUm{}\ \mu\text{m}$, $t_{\rm spoke} = \NatureRotCeightCompactNtwoLateralStabilityTSpokeUm{}\ \mu\text{m}$). (4) The sacrificial oxide is removed using anhydrous vapor-phase hydrofluoric acid (vapor-HF) to prevent capillary stiction, followed by ultra-high-vacuum annealing and active Kelvin-probe nulling of residual patch potentials below $V_{\rm crit}$.
